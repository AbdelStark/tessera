## Synthesis

**Settled.**

1. *No system has measured what the scoreboard's first line asks for.* Level 1 of [baseline.md §5](../baseline.md#5-scoreboard) wants a dense model of at least 70B under full coverage, 100-bit soundness, zero knowledge, hash-based commitments and an independent verifier, measured end to end. Fourteen months of releases produced none: Attestable (31B, alpha claim, no proving time), DeepProve (GPT-2 and a Gemma 3 of unstated size measured; a 70B dashboard entry with no number), Seal-then-Sample (70B, 5 of 163 chunks, CPU, 1.9% coverage per invalid chunk), VerInf (400B MoE, full coverage, 2^-16.6 per challenge, 93.6 GB, one run). The "best known" column therefore stays a patchwork, and every Tessera cell stays a projection.

2. *Attestable's core is still unnamed, and the only new evidence is who built it.* The site's sitemap has three posts; the technical post is the one already read; the company page (primary) names Shahar Papini (LogUp-GKR, Circle STARKs) as CTO and Shahar Samocha (S-Two) as VP R&D. The landscape's "inferred" label should stay, now anchored to the authorship rather than to a guess, and the row should carry the E0 reading of its throughput: 309 s per 16K sequence on one H100, `μ(16K)` about 300–360 at the cost model's native-rate convention (600 at INT8 peak), against Tessera's projected 35 (corrected) and 79 (counted). Tessera's lead on `μ` is 4–10× on paper, not the four orders of magnitude the 1K row shows against VerInf; the deficits (proof size about 250×, verification about 500×) are unchanged.

3. *Proof size scales with the row shape, and the corpus now shows both corners.* VerInf opens 40 columns of 1.09×10^8 rows of width 8,192 and ships 93.6 GB (2.3 GB per column); Tessera opens 130–155 columns of 5.8×10^5 rows of width 2^22 and ships about 0.6–0.72 GB of columns plus 1.14 GB of combined rows (E0). The wide-row corner is the right one for size, and the remaining Tessera lever (SUR-0005: a second Ligero level over the combined rows) attacks the part VerInf does not have. VerInf's grinding analysis (`τ` ≈ 2.4(s + log2 Q)) is the Ligero-side statement of RFC-0014's grinding row and agrees with it in form.

4. *Lookup cost does not move with table size until the table itself dominates* ([YZL26], E1 on DeepProve): B16 → B8 tables leave the prover unchanged at a fixed lookup count, B24 tables cost 2.6×. This is the lookup-count side of the separation principle [Jo26] measured on a LogUp-GKR prover, and it supports RFC-0008's choice of 2^16–2^24 tables with the count as the lever. The same paper puts RMSNorm's rsqrt as the shared utility bottleneck across four models, which is the gadget IDEA-0011 targets.

5. *Packing and chunking are measured prior art for proof units.* Seal-then-Sample's per-request cost falls from 99 s to 15.2 s at 12 packed requests because proof cost follows sealed boundaries, not tokens; zkComposer's layer-and-sequence partition gives up to 6.84× over a monolithic zkGPT proof; DeepProve's distributed proving reports 1,855 tok/min. None shares a committed prefix across requests; the shared-prefix unit (queue item 51) has no prior art in this corpus and the packing results are the nearest neighbours.

6. *General-purpose GPU figures remain end-to-end only.* OpenVM's 19.4 MHz CoreMark on one RTX 5090 and Airbender's 21.8 MHz on one H100 are the only single-GPU zkVM rates; no project publishes a per-sumcheck-round, per-lookup or per-hash GPU number (SP1's cuPQC post has none; RISC Zero's release notes have none; Stwo has no public GPU backend; Expander's GPU post is unreachable and its search-index figure is relative). The primitive scoreboard's "Best number" cells therefore keep their 2026-10-16 content.

**Contested or open.**

7. *DeepProve's throughput*: 86 tok/min for Gemma 3 in the paper against 27 tok/min in the README on a 24-core CPU; the paper's hardware and Gemma 3 size are unread, so the row carries both. The "60× / 671×" release claims have no baseline named.

8. *Attestable's secondary numbers*: "85 tokens/s on a 30B model" ([KuCoin26]) against the primary 53/77; the primary wins and the secondary is recorded as a conflict.

9. *VEIL's 3% zero-knowledge overhead* is a vendor blog figure; SUR-0008's E0 count for Tessera's masking is 0.1% of `μ`; neither is measured on a GPU.

10. *TensorCommitments* claims sub-1% overhead for "verifiable inference" without a stated adversary; it belongs with the non-cryptographic competitors until the paper says otherwise.

Where RFC decisions stand: RFC-0005 (commit only non-arithmetic outputs) is confirmed by contrast with DeepProve's per-intermediate commitments and VerInf's 7.2 TB witness; RFC-0011's wide rows are confirmed by VerInf's proof size; RFC-0008's table sizes by [YZL26]; RFC-0014's grinding row by VerInf §7.3; NG-3 (no recursion) is priced by Flock at about 200× the verifier's hashing. Nothing read contradicts an accepted RFC.

## Candidate ideas

- Scoreboard throughput metric: add parameter-tokens per GPU-second (and per committed byte) as the hardware-normalized line of [baseline.md §5](../baseline.md#5-scoreboard), with Attestable's 1.64×10^12 as the measured anchor and Tessera's projected 1.6×10^13 (corrected) / 7×10^12 (counted) at the cost model's rate; term: scoreboard; gain: an honest cross-system comparison today; killer: the cost model's `native_rate` convention is not one H100.
- Shared-prefix proof units with prefix-committed boundary activations (prior art: STS packing, zkComposer boundaries); term: `A`, `regen`; gain: per-request cost follows boundaries, up to 6.5× at 12 requests in STS; killer: a shared prefix leaks which requests share it (G-5) unless the unit boundary is itself hidden.
- Grinding-budget sizing of `τ` in the RFC-0014 table in VerInf's form (`τ` ≈ 2.4(s + log2 Q) for Ligero rate 1/4); term: `τ`, bits; gain: a closed form for the live-64 against NI-100 split; killer: Tessera's grinding row already credits the seed's proof of work (ledger item 1), so the gain is presentational.
- Table-size insensitivity test for `c_ℓ` (reproduce [YZL26]'s B8/B16/B24 result on `tessera-logup`); term: `c_ℓ`; gain: fixes which table sizes RFC-0008 can grow for free; killer: the result is DeepProve-specific if its fraction tree is not fused like Tessera's.
- Recursion price from Flock: NG-3 reopened only if in-circuit BLAKE3 falls below about 20× native; term: size, verify; gain: a numeric gate for NG-3 instead of a prose exclusion; killer: Flock is binary-field and CPU-only.
- Declared-statement benchmark contract for RFC-0020 ([HHP26]): every measured number ships with the statement, soundness bits, coverage and resource limits; term: method; gain: Tessera's E2/E3 numbers become comparable with the systems above; killer: none, cost is documentation.
- Findings without a seed: [Noze26], [KuCoin26] (secondary reports); [LLQL26] (two-party trust); [LGW+26], [Modulus26] (not inference statements); [BSW+26], [KRR+25], [OPF+25] (not cryptographic proofs); the zkVM rows (no per-primitive number to adapt); [EZKL26], [Ligero26] (no new technique).
