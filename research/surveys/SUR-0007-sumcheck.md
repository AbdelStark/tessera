## Method

**Frame.** The glue factor 1.15 of the cost model ([baseline.md §2](../baseline.md#2-the-cost-model), [08 §1](../../docs/spec/08-performance-budget.md#1-cost-model)) is "sumcheck rounds, `eq` tables"; its removal is worth −7.4% of `μ(16K)` under the corrected profile (−6.5% at 1K, −6.8% at 128K; E0, cost model). Beyond the glue, the per-round algorithm is inside every unit cost: [EXP-0006](../experiments/EXP-0006-product-leaf-cost/README.md) counts 25.5 `Fp` multiplications, 6 small multiplications and 63 additions per product-tree leaf, `eq` tables 10.3% of leaf time, additions a third of the int32 work (E1, Apple M4 Max, primary). Question 1 therefore moves glue, `c_π` and `c_ℓ`; question 2 moves `stream` (`c(S)` streamed rounds at `p_s` per score per pass, RISK-10) and `regen` (RISK-9); question 3 moves glue, `size` and `verify`. Today's answers are fixed by [RFC-0010 'Sumcheck messages'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0010-proof-schedule.md#sumcheck-messages) (round `j` sends `p_j(0), p_j(2), …, p_j(d)`, the verifier derives `p_j(1)`; degree 3 for every tree level and elementwise relation, 2 for unstacked matmuls), 'Tree levels' and 'Batching within a stage' (front-padding, `γ^i · 2^{n_max − n_i}` scaling, forests join where heights match), [RFC-0018 'Pass structure'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#pass-structure) ("one pass regenerates the level's leaves tile by tile and accumulates the round message", `c = ⌈log2(N_level · 32 B / M)⌉`, 9 at 16K and 12 at 128K by the cost model's own formula per SUR-0003) and RFC-0018 Alternative 3 (multi-variable rounds rejected because "message size and prover work grow as `(d + 1)^w`"). The engine as built ([crates/tessera-sumcheck](../../crates/tessera-sumcheck/src/lib.rs), read 2026-10-16): each instance's `eq(r_i, ·)` is a full `Fp2` table built by `eq_table` for its level and bound every round like any other table; the product is evaluated at the `d` message nodes (plus node 1 in an instance's first own round) with `k − 1` multiplications per node; no Gruen correction, no split `eq`, no univariate skip, no round batching.

## Synthesis

**Settled.**

1. *The per-round algorithm for `eq × product` is a solved problem on paper, and Tessera's engine implements none of it.* The sequence [Gru24] → [DT24] → [DDBDT26] §6 gives, for `g = eq(w, x) · Π p_k`, a prover that never builds or binds a full `eq` table (two `√N` tables, an `O(1)` scalar per round), evaluates one node fewer per round (the `eq` factor's current variable is a public linear factor), and leaves the message unchanged if one reconstructs the degree-`d` polynomial from `d − 1` sums, the claim and the leading coefficient ([JoltOpt26]). [DT24]'s accounting for the Spartan shape (two small tables and one `eq`) is 8.5N → 5N multiplications, −41%; Tessera's level shape is the same (`eq` in `Fp2`, two `Fp` tables), with the difference that the saved multiplications are the `Fp2 × Fp` and `Fp2 × Fp2` ones, so the saving in `Fp`-multiplication equivalents is at least the paper's fraction (E0). Against EXP-0006's count (25.5 `Fp` multiplications per leaf, `eq` tables 10.3% of time), the reachable reduction on the level sumcheck's round work is about a quarter to a third (E0, shape-dependent); on `μ` it is a slice of the glue (−7.4% at 16K if glue → 1.0) plus the same slice of `c_π` and `c_ℓ`. All of it is prover-only: RFC-0010's messages, labels and verifier checks stay as they are. Stwo ([Stwo26]) and Jolt ([JoltOpt26]) ship it.

2. *Small-field algorithms buy little over a degree-2 extension.* [BDT24]'s gains scale with `ee/bb`: 1.31× at extension degree 4, 10.8× at 128 (`d = 2`), with the optimal switch after round `(1.585 log2 k + 1.4)/2`. For Goldilocks/`Fp2` the ratio is 3 and the switch is after round 1–2: the first round can be computed with `Fp × Fp` products (already so in the engine, whose round-1 tables are base-field) and the gain on the rest is below 1.3× (E0, extrapolated below the table). What does pay is a different thing from the same paper's successor: when the leaf values are small integers (product-tree leaves `c_q − x_qi`, lookup keys), [DDBDT26] §3's `ss` multiplications are plain `u64` products with one delayed reduction per block (`κ ≈ 10–29` rather than 3; Table 4's 10–24× on linear combinations), which is [IDEA-0010](../ideas/IDEA-0010-shared-point-forest-fusion.md) hypothesis (b) with a primary source. Toom–Cook for the product itself ([DDBDT26] §4) helps only for `d ≥ 4`; no RFC-0010 relation exceeds degree 3, so it has no target until a relation of degree 4 or more exists (the engine's `MAX_DEGREE = 7` is capacity, not use).

3. *Passes: a streamed round need not be a pass, and the cheapest way to say so is round batching, not a protocol change.* [BCFFMMZ25]'s full text fixes the pass count at `log_η(n/k) + d k` with `η = d/(d − 1)` (for `d = 2`: `log2(n/k) + 2k`), its `k = 2` prover running 1.8–2× slower than linear-space at 0.008× the memory (E1); [DDBDT26] Lemma 6 improves the degree dependence to `d log d` and Table 10 measures its streaming variant at 1.62–2.70× the linear-space time in Jolt (E1). Tessera is not in the `N^{1/k}` regime (0.2 TB of a 70 TB level is `N^{0.95}`), so the useful object is the window: a block of `2^v` consecutive leaf pairs contributes to a `3^v`-point grid (`d = 2`, `eq` factored out by Lemma 7) in one pass, and the `v` round messages are inner products of the grid with Lagrange weights of the challenges as they arrive. Own count (E0): `1.5^v` small×small products and `1.5^v` small×big `eq`-weight scalings per pair, 15 `Fp` multiplications at `v = 4`, 23 at `v = 5`, 34 at `v = 6`, against about 33–38 for the per-round prover over the same four or five rounds (round 1 at 9 per pair, later rounds at 18 over halving pairs, plus the bindings of the two tables and the `eq` table), plus `2 · 1.5^v` `Fp2` additions per pair and about 4 multiplications per pair to bind the leaves to the previous window's challenges from the second window on. The passes fall from `c = 9` to 2 at 16K (windows 5 + 4) and from 12 to 3 at 128K (5 + 5 + 2), or 2 at `v = 6`; on the corrected profile, where streaming is 32.0% of `μ(16K)` and 45.5% of `μ(128K)`, that is −24.9% and −34.1% of `μ` (E0, dividing the streaming term by the pass ratio, as SUR-0003 did for Hobbit), with the bandwidth reading of [DMABKGR25] and [MDG+26] (a streamed round is two full passes of `N` elements) saying the multiplications are not what the passes cost. This is prover-only: messages, labels and verifier are RFC-0010's, so it does not reopen RFC-0018 Alternative 3 (whose objection, `(d + 1)^w` message growth, is about multi-variable *rounds*) and it needs no RFC. Hobbit's fold (SUR-0003) reaches one pass by changing the protocol; the window reaches two or three without. The window also answers Alternative 3's objection in another way: the univariate skip ([Gru24] §5, [DDBDT26] §7) *is* a multi-variable round whose message grows as `2^{k+1} − 1` rather than `3^k` values, but for a non-zero sum its compute saving is 1.4–1.9× on the skipped rounds' evaluations (E0, the [Gru24] row) and its protocol cost is tier A, so it loses to the window on both counts.

4. *Batching across heights is what RFC-0010 already does, and now has E1 evidence.* Stwo's GKR activates an instance at the layer where its depth matches the remaining count, scales its claim by `2^{n_unused}`, combines with `instance_lambda`, shares one `eq_evals` table per round and lets the verifier apply `eq(ood[n_unused..], sc_ood[n_unused..])` per instance ([Stwo26]); RFC-0010 'Tree levels' joins forests where heights match, front-pads with `2^{n_max − n_i}` and sends one round sequence per level. The ragged-GKR measurement ([Ign26], E1) quantifies the choice: against independent walks, one batched walk is 1.63× faster to prove, 6.1× faster to verify and 6.26× smaller; against padding to the maximum width, 4.0× faster to prove. What batching costs: the `γ`-combination (one `Fp2` multiplication per instance per node per pair, which Jolt's bespoke form cuts from `4n/2^i` to `3n/2^i` per round for one shape), constant dummy contributions for the padded rounds (`O(1)` per instance per round), `m/|Fp2|` of soundness per stage (RFC-0010 'Soundness of the schedule'), and one `eq` correction per instance for the verifier. What it saves beyond proof size: one `eq` table per level instead of one per instance (IDEA-0010 mechanism 1; EXP-0006 prices the redundant per-instance `eq` folds at 7.5% of its fraction proxy's time, E1) and a shared final point per level, which is the premise of the weight-folding pass. Stwo's mixed batch of grand-product and LogUp gates, with `λ` combining numerator and denominator inside each oracle and instance powers across kinds, is the shape OQ-0010-1 asks for.

**Contested or unmeasured.**

- Every number above is E0 or an E1 on another field (BN254, 128-bit, GF(2)): nothing in the corpus measures Gruen, split `eq` or round batching over a 64-bit field with a degree-2 extension. [Lambda26] implements [DDBDT26] over BabyBear⁴ without publishing numbers.
- The window count assumes the regenerated tile order yields `2^v`-pair blocks of consecutive hypercube indices with the pair bit lowest (RFC-0010 'Multilinear extensions'); RFC-0018 regenerates "the level's leaves tile by tile" without fixing the tile-to-index map, which is a layout question for the leaf stage, not a protocol one.
- Where sources disagree: [DT24] attributes 25% / 10% end-to-end to its optimization by multiplication counts; [DDBDT26] Table 8 measures split `eq` at 1.26–1.47× in steady state and 1.76–3.01× when the baseline is memory-bound, so the paper's count understated the gain on real hardware. Stwo computes two node sums per degree-3 round and RFC-0010 sends three values; both are sound, and if Stwo's message is also two values the difference is one `Fp2` element per round of about 10⁴ per proof (E0).
- [MDG+26] and [DMABKGR25] model 255-bit fields; the bandwidth-bound reading of a streamed round is a 16-byte-per-element question at Goldilocks and is unmeasured (RISK-10, v0.5).

**Against the RFCs.** RFC-0010 'Sumcheck messages' confirmed (the `p(1)`-derived form is Gruen §3.1); 'Tree levels' and 'Batching within a stage' confirmed by [Stwo26] and measured by [Ign26]; OQ-0010-1 has a reference shape in Stwo's mixed batch. RFC-0018 'Pass structure' ("one pass per streamed round") contradicted as a floor by [BCFFMMZ25] and [DDBDT26] §5, prover-only; Alternative 3's objection does not cover windows, and the univariate skip shows its `(d + 1)^w` figure is not the only multi-variable-round message size. RFC-0002's two-adicity makes the skip available but the field-cost ratio makes it unattractive. Nothing here touches soundness bounds: every prover-only change leaves the transcript identical, and the one protocol change considered (the skip) adds `d(2^k − 1)/|Fp2|` per skipped round.

## Candidate ideas

- **Round-batched streamed windows.** Answer `v = 4–6` streamed rounds per regeneration pass from a `3^v`-point grid with the `eq` factor decomposed ([DDBDT26] §5–6), messages and verifier unchanged. Term: `stream`. Gain: `c` 9 → 2 at 16K and 12 → 2–3 at 128K, about −25% and −34% of `μ` on the corrected profile (E0). First killer: the `2 · 1.5^v` `Fp2` additions and `1.5^v` small×big scalings per pair exceed the HBM time of the passes saved on an H100 (an E1 proxy on the M4 Max over `tessera-sumcheck` can bound it; E2 decides), or the attention tile order cannot deliver consecutive `2^v`-pair blocks.
- **Gruen-corrected rounds with split `eq` in the level kernel.** Reconstruct the degree-3 round polynomial from two node sums, the claim and the leading coefficient; replace the per-instance `eq` table by two `√N` tables and a scalar ([Gru24] §3.2, [DT24], [JoltOpt26]); one `eq` structure per batch. Terms: glue, `c_π`, `c_ℓ`. Gain: a quarter to a third of the level sumcheck's multiplications (E0 from [DT24]'s 8.5N → 5N), prover-only. First killer: the engine's `d`-nodes-by-addition loop ([kernel.rs](../../crates/tessera-sumcheck/src/kernel.rs)) already makes a node cheaper than a multiplication count suggests, so the measured gain is the `eq` part alone (10.3% + binding, EXP-0006).
- **Integer first window with delayed reduction.** Compute the first window's small×small products as `u64` multiplications of the integer leaves with one reduction per block ([DDBDT26] §3.3, Table 4), the primary source for IDEA-0010 (b). Terms: `c_π`, `c_ℓ`. Gain: the `ss` share of round 1 at `κ ≈ 10–29` instead of 3 (E0; Table 4 measures 10–24× on linear combinations). First killer: the per-pair `Fp2` `eq`-weight scaling dominates once the products are cheap, so the gain is capped by the `ss` share, about a third of round 1.
- **Mixed-kind levels after Stwo (OQ-0010-1).** Combine a fraction tree's numerator and denominator claims with a per-instance `λ` inside the oracle and the kinds with the level's `γ`, one `eq` correction per instance ([Stwo26]). Terms: glue, `size`. Gain: resolves OQ-0010-1 with a production reference; no cost change. First killer: RFC-0014 row 5's `Round2Response` shape forces separate `logup` and `product_trees` fields, which the issue must reconcile.
- **Univariate skip for the first streamed rounds.** `k` rounds in one base-field pass with a degree-`2(2^k − 1)` first message ([Gru24] §5). Terms: `stream`, glue. Gain: `k` passes → 1 and 1.4–1.9× fewer evaluations on those rounds (E0). First killer: round batching gives the pass reduction prover-only, while the skip is a tier-A change (messages, labels, a verifier interpolation of degree `2^{k+1} − 2`) with the [zkSec26] class of verifier bug to guard; file only if the window's additions kill it.
- Findings without a seed: [BDT24]'s switchover algorithms (ratio 3, not 16–145: below 1.3×); [DDBDT26] §4's product algorithm (no relation of degree ≥ 4); [DBEMPT26] (subtractions are one op on Goldilocks and the interpolating set is tier A; reopen if an E1 count over Goldilocks shows more than 5%); [DBHR26] and [Bag24] (binary fields); [FT26] and [ICICLE26] (BN254 libraries without extension-field support); [MDG+26] (accelerator model, context only); [Pos-nd] (a layout convention RFC-0010 already fixes).

## Staleness triggers

- A measurement of split `eq`, Gruen's correction or round batching over a 64-bit field with a degree-2 extension (LambdaClass's Lean-zkVM implementation publishing numbers, Plonky3 or SP1 adopting [DDBDT26] §5 on GPU).
- RFC-0020 v0.1 kernel measurements (E2) of the level sumcheck, which replace every E0 count above.
- A GPU implementation of round-batched or windowed sumcheck with per-pass bandwidth figures, or an ICICLE release with extension-field sumcheck.
- The TCC or CCS camera-ready of [BCFFMMZ25] or [DDBDT26] changing the pass-count statements, or Jolt's streaming prover shipping with measured pass counts ([Jolt26]).
- A soundness analysis or formalization of the ragged / front-padded batching beyond the Schwartz–Zippel sketch, or a reported bug in Stwo's batched GKR verifier.
- v0.3's pass counter and v0.5's RISK-10 measurement, which decide whether a streamed pass is bandwidth- or compute-bound at Goldilocks sizes.
