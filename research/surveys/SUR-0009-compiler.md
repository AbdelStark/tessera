## Synthesis

**Question 1: how tight is worst-case analysis, and what replaces the split.** Nobody has measured activation or accumulator ranges at 405B in a form usable here: [SCKL24] stops at 70B (residual-stream scalars beyond 15,000 at a median of 0.2–0.4), [CTC+26]'s abstract has no Llama 3.x, and [Grattafiori24] reports 405B's outliers only as the cap that stopped FP8 decodes from corrupting. The question is also the wrong one for the keys that split: the norm keys and the RoPE keys are products whose width comes from the *accumulator* bound `127 · fan_in · max|x|` (RFC-0006), not from measured activations, and SP-7 forbids any bound the verifier cannot re-derive from public data (RFC-0006 Alternative 4; [CFGZS25] rejects empirical overflow estimates on the same ground). The literature's sound substitute is a public, input-independent bound on the weight side: A2Q/A2Q+ and AXE ([CPP23], [CPPU24], [CFGZS25]) bound `‖w‖₁` per row and show that constraining it to a 16-bit accumulator costs 0.3–0.6 perplexity at W4A4 on Llama3 8B, while the unconstrained worst case `P*` is "the most conservative method". Tessera's need is smaller: the arithmetic of [E0-this] (d) says an INT8 row of length 16,384 with RMS 16–32 has `‖w‖₁ · 128` at 24.7–25.7 bits and, with the `‖y‖₂ ≤ 2^{κ−r_n}√d` fact that follows from `RsqrtRow` exactly as IDEA-0011's bound does, `‖w‖₂ ‖y‖₂` at 23.0–24.0 bits, against the 28 bits (stated `< 2^29`) of interval arithmetic. The RoPE key then falls from 44 bits to 38.0–39.0 with 14-bit constants and 33.0–34.0 with 9-bit constants; it does not reach 32 by norms alone, which corrects the premise of ledger queue item 26 ("below 2^32 with 9-bit constants"): a third ingredient is needed, either `B_max = 34` for the RoPE table alone or the pre-RoPE requantization (key `2 · 128 · 2^14 = 2^22`, but one extra rescale per element, which costs exactly what the split costs). Measured, the fidelity price of 9-bit RoPE constants is nowhere in the literature (one search). The `B_max` arithmetic sharpens SUR-0002's seed: widening *only* the RoPE table to 34 bits adds `2^34 = 1.7×10^10` table-side leaves and 64 GiB of counters (8 GiB per GPU) per 16K-token batch against the `17,408 × 126 × 16,384 = 3.6×10^10` lookups and as many commitments the split costs, a net gain at E0, whereas widening all ten tables (SUR-0002's estimate, 37% of linear lookups) is not. A data-dependent sparse table side (commit only the distinct keys with their table values and multiplicities, check `v = T[k]` arithmetically) dies at E0: RoPE keys over a 2^44 domain are almost all distinct, so it costs 3 commitments and 2 lookups per distinct key on top of the element's own. The debloating result [XMWZWW26] measures how much redundant range checking general zkML compilers emit (3–49% of constraints); Tessera's RFC-0006 has none of that slack by construction, so the finding is a confirmation and a test oracle (its interval domain with the signed-field-window rule is INV-DM-2), as is [DNKBY26]'s sound finite-field interval analysis. Settled: worst-case interval analysis is the right rule (every source that touches soundness agrees), and its slack on the split keys is 3–5 bits that public weight-norm certificates recover. Contested: whether the `servable` profile, not `minimal`, should be the cost model's input ([E0-this] (b): 260,096–310,272 per layer per token, +15% of `μ(1K)` corrected, +21.5% counted); the corpus has used the `minimal` count since 08 §1 while RFC-0005 makes `servable` the default and RISK-3 makes it mandatory for exact mode. Standing of the RFCs: RFC-0006 confirmed in rule and contradicted in input (the public bound `127 · fan_in · max|x|` can be replaced by a certified norm with no loss of soundness); RFC-0009 confirmed (the split is the minimum for a wide key: two non-arithmetic quantities, two commitments); 00 §9 RISK-2's 220,160 is a `minimal`-profile figure and should say so.

**Question 2: per-tensor certificates.** Three mechanisms, by cost per row: (i) relational facts cost nothing (IDEA-0011's `|ρ·res_i| ≤ 2^κ√d`, and its `ℓ2` form `‖y‖₂ ≤ 2^{κ−r_n}√d + √d/2` derived here) because the inequality is already a proof obligation of `RsqrtRow`; (ii) a sum-of-squares certificate `Σ x_i² + a² + b² + c² + d² = B` ([LNP22] transcribed to a sumcheck, [E0-this] (c)) costs four committed scalars, four range lookups and one degree-2 relation per row and proves `‖x‖₂² ≤ B` for the whole row, against `BigIntCompare`'s about 20 commitments and 30 lookups (RFC-0009 'Summary of costs'): for the 378 norm rows per token it is 1,512 against 7,560 commitments, and for a once-per-model weight-row certificate over the `1.99×10^7` rows of a 405B model it is `7.9×10^7` commitments (0.02% of one 16K-token batch's `4.6×10^11`), proven once with the weight commitment and cached by verifiers; (iii) per-element square decompositions ([CGKR22], Groth) cost three commitments per element and lose to one lookup (90 against 40 ps assumed). Zero knowledge and SP-7 are satisfied the same way in all three: the bound `B` is a quantizer-chosen public constant per tensor (a power of two), not the actual norm, so the claim list carries one public number per tensor that the honest prover always meets (the quantizer enforced it on the weights; the gadget implies it on the activations), the verifier re-derives every downstream range from it (INV-IR-5), and no secret-class value is revealed (INV-ARCH-6); a *data-dependent* bound published per input would leak and is excluded, as is the honest-prover failure RFC-0006 Alternative 4 warns about, since the facts hold for every input. The literature offers the mechanisms but no zkML system uses a norm certificate to shrink its range checks ([FQZDC21], [XMWZWW26], [Zhong26] and SUR-0002's systems all range-check per element); the three primary sources for this question are therefore mechanism sources, and the Tessera-specific transcription is this survey's own E0 work.

**Question 3: quantization co-design.** Power-of-two scales are measured cheap at 8 bits: ≤ 0.5% perplexity on Qwen3 1.7–14B when weights are requantized to the rounded scales ([ChenTR26]), and lossless within noise at block-32 granularity on GPT3-175B and LLaMA-7B (MXINT8, [RZM+23]); both are below RFC-0005's gates (ratio ≤ 1.03) by an order of magnitude, so `pot` mode's "half a bit" drawback is unlikely to trigger the `affine` fallback that would double rescale counts (RFC-0005 Alternative 3), though 405B stays unmeasured (RISK-7). Where fidelity does need finer scales, the literature points to three variants that keep the key narrow: block power-of-two scales on the weight side (MX; hidden scalars inside the matmul sumcheck, exponent window limited like RFC-0005's four classes), rotations folded into weights ([AMC+24]; invisible to the proof, lossless 8-bit with RTN), and 8-bit dyadic multipliers ([HCY+24]) in place of RFC-0005's 16-bit `M_c`, which with a norm-certified 2^24 accumulator gives a 2^32 `affine` key, i.e. `Requant1`. Migration ([XLSLLHD23]) is already in `D` and interacts with the certificates of Question 1 by inflating weight-row norms on outlier channels; AXE ([CFGZS25]) shows the quantizer can hold both constraints at PTQ time. Per-token dynamic scales ([HCY+24], Meta's row-wise FP8 in [Grattafiori24]) remain rejected (RFC-0005 Alternative 2): per-row scalars and per-row tables. The rescale *count* is moved by placement, not by scale format: the `servable` profile's three extra rescales (+122,880 per layer per token) buy `< 2^30` accumulators; no source reduces the number of rescales in a Llama block below RFC-0005's templates except by changing `D` ([AMC+24]'s online rotations add a linear op, not a rescale).

**Where Tessera stands.** Every source confirms the architecture of RFC-0006 (worst-case intervals, public re-derivation, lookups only at keys) and RFC-0009 (the split is minimal for a wide key). The exposed inputs are three: the `servable` count is absent from the cost model; the accumulator bound is the loosest sound one when a certified norm would be 3–5 bits tighter at near-zero cost; and `B_max` is a global constant where a per-table limit would let one table pay for its width.

## Candidate ideas

- **Weight-row norm certificate, proven once with the weight commitment** (queue item 26; [CPPU24], [CFGZS25], [LNP22] via [E0-this] (c)). Mechanism: `tessera-quant` enforces `‖w_row‖₂ ≤ 2^k` per tensor (public `k` in the claim list); a four-squares relation per row proves it over the committed weights; RFC-0006's `MatMulWeight` transfer uses `2^k · bound(‖x‖₂)` (or `‖w‖₁ · max|x|`). Terms `C_lin`, `K_lin` through `b`. Gain: Q/K/V and gate/up accumulators 28 → 23–25 bits (E0), the ingredient that lets the RoPE key reach 34 bits; alone it removes no split. Killer: the quantizer cannot hold RMS ≤ 32 per row after SmoothQuant migration without failing the fidelity gate, or the public per-tensor `k` is judged to leak too much about the weights.
- **`‖y‖₂` relational fact from `RsqrtRow`** (IDEA-0011's sibling; [E0-this] (d)). Mechanism: `Σ_i (ρ·res_i)² ≤ N` gives `‖y‖₂ ≤ 2^{κ−r_n}√d + √d/2` for the norm output at zero proof cost; RFC-0006 lists it as a third relational fact. Term `b` of every accumulator fed by a norm output (Q, K, V, gate, up). Gain: −2 bits against `128√d` (E0); combined with the weight certificate, Cauchy–Schwarz gives 23–24-bit accumulators. Killer: `r_n` chosen so that `2^{κ−r_n}` is near 128 (then the fact equals the worst case).
- **Per-table `B_max`: 34 bits for the RoPE requantization table only** (sharpens SUR-0002's "wider `B_max`" seed, OQ-0006-1). Mechanism: `TableLimits.max_log2_rows` per table kind; the RoPE table at 2^34 rows costs `1.7×10^10` leaves and 64 GiB of counters (8 GiB per GPU, sharded) per batch and saves the 17,408-element split (`3.6×10^10` lookups and commitments per 16K-token batch). Terms `C_lin`, `K_lin`: 187,392 → 169,984 per layer per token after IDEA-0011, `μ(1K)` −3.1 points on top of IDEA-0011's −5.5% (−8.4% together, corrected; −12.0% counted) (E0). Killer: the RoPE key still needs 34 bits *after* the norm certificates (38–39 bits with 14-bit constants), so this seed depends on the first two and on 9–10-bit RoPE constants, whose fidelity is unmeasured; or the 08 §4 counter budget (`≤ 2^32 × 4 B` per table) is firm.
- **Cost the `servable` profile in the cost model and in RISK-2** ([E0-this] (b)). Mechanism: a `profile` input to `cost_model.py` with 260,096 (no splits) / 310,272 (splits) per layer per token; 00 §9 states that 220,160 is the `minimal` figure. Term: accounting, `C_lin`, `K_lin`. Gain: a correct baseline (`μ(1K)` 10.2 corrected, 20.9 counted for the default profile); no `μ` saved. Killer: none; the v0.2 commitment report will produce the same number, and the issue is whether the corpus waits for it.
- **Four-squares `ℓ2` certificate replacing `BigIntCompare` in `RsqrtRow`** ([LNP22], [E0-this] (c)). Mechanism: `ρ² u ≤ N` becomes `N − ρ² u = a² + b² + c² + d²` with four committed 28-bit scalars range-checked once, and `N + 1 ≤ (ρ+1)² u` likewise; the limb recomposition of `u` stays. Terms `C_lin`, `K_lin` per row: about 20/30 → about 12/14 per norm row (E0, rough). Gain: 378 rows per token, under 0.01% of `μ`; its value is as the mechanism of the weight certificate, not as a saving. Killer: finding the four squares costs the prover a Rabin–Shallit decomposition per row (fast, but new code), and Lemma 4's exactness argument must be redone for the squares (each square `< 2^56`, sum `< 2^58 < P`).
- **8-bit dyadic multipliers for the `affine` fallback** ([HCY+24]). Mechanism: `M_c` 16 → 8 bits in RFC-0005 'Scale policy'; with a 24-bit norm-certified accumulator the `affine` key is 32 bits and `Requant1` applies, so a fidelity-driven fallback no longer doubles that tensor's count. Term `C_lin`, `K_lin` under RISK-7. Gain: removes the "roughly doubling" of RFC-0005 Alternative 3 for fallback tensors (E0); zero if no tensor falls back. Killer: 8-bit multipliers lose the resolution the fallback was invoked for (I-LLM pairs them with per-token dynamic scales, which RFC-0005 rejects).
- **Weight-side block power-of-two scales (MX) as hidden scalars in the matmul sumcheck** ([RZM+23], [SSXYSW24]). Mechanism: per 32-element block of the reduction axis a committed exponent class `e_b` from a window of ≤ 4, `acc = Σ_b 2^{e_b} Σ_{i∈b} w_i x_i` as a degree-2 virtual relation; no new committed activations. Term: fidelity (RISK-7) and, through avoided `affine` fallbacks, `C_lin`, `K_lin`. Gain: MXINT8's lossless-at-175B fidelity inside `pot` mode (E1 elsewhere, E0 here). Killer: the exponent window widens the accumulator by up to 3 bits (undoing the certificates' gain), and the block exponents are a new hidden-weight class with its own commitment and range checks (`32·405×10^9/32 = 1.3×10^10` scalars).
- **Rotation-aware integer model** ([AMC+24]). Mechanism: Hadamard rotations folded into `W_q, W_k, W_v, W_gate, W_up` and the RMSNorm gains (RFC-0005 'Fold'), no online rotation; static `pot` scales then see outlier-free activations. Term: fidelity (RISK-7), indirectly `b` (smaller `max|x|` lets the quantizer pick larger `r` without saturation). Gain: lossless 8-bit with RTN at 70B (E1, their claim). Killer: QuaRot's online Hadamards (down-projection input, attention) are where its 4-bit gains live, and an exact integer Hadamard adds `log2 d` ≈ 14–16 bits to the next key, forcing a split; folded-only rotations may not move the fidelity gate.

Findings without a seed: [XMWZWW26], [DNKBY26] (confirmations and a test oracle; RFC-0006 emits no redundant checks to remove); [SCKL24], [CTC+26], [Grattafiori24] (measurements to add to the fidelity harness, no proof mechanism; the "massive activation as public per-dimension bias" idea is a change to `D`'s architecture outside this topic); [CGKR22] (per-element square decomposition loses to a lookup); [FQZDC21] (its two optimizations are already RFC-0009's `pack` offset and `z_lo` remainder); [Zhong26] (certifies properties, not inferences); [Jo26] (names the lever; SUR-0002 carries its seeds); [XLSLLHD23] (already step 4 of RFC-0005; its interaction with the certificates is inside the first seed); [CPP23] (superseded by [CPPU24]).
