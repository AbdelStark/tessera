## Synthesis

**Question 1: the cost of a LogUp-GKR lookup, and where 40 ps comes from.** Settled: the 40 ps is an assumption. The source says so ("to be measured", §5) and no implementation publishes a per-lookup GPU figure; Stwo's open repository has no GPU backend [Stwo26], SP1's GPU crate has kernels but no numbers [SP1GPU26], Ziren and Expander publish whole-prover throughputs only [DYH+26], [Polyhedra24]. Contested, and the survey's main result: the analytic count [E0-this] puts the textbook algorithm at about 24.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents per lookup (101 ps at the H100's int32 peak, 169 ps at measured utilization) and the version with a numerators-1 first layer at 18.5 (76 ps peak, 127 ps at 60%). The byte count is 288 B per lookup unfused (86 ps at 3.35 TB/s), 192 B with the two-round fusion SP1 ships (57 ps) and 120 B with eight fused rounds (36 ps). So the memory side can reach the 15–20 ps target with deep fusion but the compute side cannot without an algorithmic change: under these assumptions `c_ℓ` is 2–3× *above* 40 ps, not below it, and the cost model says `lookup_ps = 80` raises `μ` by 27.5% / 28.7% / 28.3% / 25.6% at 1K / 4K / 16K / 128K (E0, primary, `cost_model.py --set lookup_ps=80`), against −13.7% to −14.4% for `lookup_ps = 20` and −16.0% to −17.9% for 15. Two external anchors agree in direction: Expander's 111–122 ps per GKR gate on an H100 [Polyhedra24] (`claim`), and Ziren's 12 MHz on an RTX 5090 with LogUp-GKR at 42% of kernel time, which at SP1's 15 interactions per row gives about 2.3 ns per interaction [DYH+26] (secondary arithmetic). Neither is Tessera's kernel, but both sit far above 40 ps. The two micro-architectural moves of bet 1a are already standard in production code, so they are not Tessera's edge: base-field numerators and the numerators-1 first layer ([Stwo26] `LogUpSingles`, [SP1GPU26] `felt_t numeratorZero`) and round fusion ([SP1GPU26] `two_round` kernels). RFC-0020's v0.1 measurement is the only way to close this; the survey's recommendation is to treat `c_ℓ ∈ [40, 130]` ps as the open interval and to plan the v1.0 ceilings (`μ(16K) ≤ 45`) against the upper end, which `lookup_ps = 80` already approaches (45.1 at 16K).

**Question 2: one-hot arguments under hash-based commitments.** Settled against one-hot for Tessera, now with numbers. Lasso commits `3cm + cn^{1/c}` small elements, 9 per lookup at `n = 2^24`, `c = 3` [STW23]; Shout commits `d·K^{1/d}` Booleans per lookup, 64 at best for `K = 2^24` [ST25], [JoltDocs26]. Tessera's commitment charges 30 ps per 64-bit element with no pay-per-bit discount (RFC-0011), so these cost 270 ps and ≥ 1,920 ps per lookup before any sumcheck, against one committed value in LogUp-GKR. The Akita abstract states the structural reason in one sentence: commitment time proportional to nonzero entries "is essential to Twist and Shout" and hash-based schemes do not provide it [DBK+26]; Jolt's own documentation ties its one-hot cost to Dory's pay-per-bit MSM [JoltDocs26], Jolt Atlas uses HyperKZG [JoltAtlas26], and Lattice Jolt moved to a lattice commitment for the same reason [a16z26]. This confirms RFC-0008 Alternatives Considered 1 and sharpens its "Reopens if": a hash-based commitment that charges per bit (ring switching, packed encodings; agenda `commitments` Question 2, NG-5) would bring Shout's 64 Booleans to one element and reopen the comparison on sumcheck cost alone, where [E0-this] (≈ 18.5 `Fp2` multiplications) and Shout's `O(d)` reads plus Booleanity and Hamming-weight checks are of the same order. The separation principle [Jo26] forbids exploiting the table's values to cut the per-lookup term of a one-hot argument, and the same holds for LogUp-GKR, whose fraction tree never reads table values. It leaves open the lookup count, the committed address (key) width, and the once-per-proof table term, which is exactly where RFC-0008 spends structure (verifier-side evaluation in `O(n · core)`). No source contradicts RFC-0008 Alternatives Considered 2 (univariate lookups) or 3 (base-field challenges).

**Question 3: sharing and removing lookups.** Settled: tuple tables are free in LogUp-GKR, since `M` columns compress into one `Fp2` element per leaf ([PH23], RFC-0008 'Relation'), and the design already uses this for the hidden class and for rescale-plus-nonlinearity (RFC-0005 "tables absorb rescales", RFC-0009 `Requant1`). Counting the `minimal` profile's 137,216 lookups per layer per token at 405B from RFC-0006's lowering: two norm rescales (2 × 16,384), RoPE on Q and K (16,384 + 1,024), V (1,024), two residual rescales (2 × 16,384) and SiLU (53,248); the sum is 137,216 exactly, so every lookup is one non-arithmetic output and none is a duplicate (E0, primary, from the RFC tables). Merging à la zkGPT [QSL+25] has one candidate, deferring a norm's per-element rescale past the following linear map (RMSNorm gains are already folded into the weights, RFC-0005 'Fold'); it fails on key width: the unrescaled operand widens the downstream `Requant` keys past `B_max = 32`, turning 18,432 single lookups into splits while removing 16,384, a net loss in `minimal` and forbidden in `servable` (S1). Turning lookups into arithmetic fails for the same reason it succeeds elsewhere: every rounding needs an integrality check, and in Tessera that check is a lookup, so Range-Arithmetic's two range proofs per rounding [RKM25] double the count, and a piecewise-polynomial exponential still needs a range lookup on its argument and shrinks only the table side, irrelevant at `N ≫ T` (exp lookups per 16K-token proof: about 2.2×10^12 against `T = 2^24`). The remaining count lever is the split gadget (RISK-2, up to +60% commitments): a wider `B_max` trades table-side leaves (`2^b` per table kind per proof, about 4×10^10 for ten 2^32 tables, 9% of the 4.6×10^11 linear-layer lookups per 16K-token proof) and multiplicity memory (`2^b × 4 B` per table, 08 §4) against the second lookup of every split; this belongs to the `compiler` topic and is seeded below with its numbers.

**Where Tessera stands.** RFC-0008's protocol choice is confirmed by every source; its cost assumption is the exposed flank. The design's edge over zkVM provers is not the kernel but the count: one lookup per non-arithmetic output and virtual keys, which [Sou25] and [QSL+25] work hard to approach from committed-index and rounding-heavy starting points.

## Candidate ideas

- **Calibrate the op count on a CPU (E1) against Stwo's `prove_batch`.** Mechanism: run Stwo's `lookups` bench (`SimdBackend`, `LogUpSingles` and `LogUpGeneric`, 2^16 leaves) beside a Tessera fraction-tree prototype in the same harness and fit nanoseconds per leaf to the 18.5 / 24.5 `Fp2`-multiplication model of [E0-this]. Term `c_ℓ` (its uncertainty). Gain: replaces a `[40, 130]` ps interval with a calibrated constant before any GPU exists. Killer: CPU SIMD utilization differs enough from the GPU int32 pipeline that the fit does not transfer.
- **Fused fraction-tree kernel: eight sumcheck rounds per kernel with the level resident in shared memory.** Mechanism: the two-round fusion of [SP1GPU26] extended to `k = 8`, cutting HBM traffic from 288 B to 120 B per lookup. Term `c_ℓ`. Gain: memory floor 86 → 36 ps (E0); only pays if the compute floor (56–76 ps at peak) is also lowered. Killer: `Fp2` register and shared-memory pressure at 2^8 entries per block, or the kernel being compute-bound already, in which case fusion is worth nothing.
- **Small-value first layer.** Mechanism: leaf denominators are `(α_0 − key − β_0·value, α_1 − β_1·value)`, so the leaf and first-layer products use 32-bit × 64-bit multiplications and the first-layer sumcheck extrapolates base-field columns; combine with the numerators-1 specialization [Stwo26]. Term `c_ℓ`. Gain: the first layer is half the work; the count falls from 24.5 to about 18.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents and lower with narrow keys (E0). Killer: the saving is in multiplications the compiler already schedules as 32-bit, i.e. the kernel does it implicitly.
- **Univariate skip on the first `k` variables of each layer's sumcheck** (agenda `sumcheck` Question 1). Mechanism: Gruen's skip proves `k` variables of the largest layer in one round with a degree-`3·2^k` message, removing `k` folding passes. Term `c_ℓ`, glue. Gain: unquantified here; the folding term is 2 of the 10 `Fp2` multiplications per pair-round in [E0-this]. Killer: message interpolation cost and verifier degree at `k ≥ 3`.
- **Wider `B_max` (34 or 36) to turn `RequantSplit` back into `Requant1` where RISK-2 bites** (agenda `compiler` Question 1). Mechanism: RFC-0006 'Gadget selection' with `TableLimits.max_log2_rows = 34`; the table side costs `2^b` leaves per table kind per proof and `2^b × 4 B` of multiplicity counters (08 §4). Term `K_lin`, `C_lin`. Gain: up to the RISK-2 excess (+60% commitments) against +4× table-side leaves (about 1.7×10^11 for ten tables at `b = 34`, 37% of linear-layer lookups per 16K-token proof; E0). Killer: 64 GB of counters per table at `b = 34`, or fewer splits than RISK-2 fears once the v0.2 commitment report measures ranges.
- **State the LogUp characteristic bound in RFC-0008.** Mechanism: add "`N + T < p`" as an explicit soundness precondition with the per-proof count (`N ≈ 2^41` at 16K tokens), citing [EH24] for the regime where it fails. Term: soundness (no cost). Gain: closes an implicit assumption. Killer: none; a documentation change.
