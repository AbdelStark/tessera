**Excluded.** Pairing- and discrete-log-based commitments (KZG, Hyrax, Dory, HyperKZG; not post-quantum, [baseline.md §1](../baseline.md#1-what-an-improvement-must-preserve)); proximity-gap theory beyond what fixes a scheme's query count ([SUR-0001](SUR-0001-proximity.md)); hash throughput as such (agenda `hash`, queue item 11), except where a GPU figure bears directly on `c_c`; recursion as a proof-size lever (NG-3), except where a scheme's own recursion is its mechanism (Ligerito, Polygero); the Z_{2^k}-ring SNARK Rotor (ePrint 2026/2175), whose evaluation was withdrawn pending corrected parameters.

**What no source answers.** No published measurement of a hash-based commitment per element on an H100 over Goldilocks exists in any form found here: not from Plonky3, ICICLE, sppark, Stwo, SP1, RISC Zero, cuPQC (BabyBear and KoalaBear only) or any 2024–2026 paper. The GPU figures below are an AMD Goldilocks NTT, a Blackwell-workstation Poseidon2 Merkle tree, a Blackwell SHA-3 throughput, an L4 Poseidon2 Merkle tree, an A40 256-bit NTT and a code-based GPU encoder whose GPU is unnamed; each is translated to Tessera's shape as E0 and labeled so. No BLAKE3 GPU measurement exists anywhere found (one Vulkan fork on a Tesla T4, impractical by its author's account).

## Findings

Shape of Tessera's commitment for every translation below: 5.8×10^5 rows (messages) of `k = 2^22` Goldilocks elements, RS at rate 1/2 to `n = 2^23` symbols on a coset, `P_A ≤ 200` mask coefficients, chained BLAKE3 column digests over row blocks `B = 32`; 2.4×10^12 committed elements per 405B 16K/16K unit; one witness pass commits, one regeneration pass opens ([RFC-0011](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0011-advice-commitment.md), [RFC-0018](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md)). In Ligerito's notation Tessera's rows are the columns of `X̃` (`n = 2^22`), Tessera's columns are the rows of `X` (length 5.8×10^5), and a combined row `u_g` is `y_r`.

## Synthesis

**Question 1: the fastest hash-based commitment per element on a GPU, and one-pass streaming.**

1. *Nobody has published the number, and the nearest measurements say it is high.* No H100 Goldilocks commitment figure exists (Method). The only Goldilocks GPU NTT in the open runs at 10.6 ps per butterfly on an RX 7900 XTX at its memory-bound plateau and 17.3 ps at Tessera's row size 2^23 ([Uulong26], E1 on another GPU). Tessera's encoder is 34 butterflies per committed element (EXP-0002, E0), so a row-at-a-time NTT in HBM costs 360–590 ps per element on that GPU and, scaled by HBM bandwidth to an H100, about 100–170 ps for the NTTs alone (E0). The hash side, on the only GPU Merkle figure at Tessera's leaf volume, is 219 GB/s of leaf data for Poseidon2 on a Blackwell workstation GPU ([NVIDIA26a], `claim`), 36 ps per 8-byte element; BLAKE3 has no GPU figure at all. Together these put `c_c` at roughly 140–200 ps for an unfused kernel on H100-class hardware (E0 from E1 on other GPUs), the upper half of EXP-0002's `[58, 157]` ps interval and RFC-0020's `ρ_c > 4` band. At 150 ps the cost model gives `μ(1K) = 16.2` and `μ(16K) = 65.0` (+82%, +85% over corrected; both SC-2 ceilings fail); at 240 ps, 21.7 and 87.4; at 360 ps, 29.0 and 117.3 (E0, `cost_model.py --set commit_ps`). The lever that these measurements single out is memory: [Uulong26]'s own plateau and [Rotko26]'s "memory-bandwidth bound" agree with ZKProphet's "limited by data movement" ([VYTA25]), so a kernel that keeps the 2^22-element message (32 MB) in L2 and fuses the coset NTT with the column hashing (IDEA-0003's byte table; EXP-0002's finding that bytes do not bind at the counted op counts assumed a fused layout) is what separates the 58 ps floor from the 360 ps measurement. This is the external anchor IDEA-0004's kill criterion asked for; it does not kill the method, it moves the interval's centre up.

2. *Linear-time and sketched codes beat an NTT in operation count, not yet on a GPU, and never in Tessera's field.* The encoder counts are 8n additions (Blaze, [BCFRRZ24]), (3 + ε)n additions plus hashing 1.0n elements (Bolt, [GNR26]) against Tessera's 34 butterflies and hashing 2n symbols (E0); on CPUs Bolt is 20% faster than Brakedown and 3–4× faster than RS schemes at 2^30 ([GNR26], E1), and SIMD Spielman codes only just reach RS parity ([KDHR26]). All of Blaze, Bolt, BrakingBase, SwitchFold and Quasar open by code switching into a folding IOPP over the full vector, and Blaze and Bolt live over binary fields. The one GPU result, Quasar's QA-code encoder (32.9–148.3× its own 32-thread CPU, GPU unnamed, [JLXYY26]), is over a 127-bit Mersenne field. RFC-0011 Alternative 3 stands; what is new is that the gap it rejected on ("NTT is fast on GPUs") is unmeasured for Goldilocks and that Bolt's 3n-addition encoder is the number an NTT kernel has to be judged against once E2 exists.

3. *FRI-family schemes are one-pass exactly when their first fold is an interleaved random combination, and Tessera already performs that fold.* Naively folding the 2.4×10^12-element advice needs the vector resident or regenerated per round: after the first fold the vector is 2^40 `Fp2` elements (17.6 TB), the eight GPUs hold about 1.6 TB free, so five folds (five regeneration passes, `n_regen` +5, about +56% of `μ(1K)`, E0) precede a resident FRI; this confirms RFC-0011 Alternative 2 with a count. ReedWeave ([JLXYYZ26]) is the published scheme whose first fold is "a random linear combination of `m` component codewords on a common domain", which is RFC-0011 step 4 with `m = 5.8×10^5`, computed in the one regeneration pass the opening already spends; everything after it (binary folding, queries) runs on a 2^23-symbol row. So the honest answer to "which FRI-family schemes admit a one-pass prover" is: those applied to the combined rows, not to the advice, and WHIR, STIR, DeepFold and ReedWeave all qualify there, with the Johnson-regime MCA of [BCGM26] available to WHIR (SUR-0001). The cost is a second proof system in the verifier (SP-6), and the gain is proof size and verifier NTTs, not `c_c` (point 5).

4. *Hashing is a quarter of the element and the GPU evidence favours re-measuring the hash choice.* EXP-0002 counts hashing at 19–26% of the committed element (E0). RFC-0004 chose BLAKE3 and rejected Poseidon2 as "about 10× slower on GPUs"; the GPU record found here is 219 GB/s for Poseidon2-BabyBear Merkle leaves and 388–891 GB/s for SHA-2/3 at 8 KB messages on Blackwell ([NVIDIA26a], [NVIDIA26b], `claim`), and nothing for BLAKE3. That is the `hash` survey's question (queue item 11); for this survey it means the 530 GB/s RISK-8 implies is plausible for a long-message hash on current silicon and unsupported for 288-byte chained inputs, where EXP-0002 measured 20–40% dearer compressions out of cache. A field-native hash would also change the recursion picture (NG-3), which Flock's 82k BLAKE3 compressions per core-second ([BRW26]) prices on the other side.

**Question 2: small-value commitments.**

5. *Hash-based pay-per-bit needs a subfield tower, and Goldilocks has none below 64 bits.* Binius commits bits "with no embedding overhead" by Brakedown over binary towers ([DP25]); ring switching ([DP26]) generalizes this to any large-field PCS, with commitment cost equal to the large-field scheme's per bit. Both need `F' ⊂ F` with the packed small-field values forming the large field's coordinates. Over Goldilocks the only subfield step is `Fp ⊂ Fp2`, so ring switching gives exactly what Tessera does today (commit 64-bit `Fp` values, challenge in `Fp2`) and nothing further; packing eight INT8 values into one `Fp` element by `Σ 2^{8j} X_j` loses the ability to open each `X_j` separately, because a single opening yields one linear combination (E0). The hash-based routes to NG-5's 2–4× are therefore a 31-bit field (2× for the ≤ 31-bit lookup outputs; RFC-0002 Alternatives, "reopens if measured rescale counts and GPU arithmetic show a net win") or a binary tower (rejected), not a new commitment on the current field. Zero knowledge is unaffected either way (hiding is by mask and blinding rows, [RFC-0013](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0013-zero-knowledge.md)).

6. *Lattice commitments have reached hash-based prover cost on CPU for bit-valued data and publish no per-bit figure, no GPU figure and no hiding statement.* LaBinius proves BLAKE3 at most 1.2× slower than the hash-based Binius and Flock provers on one core, with proofs under 100 KiB after LaBRADOR compression ([OS26], E1); Akita's commitment time is proportional to nonzero entries, its proofs 58–68 KB and its verifier 22–109× Greyhound's, with no absolute prover time on the page ([DBK+26]); Greyhound 53 or 93 KB at 2^30 ([NS24]), Hachi 55 KB ([NOZ26]), Maltese 335 KB ([CNT26]); Hyperwolf is withdrawn ([ZGX25]). None of the landing pages states hiding; Ajtai-style commitments are hiding with added randomness at a norm cost, but that is this survey's reading, not a source's. Every lattice scheme is Module-SIS, hence post-quantum, and every one targets proof size and verifier time, which are not Tessera's binding constraints. The one GPU lead is a repository name (icicle-labrador, [zkSec25]). For Tessera, the lattice route to NG-5 (ledger item 36: Module-SIS commitment of lookup outputs as INT8 tensor-core GEMMs) remains an E0 count: a Module-SIS commitment of a `b`-bit value costs about `⌈b/log2 β⌉` ring MACs for decomposition base `β`, so the pay-per-bit discount against a 64-bit element is at most `64/b` before the ring dimension and modulus (hundreds of 32- or 64-bit coefficients per ring element) are paid, and no source gives those constants for a GPU. The "pay per nonzero" property that makes Akita essential to Twist and Shout does not help Tessera's dense INT8 outputs; it helps one-hot lookups, which is SUR-0002's closed question.

7. *Packed and jagged encodings are already in the design.* The jagged PCS ([HJRRR26]) exists to avoid committing padding, at 5 field multiplications per element; RFC-0011's layout rule 4 skips zero rows at no cost and orders padded dimensions so that padding forms whole rows. Nothing to adopt.

**Question 3: Ligerito and logarithmic randomness at the same soundness.**

8. *Ligerito removes the combined rows, not the opened columns.* In Tessera's shape level 1 of Ligerito is RFC-0011's opening pass: the 17 combined rows are the `y_r` of 17 matrix-vector products with coefficient vectors `c_g` and `ξ`, computed in one regeneration pass. Ligerito's proof-size term for level 1 is `|S_1| 2^{k'_1} log|F|` plus Merkle paths: `τ_A × 5.8×10^5 × 8 B = 0.72 GB`, unchanged, because every opened column carries one symbol per message and the verifier must hash it (recursion over hashing is NG-3). What Ligerito removes is the `2^{k_1} log|F|` term: the 17 rows of 2^22 `Fp2` (1.14 GB) are committed as one 2^26-element matrix (for example 2^13 × 2^13 at rate 1/2), the `17 τ_A` column checks `Σ_row c_g(row) col_j[row] = enc(u_g)(x_j) + Z_H(x_j) M_g(x_j)` and the 17 evaluation checks `⟨u_g, eq(col_g)⟩ = Σ γ v` become `17 (τ_A + 1)` inner products of the committed rows with public vectors, batched by random `β` into one partial sumcheck of 13 rounds (3 `Fp2` elements each), after which level 2 opens `|S_2|` rows of 2^13 `Fp2` (155 × 128 KiB ≈ 20 MB; about 3 MB with a 2^10 × 2^16 shape) and the final 2^8–2^13 elements go in the clear. Proof: about 2.0 → 1.0 GB (−48%; 0.92 GB under the Draft RFC-0021's `τ_A = 130`), E0. Prover: encoding and hashing 2^26 elements plus a sumcheck over them, negligible against 2.4×10^12. Verifier: the 17 coset NTTs of size 2^23 over `Fp2` and the reading of 1.14 GB disappear; the `17 τ_A` inner products of length 5.8×10^5 over the opened columns stay (the verifier computes the left-hand sides itself, as Ligerito's verifier computes `X_S r̄`). Soundness: unique decoding at both levels, with level 2 adding `(2 k'_1 + |S_2| + 1)/|Fp2|` and a proximity term at rate 1/2 that needs `|S_2| = τ_A` (155 live, 193 non-interactive) for the same bits; the total stays within RFC-0014's budget because the added terms are `2^{-120}`-class (E0). Zero knowledge: level 2 reveals `|S_2|` rows of the committed combined rows and a sumcheck over them, a subset of what sending them in the clear reveals today, so RFC-0013's hiding argument (whatever its repair under ledger items 1–2) carries over unchanged.

9. *Succinct verification of the recursion needs coefficient-form rows.* Ligerito's verifier is succinct only when the generator rows are tensorizable (`g_i = ⊗ (1, B_j(t_i))`), so that the batched vector `w̃_ℓ` can be partially evaluated in `O(k)` per row ([NA25] §6.6). RS in evaluation form (RFC-0011 today: `u_g` holds values on `H`) has Lagrange generator rows, which are not tensorizable, so the verifier would build each of the `17 τ_A` batched vectors of length 2^22 explicitly (about 1.1×10^10 `Fp2` operations, within the 300 s budget but not succinct); RS in coefficient form (IDEA-0002) has monomial rows `(1, x_j, x_j^2, …) = ⊗_i (1, x_j^{2^i})`, which are, and the vanishing mask `Z_H(x_j) M_g(x_j)` is monomial in `x_j` too. IDEA-0002 and a Ligerito-style level 2 therefore compose: the first removes the interpolation NTT from the prover, the second removes the 17 verifier NTTs, and together they make the verifier's work in the combined rows logarithmic.

10. *Logarithmic randomness buys randomness and recursion, not size.* [DP24log] and [AER24] replace `rows` uniform coefficients by `log2(rows)` (the `eq(ξ*, row)` tensor RFC-0011 step 3 draws) at an error of `ϑ · n/|F|` instead of `n/|F|` (SUR-0001; `ϑ = 20`, `2^{-100.7}` against `2^{-105}`), and [AER24] halves the constant. The proof size is unchanged. What it buys structurally is that the combined row is a partial evaluation of the committed multilinear, which is the fact Ligerito's recursion batches on; a uniform-coefficient combination (SUR-0001's seed to remove the `ϑ` factor) would still admit the recursion, since the batched inner products are arbitrary public vectors, but would lose the tensor structure that makes `w̃_ℓ` cheap, so the two seeds trade against each other and must be scored together.

11. *Prover cost at the same soundness.* Ligerito's level 1 is Ligero, so its prover cost per element is Ligero's: on CPUs, 74.5 ns per 32-bit coefficient aggregate over 8 M1 threads at rate 1/4 ([NA25]) and 13.9 ns over 16 Ryzen cores ([Rotko26]), against EXP-0002's 5.1 ns per 64-bit element over 12 M4 cores at rate 1/2 for encode plus hash alone (E1, three machines, context only, research rule 4). The recursion adds nothing measurable to the prover at Tessera's sizes (point 8). Against WHIR, Ligerito claims 10× the prover speed at smaller proofs in the unique-decoding regime ([Ang25], `claim`); against Polygero ([FS26]), Ligerito stays in unique decoding while Polygero's polynomial challenges go beyond it with Lean-checked schedules and no published numbers; the Johnson-regime version of a Ligerito level 2 is the open item.

**Where Tessera stands.** RFC-0011's structure (interleaved RS rows, one-pass commit, one-pass fold to combined rows, column queries) is the shape every 2025–2026 scheme in this corpus either shares (Ligerito level 1, ReedWeave's first fold, PIP_FRI's fragments) or cannot stream without. Its cost assumption is the exposed flank, as it was for RFC-0008: the external measurements place the NTT-and-hash element at 100–360 ps on today's GPUs rather than 30, and the only published route below that is a memory-resident fused kernel, not a different code. Its proof size has a published fix for the larger half: the 1.14 GB of combined rows is a solved problem on paper (Ligerito, ReedWeave, WHIR on the folded row), and the 0.72 GB of opened columns is fixed by `τ`, by the row count and by nothing else short of recursion.

## Candidate ideas

- **Ligerito-style level 2 over the 17 combined rows** ([NA25]): commit them as one 2^26-element `Fp2` Ligero matrix, batch the `17 (τ_A + 1)` column and evaluation checks into one 13-round partial sumcheck, open `|S_2| = τ_A` rows; moves `size` (2.0 → about 1.0 GB, −48%, E0) and `verify` (17 size-2^23 `Fp2` NTTs removed). First killer: SP-6, a second-level Ligero verifier and a batched partial sumcheck written into `tv-*` from a superseding RFC on RFC-0011 steps 5–8 and RFC-0014's transcript schedule (tier A); then the unique-decoding term at rate 1/2 for level 2 and the interplay with RFC-0013's repaired blinding.
- **Coefficient-form rows as the enabler of a succinct recursion** ([NA25] §6.6, IDEA-0002): monomial generator rows are tensorizable, Lagrange rows are not; moves `verify` (logarithmic work in the combined rows) on top of IDEA-0002's `c_c` saving. First killer: the vanishing mask and `enc(u)(x_j)` in coefficient form must keep the exact soundness statement of RFC-0011 step 8.3 (IDEA-0002's own open item).
- **WHIR or ReedWeave-style IOPP on the folded row instead of Ligerito** ([JLXYYZ26], [ACFY24]): same one-pass fold, binary folding after it, Johnson-regime MCA available ([BCGM26]), proofs of about 60 KiB per 2^22 row at 100 bits; moves `size`, `verify`, `τ` (fewer queries than unique decoding at level 2). First killer: ReedWeave's proximity analysis of the interleaved first fold is unread (PDF blocked), and a WHIR verifier in `tv-*` is a larger SP-6 surface than a second Ligero level.
- **L2-resident fused encode-and-hash kernel, anchored on the AMD plateau** ([Uulong26], IDEA-0003): the external measurement says an HBM-bound row NTT costs 100–360 ps per element (E0 from E1); the saving is the difference between that and the 58 ps floor, that is up to −60% of `c_c` (−13% of `μ(16K)` at the counted price). First killer: needs an H100 (E2; IDEA-0003 is shelved on exactly this), and the 64 MB codeword exceeds the 50 MB L2, so the fusion must hash tiles before the full row exists.
- **Re-anchor IDEA-0004's interval on external GPU figures** ([Uulong26], [NVIDIA26a], [VYTA25]): restate `c_c ∈ [100, 360]` ps as the planning interval (E0 from E1 on other GPUs) and re-score the unit-cost ideas under it; moves `c_c`'s uncertainty, the scoreboard. First killer: the AMD kernel is unoptimized for L2-resident sizes and the Blackwell hash is not BLAKE3, so the interval's floor is soft; an H100 reproduction with [Ozcan25] or zeknox ([Loghin26]) is the check.
- **Hash choice re-measured on GPU** ([NVIDIA26a], [NVIDIA26b]; agenda `hash`, queue item 11): Poseidon2 at 219 GB/s of leaves and SHA-3 at 388–891 GB/s on Blackwell against no BLAKE3 figure; moves `c_c` (hash share 19–26%) and the recursion price (NG-3). First killer: 288-byte chained inputs, where per-call overhead and out-of-cache compressions dominate (EXP-0002), and RFC-0004's cryptanalysis-record argument.
- **Wider advice rows (2^25) to shrink the opened columns** (SUR-0001 seed, now with Ligerito's accounting): the 0.72 GB is `|S_1| × rows × 8 B` and only `τ` or the row count can lower it; 8× fewer rows is −0.63 GB; moves `size`, `verify`. First killer: a 2^26-symbol codeword (512 MB) and a 16 GB block buffer at `B = 32`, and the HBM-bound NTT cost grows with the row (point 1).
- **Lattice commitment of lookup outputs** (ledger item 36; [DBK+26], [OS26]): keep as E0 only; the survey found no per-bit or GPU figure and no hiding statement, and the pay-per-nonzero property does not apply to dense INT8 outputs. First killer: a second commitment scheme and its verifier in `tv-*` (SP-6) for a discount that is at most `64/b` before ring constants.
- Findings without a seed, and why: [BCFRRZ24], [GNR26], [NST24], [CYY26], [JLXYY26] (binary or non-Goldilocks fields, full-vector folding; their encoder counts are the yardstick, not a design), [GLHQTZ25], [LZC+26] (resident folding; DeepFold's list-decoding queries belong to SUR-0001), [BCFW25] (accumulation, NG-3), [GLQXZ26] (a SIS hash per column step is not cheaper than BLAKE3 on this evidence), [KDHR26], [HKPR25] (Spielman codes at RS parity on CPU, 1.7 µs per element on FPGA-class work), [DP25], [DP26], [Irreducible23], [Binius26] (binary towers, RFC-0002), [HJRRR26] (already achieved by RFC-0011 rule 4), [NS24], [NOZ26], [CNT26], [BS22], [zkSec25] (proof-size schemes without prover figures), [ZGX25] (withdrawn), [Jan24], [Loghin26], [Ozcan25], [BRW26], [WHV24], [Ska26] (context), [AER24], [DP24log] (in force), [FS26], [Ang25] (seeded in SUR-0001).
