## Method

**Frame.** Both terms this topic moves are fixed by [RFC-0018 'Pass structure'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#pass-structure): `n_regen` is the sum of the native-cost column of its pass table (P1 witness and commit 1×, P3 stage regeneration ≤ 1×, P4 opening 1×, P2 streamed leaf rounds on attention tiles), estimated at 4 (RISK-9); `stream(S) = c(S) · p_s · A · S / native(S)` with `c(S) = ⌈log2(N_level · 32 B / free HBM)⌉` and `p_s = 7 ps` per score per pass (RISK-10). The opening pass is required by [RFC-0011 'Opening protocol'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0011-advice-commitment.md#opening-protocol) step 4; the partitioning across eight GPUs and the per-round all-reduce by [RFC-0018 'Partitioning'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#partitioning). The decisions a result could reopen are RFC-0018 Alternatives 1 (NVMe instead of regeneration), 2 (per-tile challenges), 3 (multi-variable rounds) and open question OQ-0018-1 (streaming against smaller proof units). Lever row in [baseline.md §6](../baseline.md#6-levers-which-technique-class-moves-which-term): "Pass fusion, checkpoint placement, streaming-friendly sumcheck, proof units, multi-GPU partitioning".

## Synthesis

**Settled.**

1. *A Ligero-family commitment costs exactly two full passes, and nothing published removes the second.* Commit needs one pass over the advice (streaming rows into column chains, [RFC-0011](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0011-advice-commitment.md)); opening needs the combined rows `Σ_row c_g(row) · A_row`, whose coefficients depend on challenges drawn after the commitment, so the rows must be seen again. Every space-efficient system in the corpus has the same shape, "one pass per adaptive phase": [Nye25]'s wires/permutation/quotient/openings passes, Hobbit's streaming oracles, Scribe's read-write streams. The only way to avoid the second regeneration is to store the advice itself: 19.6–21.0 TB for a 405B 16K/16K unit (E0), which at 56 GB/s of NVMe is 340 s against 12 s of native regeneration (E0). RFC-0018 Alternative 1 is confirmed with numbers, and Scribe's 10% overhead at 2^28 gates does not transfer across the 2^13 gap in scale. The one exception is small units: a 1K-token unit's linear-layer advice is 0.23 TB (E0) and fits the 1 TiB host budget, where PCIe at 8 × 128 GB/s reads it in about 0.2–0.5 s.

2. *The floor for `n_regen` is about 2 full-forward equivalents plus attention-only passes, not 4.* RFC-0018's own table sums to P1 (1×) + P4 (1×) + P3 (≤ 1×, mostly elementwise from checkpoints) + P2. With all 126 layer boundaries checkpointed (135 GB per 16K-token unit, E0), regeneration is per layer and [CXZG16]'s `√n` tradeoff is unnecessary. P3 cannot share P4: the last stage's point is known only when that stage ends, and P4 needs all stages finished; P3 passes can only be absorbed into P2 when a stage's point is known before a streamed pass begins (RFC-0018 'Fusion'). What remains open is how much of P3 is GEMM-shaped; RFC-0018 says "most are elementwise". The cost model says `n_regen = 3` is worth −11% of `μ` at 1K and `n_regen = 2` is worth −22% (E0, [tools/cost_model.py](../tools/cost_model.py)); the measurement that settles it is v0.3's pass counter (SC-2 plan), not a paper.

3. *Streamed rounds need not be one pass each.* [CMT12]'s one-pass-per-round prover is the pessimistic end of a tradeoff: [BCFFMMZ25] answers several rounds per pass for products of multilinears at `O(N(log log N + k))` time, and the Jolt roadmap [Jolt26] relies on exactly this ("each pass over the trace can cover many rounds"). RFC-0018 Alternative 3 rejected multi-variable *rounds* because the message grows as `(d+1)^w`; the time-space provers keep the protocol and messages unchanged and move only the prover's bookkeeping, so that rejection does not cover them.

4. *Hobbit's tile folding is the strongest alternative to `c(S)` streamed rounds.* It is a protocol change, not a prover change: fold `N/B` tile instances into one in a single regeneration pass, prove the size-`B` instance in memory, and reduce the `N/B` tile evaluations at the common point to one claim with a `log(N/B)`-variable sumcheck. At Tessera's sizes `N/B` is 22, 87, 346 and 2,771 tiles at 1K, 4K, 16K and 128K (E0), so the extra proof is kilobytes and the final claim is at one point, which removes the objection of RFC-0018 Alternative 2 (per-tile points multiplying opening groups, 64 MB per group). If the fold costs about one pass of today's `p_s`, the streaming term falls from 11.24 to about 1.25 at 16K (`μ` 35.1 → 25.1, −28%) and from 77.9 to 6.5 at 128K (`μ` 171 → 100, −42%) (E0, dividing the term by `c`). The caveats are real and specific: a degree-3 summand's cross terms cost `O(d)` `Fp2` multiplications per leaf per fold, the Libra masking of RFC-0013 must cover the folded instance, and the folding adds `≈ d · N/B / |Fp2|` to the soundness error (negligible). The per-tile challenges are sequential transcript squeezes, which RFC-0014 already allows in both modes since every sumcheck challenge follows from the one seed.

5. *Distributed sumcheck is a solved problem at the communication level, and Tessera already has the right protocol.* deVirgo [XZCZ+22] sends one round polynomial per machine per round to a master (`O(Nℓ)` total), the master runs the last `log N` rounds alone, and the proof is unchanged; Pianist's `O(1)` per machine (2.1 KB) and HyperPianist's `O(log T)` per machine are bought with homomorphic commitments (KZG, Dory) that Tessera cannot use; Cirrus measures 15 KB sent and 25 KB received per worker over 71 rounds at `T = 2^22`. The communication deVirgo could not avoid was its FRI commitment (`O(2^ℓ)` total, "terabytes" per [PL24]); RFC-0011's per-segment column chains give each GPU its own commitment and remove it. What the corpus does not settle is latency: about 10^4 rounds per proof means 10^4 synchronizations, and no primary small-message all-reduce latency on 8×H100 was found; a 20–30 µs floor gives 0.2–0.3 s per proof (E0), 2–3% of a 16K-token batch's native time and inside the 15% glue, but a larger share for small units. Multi-node proving (NG in RFC-0018) would multiply this floor by the fabric's latency.

6. *The per-pass cost is bandwidth, not multiplications.* zkSpeed's profile of HyperPlonk's sumcheck (tables rewritten every round, intensity 0.01–0.22 modmul/byte, 512 GB/s saturated) says a streamed round over 2.2×10^12 leaves is paid in bytes. Regenerating leaves into registers (RFC-0018 fuses the first streamed round with the tree build) is the right shape; `p_s = 7 ps` should be re-derived from bytes moved per leaf per round before v0.5 rather than from `Fp2` operation counts.

**Contested or unmeasured.**

- The cost model gives `c = 9` streamed rounds at 16K (`⌈log2(346)⌉`), where RFC-0018 says "about 8"; the formula and the 0.2 TB figure are RFC-0018's own, so the text understates its own formula by one pass (E0). RFC-0018's estimate "6–8 ps per score per pass" and the model's 7 ps are assumptions until v0.5.
- OQ-0018-1 (streaming against smaller units) now has numbers: 2K-token units cut `μ` by 10.7% at 16K (E0) but every unit adds `G + 1 ≈ 17` combined rows of 64 MB, so eight units add about 7.7 GB of proof and break the 4 GiB gate (SC-3); the tile fold of finding 4 dominates this option.
- Freeing HBM moves `c` slowly: evicting the 405 GB of weights to host during leaf stages raises free HBM to about 0.6 TB and saves 1–2 passes (−7.1% of `μ` at 16K, E0) for about 0.4 s of PCIe reload per unit; a host-RAM tier of 1.2 TB saves 2 passes (−10.7%, E0) at 128 GB/s per GPU; GH200-class memory would save about 4 passes but is not the RFC-0020 comparison hardware.
- Where sources disagree: Pianist's own text gives 2.1 KB and 2,336 bytes per machine, and 2.2 KB against 2,816 bytes of proof, between its introduction and results; both are "constant", so nothing here depends on which.

**Against the RFCs.** RFC-0018 Alternative 1 (NVMe) confirmed with numbers; Alternative 2 (per-tile challenges) contradicted in its reasoning by [PP25], which reaches a single point; Alternative 3 (multi-variable rounds) untouched, but shown not to cover the time-space provers; the partitioning design confirmed by [XZCZ+22]; OQ-0018-1 quantified and likely moot. RFC-0011 Alternative 2's rejection of folding commitments for the advice (BaseFold, WHIR) stands: nothing here gives a one-pass opening for them.

## Candidate ideas

- **Tile-folded leaf levels (Hobbit).** Fold the `N/B` resident tiles of a streamed level into one instance in a single regeneration pass, prove it in memory, reduce the tile evaluations with a `log(N/B)`-variable sumcheck. Term: `stream`. Gain: streaming term divided by `c`, about −28% of `μ` at 16K and −42% at 128K (E0). First killer: the per-leaf cost of the degree-3 cross terms per fold exceeds `c · p_s`, or the Libra masking of a folded instance breaks the simulator argument of RFC-0013.
- **Multi-round streamed passes (time-space prover).** Keep RFC-0018's protocol and answer `w` rounds per regeneration pass from a table of partial sums over the next `w` variables, as [BCFFMMZ25] does. Term: `stream`. Gain: `c → ⌈c/w⌉` passes; halving the per-pass cost is −16% at 16K and −23% at 128K (E0). First killer: for a degree-3 summand the table has `(d+1) · 2^{(w−1)(d−1)}`-fold work per leaf, so `w ≥ 3` costs more compute than it saves passes; must be read from the full text before an experiment.
- **Small-value first rounds.** Apply the small-value prover of [DDBDT26] to the base-field leaves `c_q − x_qi` and lookup keys during the streamed rounds, replacing `Fp2` multiplications by integer operations. Term: `p_s`, `c_π`. Gain: Jolt reports > 10× on Spartan; for Tessera the first one or two rounds of each level, so perhaps −20–40% of `p_s` (E0 guess, no source). First killer: after the first `Fp2` challenge is bound the values are no longer small, so the gain is confined to one round per level.
- **Evict weights during leaf stages.** Spill the 405 GB of weights to host RAM while tree levels are proven, tripling free HBM. Term: `stream`. Gain: 1–2 fewer passes, −7.1% of `μ` at 16K and −7.6% at 128K (E0) for about 0.4 s of reload per unit. First killer: P2 regenerates attention tiles from checkpoints and the Q, K projections, which need the weights of the layer being streamed.
- **Count and fuse P3 into P2 or P4.** Make the v0.3 pass counter report GEMM-shaped against elementwise regenerations per stage and fuse every stage whose point is fixed before a streamed pass. Term: `regen`. Gain: `n_regen` 4 → 3 is −11% at 1K, 4 → 2 is −22% (E0). First killer: the stage schedule of RFC-0010 makes the last stage's regeneration GEMM-shaped (O-projection folds) and unfusable with P4.
- **Host-RAM advice cache for small units.** For units whose advice fits the 1 TiB host budget (about 0.23 TB per 1K-token unit for linear layers), keep the advice from P1 and read it back over PCIe for P4 instead of regenerating. Term: `regen`. Gain: up to one full-forward equivalent at short context (−11% at 1K, E0) if the whole unit's advice fits. First killer: 8 × 128 GB/s against 1.19×10^15 op/s makes the read about as slow as the regeneration for linear layers (0.2–0.5 s against 0.7 s per 1K-token unit, E0), and the attention advice does not fit.
- **Re-derive `p_s` from bytes.** Budget the streamed round in bytes per leaf per pass (regenerated in registers, folded state written once) following zkSpeed's finding that sumcheck is bandwidth-bound. Term: `stream`. Gain: none directly; corrects the model before v0.5. First killer: none; a measurement task, not a design change.
- Findings without a seed: Pianist, HyperPianist, Hekaton, Cirrus and Sisu rest on homomorphic or pairing-based commitments or on recursion (NG-3); Gemini and [Nye25] address regimes (logarithmic or `√T` memory) Tessera does not need; Ligetron confirms the design rather than improving it; GH200 memory tiers are outside the RFC-0020 comparison hardware (shelve until the comparison is re-stated); SP1 Hypercube and Expander give no distribution details.
