## Method

Searched on 2026-10-16: ePrint and arXiv by the agenda's seeds (ePrint 2020/654, 2025/2046, 2026/680, 2025/870; arXiv 2609.05870, 2605.07595) and by keyword ("proximity gaps", "correlated agreement", "mutual correlated agreement", "Johnson bound", "interleaved codes", "Ligero list decoding", "optimal proximity gaps", "STIR WHIR prover", "Ligerito"), ECCC by report number, the ArkLib repository (Lean formalization of several of these bounds), and the zkSecurity and zkpunk write-ups as secondary leads. ePrint PDFs sit behind a bot challenge that neither the fetch tool, `curl` nor the headless browser passes; where a paper has an ECCC, arXiv, CiC or LIPIcs copy it was read in full from there ([BCIKS20] ECCC TR20-083, [BCHKS25] ECCC TR25-169, [GG25] ECCC TR25-166 rev. 1, [DG24] CiC 1(4), [BCGM26] CCC 2026 LIPIcs, [GGSW26] RANDOM 2026 LIPIcs, [Jer26] arXiv HTML, [Kam26] arXiv HTML); the rest were read from their ePrint landing pages (title, abstract, dates) and, for [DKT26], from the Lean statements and docstrings in [ArkLib26] that formalize it. Two sources are marked `to read` where the landing page does not carry the number the survey needs ([FS26], [Hab25]). Secondary write-ups ([Moh25], [zkpunk25]) were used only to locate primary sources and for the industry impact estimate they report, labeled as such. All numbers for Tessera are E0, computed with [tools/proximity.py](../tools/proximity.py) (unique decoding, rate, mask and grinding sweeps) and with two scratch evaluations of [BCHKS25] Theorem 1.5 and of the [ArkLib26] closed Johnson bound, whose formulas appear in the findings so that an experiment can reproduce them in the tool.

Excluded: the pre-2023 Ligero/Brakedown unique-decoding analyses except as the baseline; rank-metric (Gabidulin) proximity gaps; binary-field results except the one negative result that bounds what the Goldilocks setting can hope for.

## Findings

Codes: RS over `F_q`, length `n`, degree bound `k'` (`ρ = k'/n`), proximity radius `γ` (RFC-0014's `δ`), unique-decoding radius `(1 − ρ)/2`, Johnson radius `1 − √ρ`, capacity `1 − ρ`. "Lines" is the test `u_0 + z u_1`; "affine space" is Tessera's row combination with independent uniform coefficients; "eq generator" is the coefficients `eq(ξ*, row)` RFC-0011 step 3 actually uses. Tessera's numbers: `n = 2^23`, `k' = 2^22 + 159`, `ρ' = 0.500019`, `q' = |Fp2| ≈ 2^128`, 5.8×10^5 rows.

## Synthesis

Settled.

1. **Unique decoding stays provable and gets slightly better.** [BCHKS25] Corollary 1.4 lowers the false-witness count from `n` to `γn + 1` for `γ < δ/2 − 3/(δn)`. For Tessera's test the count that matters is the tensor-style one: RFC-0011 draws `ξ_row = eq(ξ*, row)`, so the applicable error is `ϑ · ε/q'` with `ϑ = 20` ([DP24log], [DG24]), that is `2^-100.7` today and `2^-102.7` with Corollary 1.4, not the `n/|Fp2| = 2^-105` RFC-0014 'Soundness budget' writes for the affine-space test. Both pass the live and non-interactive targets (the non-interactive one with its 20 grinding bits); the budget row should name the tensor bound (an issue, not an RFC). `τ_A = 155` and `τ_W = 97` do not move under unique decoding: the field term is not what binds them.
2. **The Johnson regime is now conjecture-free with an error linear in `n/q`**, exactly the condition IDEA-0001 recorded for reopening. [BCHKS25] Theorem 1.5 (correlated agreement, `a = O_ρ(n/η^5)`) and [DKT26] as formalized in [ArkLib26] (mutual correlated agreement, `E_0 < (8/3) n t^3/ρ`, affine spaces at cost `q'/(q'−1)` in every dimension) both give, at the live-64 set, `τ_A = 130` with a field term of 66.7 bits (BCHKS, `η = 0.002`) or 80.2 bits (ArkLib, `η = 0.002`), and `τ_A = 129` at 77.2 bits (ArkLib, `η = 0.001`); `τ_W` falls from 97 to 68. The non-interactive set falls from 193 to 162 (ArkLib, 80.2 bits before grinding) or 171 (BCHKS, 81.0 bits). In proof size: 0.72 GB → 0.60 GB of opened columns (−6% of the 2 GB proof), 0.90 GB → 0.75 GB non-interactive, and the verifier hashes proportionally less. This contradicts RFC-0014 Alternatives Considered 3 as a matter of fact (its reason, "provable bounds in that regime carry field-size terms that erode the margin", was true of [BCIKS20] and is false of [BCHKS25] and [DKT26]) while confirming its rule (no conjectures). The remaining gaps before an RFC: (a) the Ligero binding argument beyond unique decoding needs the mutual form and a list-based extractor, which [DKT26]'s Johnson MCA supplies for the affine-space generator; (b) the extension-field setting (rows over `Fp`, coefficients over `Fp2`) needs the one-line lemma that an `Fp2` codeword agreeing with an `Fp`-valued row on `≥ k'` points of `D ⊆ Fp` has coefficients in `Fp`; (c) the generator: every Johnson-regime bound above is for lines or affine spaces, and the only one covering the eq generator ([BCGM26] Theorem 5) carries `n^2` and lands near `2^-54`. Switching RFC-0011 step 3 to independent uniform `Fp2` coefficients (a transcript-seeded stream; the verifier already evaluates `5.8×10^5` coefficients per group for `c_g`) removes the `ϑ` factor today and makes the Johnson-regime bounds apply verbatim.
3. **Row-count terms.** For independent uniform coefficients there is none beyond `q'/(q'−1)` ([BCIKS20] Thm 1.7, [GG25] Lemma 2.11/2.13, [ArkLib26] `LineToAffine`). For the eq generator: `ϑ = log2(rows)` within unique decoding ([DG24]); unknown in the Johnson regime. For a single-challenge curve `Σ z^i u_i`: a factor `M` = rows ([BCIKS20] Thm 1.6, [BCHKS25] Thm 4.1), fatal at `5.8×10^5` rows. For interleaved list decoding: the exponent grows linearly in the number of rows ([Jer26] Cor. 8.2).
4. **Capacity is closed for Tessera's codes.** The up-to-capacity conjectures for correlated agreement ([BCIKS20]), MCA (WHIR) and list decodability (DEEP-FRI) are false in general ([CS25], [DG25]) and false specifically for RS codes over smooth multiplicative subgroups of prime fields in a window `Θ(1/log n)` below capacity ([KKH26], [Kam26]). Beyond the list-decoding radius the error is at least `1/(2n)` for every field size ([BCHKS25] Thm 1.9), and improved gaps beyond Johnson would imply improved RS list-decoding bounds, which do not exist for arbitrary evaluation sets. The `τ_A = 66` of the capacity conjecture is therefore unreachable with Tessera's code; it is reachable only by changing the code to folded RS or multiplicity codes ([GG25], [LXYYZ26]), whose field-size requirement is linear in `n` but whose encoder, alphabet and folding prover RFC-0011 and RFC-0012 would have to adopt (tier A).
5. **Between Johnson and capacity**, the usable territory is thin: [DKT26]'s first-derivative threshold `a_1(ρ) < √ρ` with error `O(n^2/(q η_1^4))` (needs `η_1 ≥ 0.044` for 64 bits over `Fp2` at `n = 2^23`; the gain depends on `a_1(1/2)`, unread); [Jo26b]'s `O(K^6/q)` is vacuous at `K = 2^22`.

Contested or open. Whether an explicit NTT-friendly evaluation set can have gaps past Johnson (asked open by [GGSW26]; the negative results of [BCHKS25] are in characteristic 2 and the positive capacity results need random or folded codes). The exponent in [Jer26]. What [ABF26]'s July revision estimates as concrete attacks (unread). What [FS26] achieves for the many-row case.

Question 3. Rate: under unique decoding, rate 1/4 gives `τ_A = 95` and 0.44 GB of opened columns, rate 1/8 gives 78 and 0.36 GB, at 2× and 4× the encoded and hashed volume (`c_c`), E0 from the tool; under the Johnson bounds the same ratio applies on top (`τ_W` at rate 1/4: 97 → 68). Mask: `P_A` is irrelevant to `τ` below about `10^4` coefficients (`τ_A = 155` up to `P = 10^4`, 159 at `10^5`), E0. Grinding: in live mode the proximity test is one-shot and grinding is not admissible; counted anyway, 20 bits would give `τ_A = 107` (E0), which is why the non-interactive set needs 193 at 80 one-shot bits. Query reductions: [ACFY24s] reports 1.25–2.46× smaller arguments than FRI at similar prover time, [ACFY24] 63 KiB and 360 µs verification for a degree-`2^22` opening at 100 bits (prover 1.2 s commit-and-open), [NA25] 255 KiB for `2^24` coefficients. None of these applies to the streamed advice (RFC-0011 Alternatives Considered: folding needs the vector resident), but the `G + 1 ≤ 17` combined rows of `2^22` `Fp2` values are resident and are 1.14 GB of the proof, more than the opened columns; committing them with a WHIR- or STIR-style IOPP and proving the `17 (τ_A + 1)` linear functionals the verifier needs (`⟨u_g, eq(point)⟩` and `enc(u_g)(x_j)`, each a multilinear evaluation) would replace 1.14 GB by about a megabyte and remove the verifier's 17 coset NTTs of size `2^23`, at a prover cost of 17 commitments to `2^22` elements, negligible against `2.4×10^12` committed elements.

## Candidate ideas

- **Johnson-regime `τ` under [BCHKS25]/[DKT26] with the affine-space generator**: `τ_A` 155 → 130 and `τ_W` 97 → 68 (live-64), 193 → 162 (ni-100); −0.12 GB of opened columns and the same fraction of verifier hashing; moves `τ`, `size`, `verify`. First killer: the Ligero extractor beyond unique decoding (needs MCA and the list argument) and the `Fp`/`Fp2` coefficient lemma; the experiment evaluates `johnsonExceptionCount` exactly from the Lean definitions in `tools/proximity.py` and reopens IDEA-0001.
- **Uniform `Fp2` proximity coefficients instead of `eq(ξ*, row)`**: a transcript-seeded stream; removes the `ϑ = 20` factor (`2^-100.7` → `2^-105` today) and is the prerequisite for the Johnson-regime bounds; moves `bits`, enables `τ`. First killer: RFC-0011 step 3 and the transcript schedule are tier A; a superseding RFC must show the verifier's cost of deriving `5.8×10^5` coefficients is within budget (it already evaluates as many for each `c_g`).
- **Commit the combined rows with a Johnson-regime IOPP ([ACFY24], [ACFY24s])**: 1.14 GB → about 1 MiB, 17 verifier NTTs removed; moves `size`, `verify`. First killer: the `17 (τ_A + 1)` functional claims must be batched into one opening without a second proof system in the verifier (SP-6: a WHIR verifier written from the specification into `tv-*`); zk of the combined rows is unchanged (they are sent in the clear today).
- **Ligerito-style delegation of the column checks ([NA25])**: 0.72 GB → polylog; moves `size`, `verify`. First killer: the recursion needs the opened columns resident on the prover (they are: `τ_A × 5.8×10^5` symbols) but its batched sumchecks must tolerate the vanishing masks and blinding rows of RFC-0013.
- **Rate 1/4 for the advice** (queue item 5): `τ_A` 95 under unique decoding, about 67 under Johnson; −0.28 to −0.45 GB; moves `τ`, `size` against `c_c`. First killer: the doubled encode-and-hash volume at `c_c = 30 ps` (RISK-8).
- **Polygero-style polynomial challenges with recursion ([FS26])**: soundness beyond unique decoding for a Ligero IOP, machine-checked; moves `τ`, `size`. First killer: the paper is unread and the plain curve form pays a factor `M` = rows.
- **Folded-RS or multiplicity-code advice commitment ([GG25], [LXYYZ26])**: the only route to `τ_A` near 66 (capacity regime, linear field size); moves `τ`, `size`. First killer: a new encoder and resident folding against RFC-0011's streaming requirement (RFC-0011 Alternatives Considered; tier A).
- No seed from [GCXK25] (row factor `t`), [Jo26b] (vacuous at `K = 2^22`), [Jer26] (no explicit exponent), [GGSW26], [YZ26] (random evaluation sets), [KTZ26], [DKTZ26], [GYXK26] (negative results), [Oka25] (unverified claim), [CS25], [DG25], [KKH26], [Kam26], [ABF26] (they close doors; their content is the security gate of the ideas above).
