## Findings

Tessera's quantities: `n = 2^23` columns, `m = 5.8×10^5` rows per 16K-token proof, 8-byte `Fp` symbols at rate 1/2 (16 codeword bytes per committed element), chained `column-step` inputs of `32 + 8B` bytes (`B = 32`: 288 B, 5 compressions per 32 symbols, 0.3125 compressions per committed element, [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-commit-cost-calibration/README.md)). H100 SXM int32 rate: 132 SMs × 64 lanes × 1.98 GHz = 1.67×10^13 op/s, the figure EXP-0002 uses ([VYTA25] for the 60% utilization). A BLAKE3 compression costs 792–960 int32 operations in EXP-0002's translation, 12.4–15 operations per byte.

## Synthesis

**Settled.**

1. **No public measurement answers Question 1.** Nobody has published BLAKE3 throughput on an H100, or on any CUDA GPU, for inputs of the column-chain shape; the only public GPU BLAKE3 for large inputs is a 2020 Vulkan proof of concept slower than one CPU core ([Chappjc20]). The 530 GB/s per GPU of [RFC-0004 'Motivation'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0004-hashing-and-transcript.md#motivation) therefore stays unmeasured (RISK-8 open), and the RFC-0020 v0.1 microbenchmark is still the first measurement. What exists are three indirect anchors, all consistent with the budget being reachable and none proving it: hashcat's SHA-256 at 15.1 G compressions/s on an H100 ([OpenBench25], `secondary`, short inputs); BLAKE3 mining rates that imply 490–550 int32 operations per compression on Ampere and Ada at full lane rate ([Lin23], [WhatToMine26], `secondary`/`claim`), below EXP-0002's 792–960 count, so either fused instructions cut the count by about 40% or the rates are overstated; and cuPQC's SHA family at 388–891 GB/s on a Blackwell workstation part ([NVcuPQC26], `claim`), which scales to 110–500 GB/s on an H100 and, at BLAKE3's 2–3× lower operation count per byte, to 0.2–1.1 TB/s (E0 scaling). The counted floor is the binding fact: at rate 1/2 the hash alone needs 39–48% of the H100's integer pipeline to make 530 GB/s (E0, [E0-hash]), and EXP-0002 already counts the encoder at 721–1,274 operations on top, which is why the whole `c_c` lands at 58–157 ps and not 30.
2. **Field-native hashes stay an order of magnitude away.** RFC-0004 Alternative 3 said "roughly an order of magnitude slower" for Poseidon2; the operation count ([GKS23], 20–25×) and the scaling of the only vendor Poseidon2 GPU figure ([NVcuPQCmt26], 13–35× after the BabyBear-to-Goldilocks penalty) both say more. Monolith narrows it to about 3× by its CPU ratio to Poseidon2 ([GKLRSW24], E0 from `secondary`), still above the budget, with no GPU figure and a lookup layer that circuits must pay. Tip5 is slower than Monolith; Skyscraper is for 256-bit primes. [Baseline §7](../baseline.md#7-already-rejected)'s reopening condition, "GPU measurements show a field-native hash within the 30 ps budget", is not met by any public number; the condition stands.
3. **Question 2: the volume lever is the chain overhead, nothing else.** The tree is 10^-5 of the hashing; arity ([P3mt26]) and caps ([Plonky2caps21]) move only the multiproof (< 0.2 MB) and the verifier's per-path hash count; column batching is what the chained column digest already is (Stwo and Plonky3 do the transposed version per row, [StwoBook26], [P3mt26]); packed symbols save nothing because an `Fp` element fills its 8 bytes (`p > 2^64 − 2^33`). The one lever is the `32 + 8B` input per step: today 5 compressions per 32 symbols (0.3125 per committed element), the floor at rate 1/2 is 0.25 (every block full of symbols). EXP-0002's `B = 28` gets 0.286 (−8.6%) by filling blocks under the `derive_key` input layout; feeding the chain value as BLAKE3's key (its initial chaining value, [BLAKE3ref20]) instead of as input bytes reaches the floor at `B = 32` exactly (256 B = 4 blocks), −20% of the hash, with the block buffer unchanged. Rate is the only other dial (`8/ρ` bytes per element) and SUR-0001 and EXP-0004 priced it: lower rate costs more hashing, higher rate costs `τ` and proof size.
4. **OQ-0004-1 as literally stated is unsound.** Hashing only the systematic symbols commits to the message, not the codeword: the proximity test becomes vacuous (every message is a codeword of the trivial code) and a combined row `u ≠ Σ r_i M_i` agrees with the honest one on all but one systematic position, so `τ` queries catch it with probability about `τ/k = 155/2^22` (E0). Reducing hashed volume this way is not available; the open question should be answered in the negative or restated.
5. **Question 3: recursion is gated by volume, not by the hash.** A recursive verifier must re-hash what the verifier hashes: 0.72 GB of opened advice columns and 0.3 GB of weight columns ([RFC-0019 'Performance plan'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0019-verifier.md#performance-plan-16k-tokens-16k-context-405b-live-64)). As BLAKE3 that is 1.6×10^7 compressions, each on the order of 10^4–10^5 cells in a Goldilocks AIR, so the recursion circuit would be as large as the proof it compresses (E0). With Poseidon2-Goldilocks the same volume is 1.6×10^7 permutations at about 600 cells each in a dedicated component ([ChenW25]'s layout), 10^10 cells, 3% of the proof: feasible, but only after the prover has hashed 39 TB per 16K-token proof (`m · n · 8` B) with a hash 20–25× dearer, which adds 80–200% to `c_c` and 17–50% to `μ` (E0, cost model shares). The route that pays is the one SUR-0001 seeded: shrink the hashed volume at the verifier (`τ_A` 155 → 130 under RFC-0021, committed combined rows, Ligerito-style delegation, wider rows) and only then ask whether the residual column hashing deserves a dual-hash design. [CKMW26] adds that a cheaper node compression with pre-hashed leaves is sound, which would matter for a recursion circuit's tree hashing but not for the column chains.

**Confirmed or contradicted.** RFC-0004 Alternative 3 (Poseidon2) confirmed with a larger margin; Alternative 4 (BLAKE2s: 10 rounds against 7) confirmed, Stwo publishes no GPU figure; Alternative 6 (one hasher per column) confirmed, and the keyed-chain variant keeps its 32-byte state. RFC-0004 Alternative 1's instruction ratio (SHA-256 "about 40" against BLAKE3 "about 15" operations per byte) is overstated by hashcat's evidence (SHA-256 compressions run at 15–22 G/s on H100 and 4090, which is nearer 16–27 op/B); the decision stands, the ratio should read 1.5–2×. RFC-0011's `B = 32` is optimal under keyed chaining and 8.6% off optimal under today's layout (EXP-0002). RFC-0020's `c_c` benchmark must report the hash and the encoder separately and record int32-pipe utilization, since the hash alone can take 50–100% of the budget by count. OQ-0004-1 is answered in the negative (item 4). NG-3 stands on cost grounds (item 5).

**Contested or open.** The 40% gap between mining-implied and counted operations per BLAKE3 compression (fused instructions against overstated rates). Whether Blackwell's 128 cores per SM all issue INT32 (sets the 1.8× against 3.6× scaling of every cuPQC number). Monolith's absolute Goldilocks numbers and GPU behaviour of its Bars layer (tables are images in every source read). The ARX-compression cost in a Goldilocks AIR (no primary source read; needed only if recursion enters scope).

## Candidate ideas

- **Chain value as the BLAKE3 key (free-start chaining)**: `column-step` becomes `keyed_hash(key = d_j[i], symbols)` so the 32-byte chain enters as the chaining value, not as input bytes; `B = 32` then fills 4 blocks exactly and compressions per committed element fall 0.3125 → 0.25 (−20% of the hash, −3.8% to −5.1% of `c_c` at EXP-0002's hash share); `Δμ` −0.8% to −1.1% (corrected) or −1.1% to −1.7% (`counted`) at 1K and 16K (E0, cost model); block buffer unchanged; supersedes `B = 28` (ledger item 18), which reaches 0.286. Moves `c_c`. First killer: the security argument must be restated as free-start collision resistance of BLAKE3's compression with distinct flags, the assumption BLAKE3's own chunk chaining makes; and `column-init` must carry the domain separation `derive_key` provided per step (tier-A change to RFC-0004 and RFC-0011; the verifier's own BLAKE3 must expose keyed mode, which `tv-hash` implements from the specification anyway).
- **Hash on the integer pipe, encoder multiplications on the FMA pipe (input to IDEA-0003)**: BLAKE3 is additions, xors and funnel shifts; the NTT's wide products are IMAD, which Hopper issues on the FMA-heavy pipe; a fused encode-and-hash kernel that balances the two could hide 19–26% of `c_c` behind the other pipe, `Δμ` −3.9% to −5.5% (corrected) or −5.7% to −8.4% (`counted`) if the hash becomes free (E0 upper bound, cost model). Moves `c_c`. First killer: needs an H100 and SASS-level pipe accounting (shelved like IDEA-0003); the mining-implied 490–550 operations per compression suggest the hash already uses fused instructions the count does not credit, which would shrink the hideable share.
- **Rate 2/3 advice encoding (the mirror of EXP-0004)**: `n = 3·2^21` (Goldilocks has 3-smooth roots: `3 | p − 1`), 12 hashed bytes per element and a 25% smaller coset NTT, `c_c` −15% to −20% (E0, rough), `Δμ` −3.1% to −4.2% at 16K (cost model); against it `b(ρ')` 0.415 → 0.263 bits per query under unique decoding, `τ_A` 155 → about 245, opened columns 0.72 → 1.14 GB and the verifier's hashing +58%. Moves `c_c` against `τ`, `size`, `verify`. First killer: the scoreboard's proof size and verification time already trail the field by two to three orders (agenda priority line), so a trade that enlarges both is out of direction unless RFC-0021's Johnson-regime queries and committed combined rows have first made the proof small; also a mixed-radix coset NTT kernel.
- **Separate hash and encoder in the RFC-0020 `c_c` benchmark, with a keyed-chain variant**: a measurement seed rather than a design idea; record compressions per second and int32-pipe utilization for the chain kernel alone at `B = 32` under both layouts, and the SHA-256 hashcat figure (15.1 G/s) as the sanity bound. Moves the uncertainty of `c_c`. First killer: none; it is the gate's own protocol, so it goes to the RFC-0020 owner as an issue.
- **Answer OQ-0004-1 in the negative**: hashing systematic symbols only is unsound (Synthesis item 4); a one-paragraph resolution in RFC-0004's open questions. Moves nothing; closes a question. First killer: none.
- No seed from [GKS23], [GKLRSW24], [SLSTA23], [BGKKRSS25] as commitment hashes (20–25×, about 3×, more than Monolith, and wrong field respectively, against a budget the current hash already strains); from [NVcuPQCmt26], [Zeknox26], [RiscZeroRel24], [SP1sec26], [Icicle26] (no Goldilocks BLAKE3 figures to adopt; context for the landscape); from [P3mt26], [Plonky2caps21], [StwoBook26] (arity, caps and batching are 10^-5 of the prover's hashing); from [CKMW26] (a confirmation; Tessera's tree is in the safe regime); from [ChenW25] (recursion is gated by hashed volume, which SUR-0001's seeds address first); from [Chappjc20], [Nguyen19], [HashcatF23], [OpenBench25], [Lin23], [WhatToMine26], [Alephium26] (anchors for the E2 measurement, not mechanisms).

## Staleness triggers

- The RFC-0020 v0.1 `c_c` measurement on an H100 (E2): it replaces every anchor in this survey and decides RISK-8.
- A published CUDA BLAKE3 or BLAKE2s kernel benchmark on large inputs (any vendor), or cuPQC adding BLAKE3 or a Goldilocks Poseidon2 with absolute numbers.
- ICICLE, sppark, Plonky3, RISC Zero or Stwo publishing a GPU hash rate; Stwo gaining a public GPU backend.
- The Blackwell architecture whitepaper settling whether all 128 cores per SM issue INT32 (fixes the cuPQC scaling).
- Monolith or a successor measured on a GPU over Goldilocks; new cryptanalysis of Monolith, Tip5 or Poseidon2 Goldilocks instances.
- Recursion (NG-3) entering scope through an RFC, which would make [ChenW25]-style in-circuit costs load-bearing.
