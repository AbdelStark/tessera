## Synthesis

**Question 1: nobody binds a KV cache across separately verified proofs.** Every system read either proves the whole sequence in one proof (zkLLM, zkGPT, VerInf, DeepProve's single pass "because KV caching is poison"), splits by layers with boundary activations committed and attention never split (DeepProve chunks, Seal-then-Sample, NanoZK's hash chain), or amortizes within one proof (zkAgent). zkComposer is the single exception: it commits K/V per token partition and has later partitions read them as advice. But its sub-proofs are verified together, rest on homomorphic Hyrax, and have no theorem for the sequence case. RISC Zero and OpenVM chain state across segment proofs by a pre/post digest at the statement level. That is the only cross-proof state-linking pattern in production, and two shipped linking defects in 2026 (Jolt's trusted advice, May; OpenVM's memory root, October) show where it breaks. This confirms RFC-0057's reading: without a binding, earlier K/V are unproven prover input. VerInf's 'inherited context' lines say the same.

**Question 2: the template exists and is Tessera's own.** Reopening one hash-based commitment in many proofs with fresh challenges needs three things. RFC-0012 and VerInf §6.4 state them independently. (a) Binding: the root is fixed and absorbed before every challenge that depends on it. (b) Proximity: tested by each opening's own queries, as RFC-0064 'What the link does not need' argues. (c) Hiding: the union of opened columns stays within the pad, and each opening uses fresh blinding rows. Longfellow's MAC link is the deployed precedent for linking separate Ligero commitments; its formal premise is the challenge drawn "at the two roots". Homomorphic linking (LegoSNARK) and elliptic-curve multiset hashes (SP1) fail the post-quantum requirement. Memory-checking continuations (Twist, Lasso offline checking) are heavier than needed: attention reads every cached key densely, so the link is evaluation claims on K̃ and Ṽ at sumcheck points, a plain PCS opening, not a read/write argument. Two shapes follow.

- **Persistent context commitment (cross-session).** Chunk `i` commits its K/V in a separate commitment `C_i`, stored like RFC-0012's codeword store. Advice codewords exist only during the witness pass (RFC-0012 'Column answers'), so `C_i` must be a separate commitment. Every later chunk opens `C_i` under RFC-0012's rules, and a prefix commitment shared by many requests is RFC-0012 with a context-class budget (ledger item 54). By the E0 fit this is cheap in bytes. The hiding pad needs only about 4.8K columns for 32 chunks.
- **Shared-session chunks (one statement, many messages).** All chunks are committed first. One seed is drawn from all roots, as DeepProve's chunk transcripts and Longfellow's MAC do. Then each chunk is proven separately. No cross-session budget is needed, but proving cannot start until the request ends, and the witness passes repeat.

Both shapes are new tier-A statements (RFC-0057 Alternative 3).

**Question 3: chunking does not shrink a request's proof; it only splits it.** At 128K the opened columns sum to about 45 GB over all chunks, 90% from scores (E0). To keep each proof under 4 GiB, chunks must be 3.3K–4.1K tokens at the end of a 128K request. That is 32–40 proofs, each paying 0.35 GB of weight openings, plus 4.8% of a weight epoch's 670 proofs. RFC-0057's "16K-token chunk at 128K" (5–10 GB) is out of the gate by itself. Only two levers reduce the total. One is fewer committed scores: Shelat's GKR point that intermediate layers need no commitment, against RFC-0009's need for committed lookup keys, and VerInf's windowed-attention reduction, which is a model change. The other is fewer bytes per committed row: Ligerito-style recursion, SUR-0005's level 2. Attestable's context-independent proof size is the bar, unexplained (`claim`). RFC-0011's choice to open plain Ligero columns is what makes the gate bind at long context, so the 128K row of 08 §3 needs a Q3 lever as well as #470.

## Candidate ideas

- **Context commitment opened under RFC-0012 rules.** Mechanism: K/V of each chunk in its own stored, hiding commitment, opened by later chunks at their attention sumcheck points with fresh blinding rows, and roots chained in the statement. Term: `bits`, `zk`, `size`. Gain: binds cached K/V for about 0.1–0.3 GB per later proof (E0). First killer: the statement and anchor chain (RFC-0007/0015), i.e. whether a verifier that sees chunks separately can be prevented from accepting a chunk whose predecessor it never verified (the Jolt/OpenVM defect class).
- **Shared-seed chunked session.** Mechanism: commit all chunks, draw one seed from all roots, prove chunks as separate messages, link K/V by equality claims inside the session. Term: `size` per message, `stream`. Gain: each message under the codec limit with no cross-session budget. First killer: the commit pass must regenerate every chunk's witness before any proving (an extra pass of a 128K request), and the total bytes the verifier reads are unchanged.
- **Prefix commitment for shared prompts (ledger item 54).** Mechanism: one context commitment for a prefix, opened by every sharer's proof, keyed by a chained digest like vLLM's block hash. Term: `A`, `C_lin` per request. Gain: per request `P/S` of linear work and `(P/S)²` of scores (E0, [E0-this]). First killer: the hiding budget of the prefix commitment caps sharers at about `P_C/τ` per refresh, and cross-request linkability of a shared root may itself leak (G-5).
- **Ligerito-style second level for advice columns.** Mechanism: recursive commitment of the combined-row checks instead of sending `τ_A` columns per row. Term: `size`. Gain: the only lever that cuts the 45 GB total; SUR-0005 estimates about 1.0 GB at 16K (E0). First killer: RFC-0011's streaming constraint (folding needs residency).
- **Score-free attention rows.** Mechanism: prove `softmax·V` products by sumcheck with scores as uncommitted intermediate layers, committing only lookup keys that RFC-0009 needs. Term: `A`, `size`. Gain: up to 90% of long-context columns if scores need no commitment (E0 upper bound). First killer: SP-3 uniqueness and the exp lookup keys are the scores themselves.

No seed for SP1's multiset hash (not post-quantum), for LegoSNARK (homomorphic), for ARC/WARP and zkRNN (need recursion, NG-3), or for NanoZK hash chaining (in-circuit hashing of 10^10 values).
