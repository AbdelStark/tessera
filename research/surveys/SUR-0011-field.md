## Findings

Tessera's quantities for every translation below: committed elements are 8-byte `Fp` symbols at rate 1/2 (16 codeword bytes per element); `c_ℓ` is 18.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents per lookup ([E0-this]); the H100 SXM int32 rate is 1.67×10^13 lane-operations/s at 60% utilization ([VYTA25]); the quantities that must be true integers are RFC-0005's worst-case column: INT20 residual, norm sum `< 2^54` and key `< 2^37`, RoPE key `< 2^44`, matmul accumulators `< 2^29` (`servable`) or `< 2^58`–`2^60` (`minimal`), attention output `< 2^39`, softmax sum `< 2^33` and reciprocal `floor(2^32/s_q)`. Goldilocks' centered range is ±2^63; a 31-bit field's is ±2^30; Mersenne-61's is ±2^60.

## Synthesis

**Settled.**

1. **No measurement answers the question; the counts do, at E0, and they favor Goldilocks.** Nobody has published the multiplication rate of Goldilocks, M31, BabyBear or KoalaBear on an H100, nor a GPU NTT or circle FFT over a 31-bit field, nor a binary-tower multiplication rate on a GPU; the one same-GPU cross-field prover figure compares M31 with BN254 ([Polyhedra24]) and the one same-code cross-field comparison is on a CPU ([Syxton25]); the 2026 SoK names the absence ([Ska26b]). Counting lane operations from the production code ([P3field26], [StwoFields26]) gives the shape the source guessed: a 31-bit base multiplication is 2.6× cheaper than Goldilocks' and a 31-bit NTT butterfly 2.2× cheaper, the symbol bytes halve, so `c_c` halves; but the 128-bit challenge arithmetic that `c_ℓ` and `c_π` are made of costs 1.7–2.1× more in QM31 or BabyBear⁴ than in Goldilocks `Fp2`, because a degree-4 extension needs 9–16 base products and 16–25 base additions where a degree-2 extension needs 3–4 and 5, and 31-bit additions are nearly half the cost of 31-bit multiplications. With `c_ℓ` and `c_π` at 51–53% of `μ(16K)` under the `counted` profile and `c_c` at 30% ([baseline §2](../baseline.md#2-the-cost-model)), the extension term wins: `μ` rises 24% at 16K at the centre of the counts, 7% in the optimistic case and 41% in the pessimistic one, and falls only if the extension cost were even (−13%), which no count supports ([E0-field]). The source's "about 20% either way" is the right width and the wrong symmetry.

2. **The rescale-count effect is a uniqueness effect, not a count effect.** RFC-0005's worst-case column has seven quantities above 2^30 (RoPE keys 2^44, norm sums 2^54 and keys 2^37, attention output 2^39, softmax sums 2^33 and reciprocals 2^32, the `minimal` projection chains 2^58–2^60). In a 31-bit field none of them is a field element, and the sumcheck that computes an accumulator proves it only modulo `p`, so a 2^39 integer has 2^8 passing residues: to keep SP-3 the prover must split the *operands* into limbs, run one sumcheck per limb, commit each partial accumulator and range-check it, then recombine with a carry. That is the mechanism behind the source's "roughly twice the linear-layer rescales" and "harder RMSNorm" ([Source26] §10): the cost is at least 2 commitments, 3 lookups and 2 sumchecks per such element against Goldilocks' 1 or 2 ([RFC-0009 'RequantSplit'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0009-gadgets.md#requantsplit)), on 66,560 of 137,216 rescaled elements per layer per token under `servable` and on all of them under `minimal`. [RFC-0002 Alternatives 1 and 2](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0002-field-arithmetic.md#alternatives-considered) are confirmed, with the reason sharpened from "overflow sooner" to "no unique true integer above 2^30"; [RFC-0006 'Range analysis'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0006-compiler-ir.md#range-analysis) is what makes the Goldilocks case sound, and would have to re-derive limb ranges in any smaller field.

3. **Binary fields are out for the base field and even on arithmetic.** Tessera's statement is integer-valued with carries, orderings and shifts; characteristic 2 turns every range check into a bit decomposition and removes "let the field absorb chains" ([Irreducible24] row). The hardware argument has also moved: since CUDA 13.3 a GF(2^128) product costs 6 `clmad` and runs at about the rate a Goldilocks `Fp2` product reaches by count ([NVclmad26], E0 scaling of a vendor figure), so binary towers no longer promise cheaper 128-bit arithmetic on GPUs; their remaining case is per-bit commitment of small values, which is NG-5 and [SUR-0005](SUR-0005-commitments.md)'s ring-switching row ([DP26]). The ASIC efficiency claim (5× over M31, [Irreducible24]) is for custom silicon the roadmap does not have.

4. **KoalaBear and BabyBear are M31 with Montgomery form and a standard NTT.** The same limb and extension arithmetic applies (BabyBear⁴ 153–173 against `Fp2`'s 83), the multiplication is 1–2 operations dearer than M31's, and the standard two-adic NTT (24 or 27) removes the circle-FFT question; KoalaBear's reason to exist is the hash S-box degree ([Syxton25]), which Tessera does not use. No separate conclusion.

5. **Where 64 bits cost Tessera.** `c_c`: 16 codeword bytes per committed element against 8, and a 28-operation butterfly against 13; by [EXP-0002](../experiments/EXP-0002-commit-cost-calibration/README.md)'s split (hash 19–26%, encoder the rest) a 31-bit field would roughly halve `c_c`, worth −14% to −17% of `μ` if nothing else changed ([E0-field], the upper bound). The levers that recover part of that without leaving Goldilocks are already in the corpus: committing small values at fewer bits ([IDEA-0005](../ideas/IDEA-0005-base-field-leaf-small-value.md), NG-5), lazy reduction in dot-product-shaped kernels ([SUR-0007](SUR-0007-sumcheck.md), [DDBDT26] §3), and the keyed-chain hashing of [SUR-0006](SUR-0006-hash.md). The MamaBear result ([ZGLCZ26]) shows what headroom bits buy on a CPU (15–45×, with an unstated baseline); Goldilocks has 2^-32 relative headroom in 64 bits, so on a GPU the equivalent is 128-bit accumulation, not a smaller prime.

**Contested or open.** The utilization each field's kernels reach (addition-heavy QM31 at [VYTA25]'s 40% against multiplication-heavy Goldilocks at 60% would widen the gap; `IMAD.WIDE` issue cost would narrow the base-multiplication ratio). The circle FFT's constant against a two-adic NTT on a GPU (1.4× faster per [HLP24]'s preliminary CPU figure, slower per [Syxton25]'s CPU measurement, unmeasured on any GPU). The limb-split rescale gadget's true cost in a 31-bit field (lower-bounded here, never designed). Whether tensor-core arithmetic ([LZG+26]) can take a 64-bit field at all.

**Against the RFCs.** RFC-0002 Alternatives 1, 2 and 5 confirmed with counts (Alternative 5's "2.25× or more" for cubic or quartic challenge extensions over Goldilocks is consistent with the 9-against-3 product count); 'Representation and reduction' confirmed as the cheaper form (Montgomery buys nothing on a special-form prime, [P3field26] uses the same split). RFC-0005's worst-case column is the artifact that decides the field question and should be cited by RFC-0002's Alternatives when they are next revised. [Baseline §7](../baseline.md#7-already-rejected)'s reopening condition ("measured rescale counts and GPU arithmetic show a net win at 4K and 32K") is not met by any public number and is unlikely to be met by a measurement, since the counts put the win at the extension level the measurement would have to overturn. The `field` row of [baseline §6](../baseline.md#6-levers-which-technique-class-moves-which-term) stays "every unit cost".

## Candidate ideas

- **Cross-field microbenchmark inside the RFC-0020 v0.1 gate (measurement seed).** Add to the `c_ℓ` and `c_c` protocol one kernel each for Goldilocks, M31 and KoalaBear base multiplication, their 128-bit extension multiplications (`Fp2`, QM31, KoalaBear⁴) and a 2^23 butterfly pass, reporting int32-pipe utilization; it fixes the 1.7–2.1 ratio this survey counts and closes [Source26] §9 problem 5 with E2 instead of E0. Terms: the uncertainty of every unit cost. Gain: resolves a −14% to +44% envelope. First killer: none; it goes to the RFC-0020 owner as an issue, and a CPU version of the same kernels is an E1 anyone can run here.
- **Mersenne-61 with circle codes.** `p = 2^61 − 1`: centered range ±2^60 keeps every RFC-0005 quantity a single true integer under `servable` (the `minimal` projection chains at "near 2^60" need re-deriving), reduction is a shift-and-add (about 12 operations per multiplication against 18, 56 per `Fp2` product against 83 with `i² = −1` since `p ≡ 3 mod 4`), symbols stay 8 bytes; `p + 1 = 2^61` gives a circle group of order 2^61 for the encoding domain. Cost model (`counted`): `c_c`, `c_ℓ`, `c_π` × 0.75 gives `μ` −19% to −20% at every context, −15% to −19% if the counts rise 10% (E0, [E0-field]). First killers, in order: the challenge space is 2^122, 6 bits under Goldilocks `Fp2`, against [RFC-0014 'Soundness budget'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0014-interaction-and-soundness.md#soundness-budget) that already grinds for LogUp at frontier volume; RFC-0011's coset encoding and vanishing-mask structure and RFC-0021's proximity analysis must be restated over circle RS codes ([HLP24]); no implementation exists at any size (the search found none) and Stwo's CPU circle DFT is slower than its two-adic NTT ([Syxton25]); a tier-A change to RFC-0002 with a verifier re-implementation.
- **128-bit accumulate-then-reduce in dot-product-shaped Goldilocks kernels.** Sumcheck round sums and LogUp leaf linear combinations sum products before anyone needs the reduced value; accumulating unreduced 128-bit products with carry (three 64-bit words) replaces a 10-operation reduction per product with about 2 carry additions, 18 → about 10 operations per multiply-accumulate (E0, −45% on the multiplication share of such kernels). Terms: `c_ℓ`, `c_π`, glue. First killer: [E0-this] and [EXP-0006](../experiments/EXP-0006-product-leaf-cost/README.md) may already assume it in their 18-operation count, and the fraction tree's nodes are single products (`d_L · d_R`) or sums of two, where the saving is 0–25%; [SUR-0007](SUR-0007-sumcheck.md) lists the general form under [DDBDT26] §3, so this is an input to that seed, not a new idea note, unless the counts show the kernels are dot-product-shaped.
- No seed from [Solberg25] (mid-size primes break the same RFC-0005 quantities and give 120-bit extensions), [Irreducible24], [NVclmad26], [BRW26], [DBHR26] (binary fields: parity on GPU arithmetic, bit decomposition of every range check), [ZGLCZ26] (a 49-bit field and a CPU instruction set), [LZG+26] (field unnamed; a 64-bit field does not fit tensor-core accumulators without limbs, and the per-element figures do not beat the budget), [Syxton25], [StwoFields26], [P3field26], [StwoBook26b], [Buterin24], [HallAndersen24], [Benno23] (mechanisms the counts use), [Nitroo26], [IngStwo25], [NethStwo26], [StarkWare24], [Polyhedra24], [SP1GPU26], [DYH+26], [Uulong26], [VYTA25], [NVIDIA26a], [Icicle26], [Ozcan25], [LYWFD25] (context and anchors, no per-field unit cost to adopt), [Ska26b] (a confirmation of the gap).

## Staleness triggers

- The RFC-0020 v0.1 measurements (E2) of `c_c`, `c_ℓ` and `c_π`, with or without the cross-field kernels above: they replace every count in [E0-field].
- Any published GPU measurement of Goldilocks against a 31-bit field in one code (Plonky3 or SP1 gaining a GPU Goldilocks path, cuPQC-NTT publishing throughput for its built-in and custom primes, ICICLE publishing a field-operation table).
- A GPU measurement of QM31 or KoalaBear⁴ multiplication throughput, or of a circle FFT against a two-adic NTT, on Hopper or Blackwell.
- A Mersenne-61 circle-code implementation at 2^23 or larger.
- The full text of [Ska26b], [ZGLCZ26] or [LZG+26] becoming readable, if it contains a cross-field or Goldilocks-on-GPU table the landing pages hide; TensorZKP naming its field.
- A change to RFC-0005's worst-case ranges (the v0.2 commitment report), which moves the limb count in item 2.
