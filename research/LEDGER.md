Survey summaries (what each one settled; every number as the survey labels it):

- **SUR-0001.** The Johnson regime is now conjecture-free with an error term linear in `n/q` ([BCHKS25] Thm 1.5; [DKT26] with the Lean-checked bound of [ArkLib26]): at live-64, `τ_A` 155 → 130 (−16%), `τ_W` 97 → 68 (−30%), 193 → 162 for ni-100 (E0, primary), which is the condition IDEA-0001 recorded for reopening. The capacity regime is closed for Tessera's codes ([CS25], [DG25], [KKH26], [Kam26]; error ≥ 1/(2n) beyond list decoding), so `τ_A` near 66 needs folded RS or multiplicity codes ([GG25], [LXYYZ26]). Two spec-level facts: RFC-0011 step 3's eq-tensor generator has provable unique-decoding error `ϑ·n/q'` = 2^-100.7 with `ϑ = 20` ([DG24], [DP24log]), not the 2^-105 RFC-0014 writes (both pass), and no Johnson-regime bound covers that generator at 64 bits ([BCGM26] carries `n^2`), so independent uniform `Fp2` coefficients are the prerequisite for any `τ` reduction. Rate 1/4 gives `τ_A` 95 under unique decoding at double the encoded volume; STIR/WHIR/Ligerito-style reductions apply to the 17 resident combined rows (1.14 GB of the proof). Unread primary PDFs are staleness triggers: [DKT26], [FS26], [ABF26]'s July revision.
- **SUR-0002.** The 40 ps per LogUp-GKR lookup has no measurement anywhere (Stwo, SP1, Ziren, DeepProve, Expander publish none). An operation-and-byte count of Tessera's combined fraction tree ([E0-this], E0, primary) gives 24.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents textbook (101 ps at H100 int32 peak, 169 ps at the 60% utilization of [VYTA25]) and 18.5 with the numerators-1 first layer Stwo and SP1 ship (76 / 127 ps); memory 288 B per lookup unfused (86 ps) to 120 B at eight fused rounds (36 ps). The 15–20 ps target of bet 1a is reachable on bandwidth, not on compute, without an algorithmic change; `c_ℓ` = 80 ps raises `μ` by 25.6–28.7% (E0). One-hot arguments lose under hash-based commitments by construction (Lasso 9 small elements per lookup at `c = 3`, Shout 64 Booleans; [DBK+26]: nonzero-proportional commitment "is essential to Twist and Shout"), confirming RFC-0008 Alternatives 1 with a named reopening condition. The separation principle leaves the count, the key width and the once-per-proof table term; the remaining count lever is the split gadget.
- **SUR-0003.** A Ligero-family commitment costs exactly two full regeneration passes, and storing the advice instead is 19.6–21.0 TB per 405B 16K/16K unit, a 340 s NVMe pass against 12 s of regeneration (E0), so the floor for `n_regen` is about 2 full-forward equivalents plus attention-only passes against the 4 estimated (`n_regen` 3 is −11% of `μ` at 1K, 2 is −22%, E0). Hobbit's tile fold ([PP25], E1 primary) removes the objection behind RFC-0018 Alternative 2 and would divide the streaming term by `c` (9 at 16K, 12 at 128K): about −28% of `μ` at 16K and −42% at 128K (E0) if the fold's cross terms cost about one pass. Time-space provers ([BCFFMMZ25], [DDBDT26], [Jolt26]) show streamed rounds need not be one pass each. Distributed sumcheck is settled at the communication level (deVirgo's master-aggregated rounds are RFC-0018's all-reduce; Pianist/HyperPianist's O(1)/O(log T) need homomorphic commitments); the unmeasured quantity is per-round synchronization latency (about 10^4 rounds, 0.2–0.3 s at a 20–30 µs floor, E0). Corrections: `c = 9` streamed rounds at 16K where RFC-0018 says about 8; OQ-0018-1's smaller proof units save 10.7% of `μ` at 16K (E0) but add about 7.7 GB of combined rows and break the 4 GiB proof gate.
- **SUR-0004.** The certificate a skipped (query, key block) must carry is fixed by RFC-0005's exp table (`σ = 16`): exact zero when the score is more than `17 ln 2 ≈ 11.78` nats below the row maximum; Louver's ball bound ([Dehghankar26]) is the only published bound shape cheaper than it saves (about 50–100 ps against 5.2 ns per 64-key block, E0). No source measures the hard sparsity this needs; the mass figures ([Jiang24] 96.8% in the top 4,096 of 131,072; [Liu24] 89% in the top 1,000; [Zhu25] 90% in 3–8% of tokens) do not answer it, so the prize (`μ(16K)` 35.1 → 20.5 / 13.5 / 9.5 at 50 / 75 / 90% skipped, E0) hangs on an unpublished measurement and on padding the secret skip count. Replacing or sharing exp lookups is settled and negative (every system read spends at least one lookup per exp; the I-BERT route costs two), confirming RFC-0008 and RFC-0009 Alternatives 1–2. Nothing cheaper than the GKR product tree exists for the max certificate; the levers are base-field leaf levels (at most −3.5% of `μ`) and a shared pass with the exp fraction tree, both second order. Proof-aware `D` shapes ship in production (Gemma 3 `A_eff/A` 0.27 / 0.18 at 16K / 128K, Llama 4 0.63 / 0.30, DeepSeek V3.2 about 0.03, E0); no study reports the bits-per-token `Û` bounded mode prices.
- **SUR-0005.** No published measurement of a hash-based commitment per element on an H100 over Goldilocks exists anywhere found (Plonky3, ICICLE, sppark, Stwo, SP1, cuPQC and every 2024–2026 paper), and the nearest external anchors, the only public Goldilocks GPU NTT (10.6–17.3 ps per butterfly on an RX 7900 XTX, E1) and the only GPU Merkle figure at Tessera's leaf volume (Poseidon2 at 219 GB/s on a Blackwell workstation GPU, vendor claim), translate to about 140–200 ps per committed element for an unfused HBM-bound kernel (E0), the upper half of EXP-0002's [58, 157] ps interval, where the cost model gives `μ(16K)` = 65 at 150 ps and both SC-2 ceilings fail. Linear-time and sketched codes (Blaze 8n additions, Bolt (3+ε)n additions plus hashing 1.0n elements) undercut Tessera's 34 butterflies per element on paper and on CPUs but live over binary or 127-bit fields and open by full-vector folding, so RFC-0011 Alternative 3 stands with the yardstick sharpened. FRI-family schemes admit a one-pass streaming prover exactly when their first fold is an interleaved random combination of rows, which ReedWeave (ePrint 2026/2147, Goldilocks) formalizes and which is RFC-0011's combined-row step already, while naive folding of the advice would cost about five regeneration passes (E0); applied to the 17 combined rows, a Ligerito-style second level (read in full) removes the 1.14 GB of combined rows and the 17 verifier NTTs for a proof of about 1.0 GB (−48%, E0) while leaving the 0.72 GB of opened columns untouched, and its verifier is succinct only with coefficient-form rows (IDEA-0002), so the two compose. Hash-based pay-per-bit (Binius, ring switching) needs a subfield tower that Goldilocks lacks below 64 bits, so NG-5 on this field reduces to a 31-bit field or a lattice scheme, and the lattice line (Greyhound, Hachi, Akita, Maltese, LaBinius; Hyperwolf withdrawn) has reached hash-based prover cost on CPU for bit-valued data but publishes no per-bit figure, no GPU figure and no hiding statement. Logarithmic randomness buys log2(rows) verifier randomness and the partial-evaluation structure Ligerito's recursion batches on, not proof size, and the hash choice (BLAKE3 against Poseidon2 and SHA-3, which vendors measure at 219 and 388–891 GB/s on Blackwell while BLAKE3 has no GPU figure at all) is handed to the hash survey.
- **SUR-0006.** No public measurement of BLAKE3 on an H100 (or any CUDA GPU) exists, so the 530 GB/s per GPU of RFC-0004 and RISK-8 stays unmeasured; the only public GPU BLAKE3 is a 2020 Vulkan toy slower than one CPU core, and the indirect anchors (hashcat SHA-256 at 15.1 G compressions/s on an H100, secondary; BLAKE3 mining rates implying 490–550 int32 ops per compression, claim, below EXP-0002's 792–960 count; cuPQC's SHA family at 388–891 GB/s on a Blackwell workstation GPU, claim) say the budget is reachable but not proven, while EXP-0002's counts say the hash alone needs 39–48% of the H100 integer pipeline at rate 1/2 (E0). Field-native hashes stay far outside the budget: Poseidon2-Goldilocks is 20–25× BLAKE3 by operation count and 13–35× by scaling the only vendor Poseidon2 GPU figure (cuPQC, 219 GB/s of BabyBear leaf data on an RTX PRO 6000, claim), Monolith about 3× (E0 from secondary CPU ratios), Tip5 slower than Monolith and Skyscraper is for 256-bit primes, so RFC-0004 Alternative 3's rejection stands with a larger margin. On hashed volume, Merkle arity, caps, column batching and packed symbols move at most 10^-5 of the prover's hashing; the one lever is the 32-byte chain value fed as input bytes each step: feeding it as BLAKE3's key (its initial chaining value, exactly how BLAKE3 chains blocks inside a chunk) reaches the floor of 0.25 compressions per committed element at `B = 32` (−20% of the hash, `Δμ` −0.8% to −1.7%, E0), superseding the `B = 28` finding, and OQ-0004-1 (hash only systematic symbols) is unsound as stated. Recursion (NG-3) is gated by the volume the verifier re-hashes (1.02 GB of opened columns, 1.6×10^7 compressions) rather than by the hash: as BLAKE3 the recursion circuit would be as large as the proof, as Poseidon2 about 3% of it but at 20–25× the prover's hashing cost over 39 TB per proof, so SUR-0001's volume seeds come first. The survey also finds RFC-0004 Alternative 1's SHA-256/BLAKE3 instruction ratio overstated (nearer 1.5–2× than 2.7×) and recommends the RFC-0020 `c_c` benchmark report hash and encoder separately with int32-pipe utilization.
- **SUR-0007.** The per-round algorithm for `eq × product` sumchecks is settled on paper by Gruen (2024/108), Dao–Thaler (2024/1210) and the extended "Speeding Up Sum-Check Proving" (2026/587, full text read via the Internet Archive): never build or bind the full `eq` table, evaluate one node fewer per round, and reconstruct the degree-d message so the transcript is unchanged, a prover-only change worth about a quarter to a third of a level sumcheck's multiplications (E0, from the 8.5N → 5N Spartan accounting), none of which Tessera's `tessera-sumcheck` engine implements today (it builds one `Fp2` eq table per instance and binds it every round). Small-field (Bagad–Domb–Thaler) and Toom–Cook product algorithms buy little over Goldilocks with `Fp2` challenges because the extension cost ratio is 3 rather than 16–145 and no RFC-0010 relation exceeds degree 3, but 2026/587 §3's delayed-reduction integer arithmetic (10–24× on linear combinations, E1 on BN254) is the primary source IDEA-0010's integer first round lacked. For passes, the full text of the time-space provers (2025/1473: `log_η(n/k) + dk` passes, `k = 2` at 1.8–2× linear time and 0.008× memory, E1) and 2026/587 §5–6's round batching show that a window of `v = 4–5` streamed rounds can be answered from a `3^v`-point grid in one regeneration pass at 15–23 `Fp` multiplications per pair against about 33–38 for the per-round prover (E0, own count), cutting RFC-0018's streamed passes from 9 to 2 at 16K and 12 to 2–3 at 128K (about −25% and −34% of `μ` on the corrected profile, E0) with no protocol change, which dominates both the univariate skip (tier A, 1.4–1.9× on skipped rounds) and Hobbit's fold. GKR batching across heights is confirmed as RFC-0010's existing design by Stwo's code (join at matching depth, `2^{n_unused}` scaling, one shared eq table, two node sums per degree-3 round, mixed grand-product and LogUp gates in one batch, which is the shape OQ-0010-1 asks for) and first measured by the ragged-GKR post (one batched walk 1.63× faster to prove, 6.1× to verify and 6.26× smaller than independent walks, 4.0× faster than max padding; E1 on an i7-1365U). Every number is E0 or E1 on another field (BN254, 128-bit, GF(2)); nothing measures these techniques over a 64-bit field with a degree-2 extension, and the Jolt uni-skip verifier bug (zkSecurity, May 2026) is the caveat any skip or batching round in Tessera must test against.
- **SUR-0008.** Libra-style masking is the cheapest known zero knowledge for a sumcheck and is size-optimal (a mask with O(ℓ) coefficients counters exactly the O(ℓ) revealed elements, [Xie24] §2.4.1), and every later hash-based or lattice-based construction (ZOOK [CFW26], LatticeBlindFold [DallAva26], zkComposer) keeps that shape, while the homomorphic-commitment family (Hyrax 2018, BlindFold 2026: +3 KB, 0–10% prover, Dory/Pedersen/HyperKZG) is not post-quantum and so inapplicable to RFC-0011. Counted against RFC-0013's own cost table with the counted profile of the cost model, Tessera's zero knowledge costs about 0.1% of `μ(16K)` (mask rows 8×10^9 elements = 0.09%, masking polynomials < 10^-5, blinding rows 0.001 even after the #239 doubling to 34, weight budget +0.02% at most) and under 0.01% of proof size (E0), so the premise that zk hides inside the 1.15 glue misattributes a 15% term by two orders of magnitude and the cost model should carry zk ≈ 1.001 as its own line. The hiding rules of RFC-0011/RFC-0013 match production Ligero (Google's Longfellow: R = nreq random cells per block, three blinding rows with coefficient 1, blinding in the field of the revealed combination), whose subfield remark is the mirror image of the #239 defect; four leading hash-based provers failed on zero knowledge in exactly such mechanical details (Virgo shipped without its masking polynomial, +90% prover to add it [RV24]; Plonky2/RISC Zero/Triton base-field randomizers [HK24], RISC Zero "no solution available yet" [RZ24]; Orion's ZK revision broke soundness [dHS25]), which confirms RISK-6's written-simulator gate and suggests three structural distinguisher tests. Composition across stages is understood in three layers: auxiliary-input ZK composes sequentially with additive statistical error ([Gol02]/[GO94], RFC-0013 step 6), [CFS17] §11 is the only formal composition for GKR-shaped recursion (one random linear combination of claims per vertex and one random extension per vertex, i.e. RFC-0013's stacked layers and masked extensions), and [CFW26] gives the first composable honest-verifier ZK definition for oracle-sharing reductions with an explicit per-reduction error, which the BCS compiler carries to the non-interactive mode [BCS16]; what no source supplies is a theorem for RFC-0013's exact shape, so the v0.4 simulator argument must be written in that structure, with the live verifier's free column choices argued separately. OQ-0013-1 is answered by the independence condition every source proves (packing is admissible and worth at most 0.3% of committed rows, 0.09% of `μ`, so it should be closed as not worth a label change), OQ-0013-2 by [GOS24]'s warning that a mask vanishing at a revealed point hides nothing (fix: prefix-indexed masks with `Z` over the random coordinates only plus zk labels per level, < 10^6 elements), and the same prefix-indexed construction plus a "random instance folded first" one-time pad is the prerequisite for a zero-knowledge Hobbit tile fold.
- **SUR-0009.** SUR-0009 reconstructs RISK-2's +82,944 commitments per layer per token as exactly the two norm keys (32,768, removed by IDEA-0011), the RoPE q/k keys (17,408, `< 2^44`) and the two `minimal`-profile accumulators (32,768, `< 2^60`/`< 2^58`, unfixable by range analysis), and finds that the default `servable` profile the cost model never counted has 260,096–310,272 elements per layer per token, +15% of μ(1K) corrected and +21.5% counted (E0, primary). No source measures activation or accumulator ranges at 405B; the sound substitute the literature offers is a public weight-row norm bound (A2Q+/AXE), which with a free `‖y‖₂` fact from `RsqrtRow` tightens Q/K/V accumulators from 28 to 23–25 bits (E0), so the RoPE key reaches 33–34 bits only with 9-bit constants and still needs `B_max = 34` for that one table (a net E0 gain: 1.7×10^10 table leaves and 64 GiB of counters against 3.6×10^10 lookups and commitments per 16K batch), correcting ledger item 26's premise. Per-tensor certificates are cheaper than per-element checks when they are relational facts (free) or sum-of-four-squares `ℓ2` relations (4 commitments and 4 lookups per row against BigIntCompare's 20/30; a once-per-model weight certificate costs 7.9×10^7 commitments, 0.02% of a batch), with zero knowledge and SP-7 kept by publishing only a quantizer-chosen power-of-two cap per tensor; per-element square decompositions lose to a lookup. Power-of-two scales are measured cheap at 8 bits (≤ 0.5% perplexity at ≤ 14B, lossless MXINT8 at 175B), so the `affine` fallback that would double rescale counts is unlikely, and where it is needed, 8-bit dyadic multipliers plus certified accumulators keep the key at 32 bits. Every source confirms RFC-0006's worst-case interval rule and RFC-0009's split as minimal for a wide key; the exposed inputs are the uncounted `servable` profile, the loosest-possible accumulator bound and a global `B_max`.
- **SUR-0010.** Fourteen months of releases produced no system that meets scoreboard level 1 (dense ≥ 70B, full coverage, 100 bits, zero knowledge, hash-based, measured on a GPU): the frontier-adjacent GPU number is still Attestable's alpha claim (53/77 tok/s, Gemma 4 31B, one H100, no proving time or memory published) and the only full-coverage frontier-scale measurement is VerInf's single run, whose repository, run archive (2026-07-10) and draft paper are now public (Ligero rate 1/4 over Goldilocks, BLAKE3, Freivalds matmuls, LogUp; 14.26 h prove, 93.6 GB at 40 columns, 17.7 h verify, 2^-16.6 per challenge). - **SUR-0011.** No public measurement compares Goldilocks with M31/QM31, BabyBear or KoalaBear on an H100 or any CUDA GPU per multiplication, NTT or circle FFT, no GPU binary-tower multiplication benchmark exists (open-binius lists it as an open issue), no Mersenne-61 prover exists, and the July 2026 small-field SoK (ePrint 2026/1371) names the absence of a controlled cross-field comparison as the field's main empirical gap. Counting int32 lane operations from the Plonky3 and Stwo field sources (E0, primary): a 31-bit base multiplication is 2.6× cheaper than Goldilocks' (6–8 against 18 operations), a butterfly 2.2× cheaper (13 against 28) and the hashed codeword bytes halve, so `c_c` would roughly halve; but a 128-bit challenge multiplication costs 1.7–2.1× more in QM31 (Stwo: 16 M31 products, 166 operations) or BabyBear⁴ (Plonky3: schoolbook, 173) than in Goldilocks `Fp2` (83), because additions dominate the degree-4 extensions, so `c_ℓ` and `c_π` (51–53% of μ(16K) under `counted`) rise. The rescale-count effect RFC-0002 cites is a uniqueness effect: seven quantities in RFC-0005's worst-case column exceed 2^30 (RoPE keys 2^44, norm sums 2^54 and keys 2^37, attention output 2^39, softmax sums 2^33 and reciprocals 2^32, `minimal` projection chains 2^58–2^60), a residue mod p of a 2^39 integer has 2^8 preimages, so SP-3 forces limb sumchecks on split operands with per-limb range lookups (at least 2 commitments, 3 lookups, 2 sumchecks against Goldilocks' 1–2, 1–2, 1) on 66,560 of the 137,216 rescaled elements per layer per token under `servable` and all of them under `minimal`. The cost model prices the switch at μ(16K) +24% at the centre of the counts (+7% optimistic, +41% pessimistic, −13% only under a hypothetical even extension cost) under `counted` and +17% under `corrected`, so the source's "about 20% either way" is the right width with the wrong symmetry, and RFC-0002 Alternatives 1, 2 and 5 are confirmed with the reason sharpened from "overflow sooner" to "no unique true integer above 2^30". Binary fields are out for the base field (every range check and carry becomes a bit decomposition) and at arithmetic parity on GPUs since CUDA 13.3's `clmad` puts a GF(2^128) product (6 instructions, GHASH 6,335 GB/s on a B200, vendor claim) at about the rate of a Goldilocks `Fp2` product by count, leaving per-bit commitment (NG-5, SUR-0005) as their only case; the three seeds are a cross-field microbenchmark inside the RFC-0020 v0.1 gate, Mersenne-61 with circle codes (−19% to −20% of μ at E0 against a 2^122 challenge space, a restated RFC-0011/RFC-0021 over circle RS codes and no implementation anywhere) and 128-bit accumulate-then-reduce as an input to SUR-0007's lazy-reduction seed.
- **SUR-0012.** Pricing RFC-0011's verifier-work row with the repository's own unit costs (EXP-0002, EXP-0007 and a run of the verifier's tv-hash bench today: safe-Rust BLAKE3 at 1.188 GB/s on 288-byte column steps, one M4 Max core) gives about 13 s on one core, of which the 17 Fp2 coset NTTs plus the 17 omitted interpolation NTTs are 7.5 s (57%), the inner products 2.2 s, decoding about 2 s and hashing only 0.6 s, and 0.4 s on 32 cores, except that the verifier as written is single-threaded (rayon allowlisted, used by no crate), so the scoreboard's "about 500×" against Attestable compares the 300 s budget with a claim while the projected gap is 20–80× on one core and 1–3× on 32, and the 2 GB transfer itself (17 s at 1 Gbit/s) is the term no CPU touches (E0 over E1, primary). Recursion (NG-3) is priced for the first time: the verifier's arithmetic is about 1.1×10^10 layered multiplication gates, about 1.4 GPU-s at Expander's rate, and the 1.4×10^7 BLAKE3 column compressions cost about 170 core-seconds at Flock's 82k/s or 0.2% of the proof's committed volume at the leanVM circuit's 340 words per compression (E0, primary), so the cost of compressing a 2 GB proof to tens of MiB is not prover time but a second proof system in the independent verifier's trusted base (SP-6, RFC-0013's objection), a binary-versus-Goldilocks field mismatch, and non-interactive mode only. Attestable's pages make no formal-verification claim at all (technical post and homepage re-read 2026-10-17), so the landscape's "formal verification claimed" cell and the scoreboard's "bar to clear" are unsupported and the bar is Tessera's own NG-7. The state of verifier formalization is layered: protocol soundness exists for the sumcheck (Isabelle AFP), FRI round-by-round soundness (Lean, six weeks with AI provers), a fixed-statement zk-STARK end to end (Isabelle, 2^-137) and Johnson-regime RS bounds (Lean, already imported by Draft RFC-0021), nothing for an interleaved Ligero-style test or LogUp-GKR; statement-encoding soundness is the one layer a vendor has finished twice (StarkWare's Cairo and S-two AIRs in Lean); implementation extraction (hax 0.4 via Charon and Aeneas) covers tv-core's safe-Rust subset by construction but has unverified fidelity with documented semantic gaps, and the SP1 Lean effort (51 of 62 opcodes complete, a JALR bug hidden by a scoping hypothesis) shows how "verified" becomes a facade. The route for NG-7 is therefore a Lean blueprint of docs/verifier/spec.md tied to tv-core by the tamper corpus first, with extraction only for leaf functions, and the two high findings of the Stwo-Cairo verifier audit (public inputs not absorbed before the LogUp challenge; unused instances left in the LogUp sum) name what the tamper corpus must contain before any of it.
- **SUR-0013.** The approximate sum-check of [BDGIKT25] (EUROCRYPT 2026, read in full) is closed as a competitor at Tessera's sizes by its own bounds: round-by-round soundness `d/\|S\| + 8√(vd·δ/Δ)` with completeness `δ ≥ d·2^v·ε` forces a precision ratio `Δ/ε` of 2^252–2^256 for 100 bits at `v = 41` and 2^223–2^227 for one 16K softmax row (E0, [E0-this]), i.e. a 220–260-bit mantissa where Goldilocks pays 64 integer bits, and [Zamir26] shows layerwise tolerances do not compose, which confirms exact mode, RFC-0017's `Û` and RFC-0009's tolerance-band rejection. Bounded-mode fidelity is moving from the deployment side: an unmodified stack gives 80 distinct outputs in 1,000 greedy runs ([He25]), batch-invariant kernels cost 1.6–2.1×, fused-upcast kernels make linear layers bit-identical across Ampere, Ada and Hopper ([ReproFuse26]), and power-of-two INT8 scales, RFC-0005's own choice, make W8A8 inference kernel-invariant and byte-identical in tokens at 0.3–0.5% perplexity ([ChenTR26], E1), so an integer-exact deployment is exact mode without the modified-stack cost. Non-cryptographic verification now has measured members at every point (fingerprints at about 1% of inference with 96% detection, statistical re-execution with the weights, refereed delegation at ≥ 2×, optimistic tolerance games at 0.3% plus a challenge window, deterministic re-execution in TEEs at about 2% as a vendor claim) and none hides weights, survives a hardware-controlling adversary or gives a soundness bound, while TEEs cost 4–8% alone and 17.7–21.1% under TDX (E1) and attest firmware, driver and microcode rather than the model, which makes them a coverage anchor (NG-8, source §9 problem 7) rather than a competitor. Folding and accumulation (ZKTorch: LLaMA-2-7B in 2,645 s with a 22.85 MB proof and 100 s verifier over KZG; Kaizen; Collapse by gate count only) remain the homomorphic-commitment recursion route of NG-3, and no paper proves MoE expert routing with routing privacy in any proof system, though CryptoMoE's fixed-capacity slots are the MPC precedent for source §9 problem 2's mechanism.
