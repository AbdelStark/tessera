## The question

INV-ARCH-2 ([01 §6](../../../docs/spec/01-architecture.md#6-concurrency-and-determinism)) says prover control flow and memory access patterns depend only on public data, and [06](../../../docs/spec/06-security.md) lists it as the only defense against a timing side channel toward a malicious verifier (ADV-V); [RFC-0013](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0013-zero-knowledge.md) leaves timing to it. Issue #257 observes that `tessera_logup::multiplicities` (`crates/tessera-logup/src/instance.rs` on `main`, the loop `counts[row] += 1` over each lookup's row) increments a counter at an address the witness chooses, that reading a table value at a secret key (the `Exp` dense core of up to 2^24 entries, [RFC-0008 'Structured evaluation'](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#structured-evaluation)) does the same, and that P1, the pass that counts multiplicities before `Round1` ([RFC-0018 'Pass structure'](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#pass-structure)), is observable by a live verifier as the latency before `Round1`. [RFC-0018 'Determinism'](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#determinism) already carries half an exception ("data-dependent table gathers are memory accesses whose addresses depend on secrets, which is acceptable because the verifier observes no timing or memory traffic of the prover node"), whose premise the live protocol contradicts: the verifier does observe P1's duration.

The issue names three ways out: a constant-duration envelope for P1 (release `Round1` at a deadline public shapes fix), oblivious counting and reads, or a stated exception with the leakage recorded as an accepted risk. This experiment prices all three: (a) an E0 upper bound on the leak's bandwidth, in bits per proof and per epoch; (b) an E1 probe of how much the counting loop's duration actually depends on the key distribution on a CPU; (c) the cost of the envelope and of oblivious counting under the research cost model's `counted` profile.

## Hypothesis

A live verifier learns fewer than 8 bits per proof about the witness from P1's duration through the secret-indexed increments and table reads at a jitter floor of 0.1% of P1's duration (fewer than 3 bits at the 3% level at which RFC-0020 flags a measurement as noisy), because the secret-dependent part of that duration is bounded by the all-miss cost of the increments — on this CPU no pre-registered key distribution is slower than the uniform, all-miss one by more than 25%, while the slowest and the fastest distributions differ by at least 2× at 2^28 counters, so the channel is real but one-sided — and that cost, priced at the H100's random-access ceiling over the 2.6×10^12 lookups of a 16K-token proof, is below 15% of P1's `counted`-profile duration.

## Success criterion

Written before any code. Three parts; the verdict needs all three.

**Part (a), the E0 bound.** P1's duration is one noisy scalar per proof. Its dependence on the secret runs only through the cache behaviour of the multiplicity increments and the table reads (every other access pattern of P1 is public by INV-ARCH-2), so the secret-dependent part lies in an interval of width `Δ`, the difference between the slowest and the fastest key distribution, and an observer with timing noise of standard deviation `σ = Z · D_P1` distinguishes at most `1 + Δ/σ` durations. The leak is at most `B(Z) = log2(1 + Δ / (Z · D_P1))` bits per proof (the log of the number of distinguishable observations, the bound [AZM10] use for mitigated timing channels; the Gaussian-channel capacity `½·log2(1 + (Δ/2σ)²)` is reported beside it as the tighter figure). Pre-registered inputs, all E0:

- `N_big = lookups_per_token × batch_tokens = 27,740,160 × 16,384 = 4.545×10^11` lookups into the linear layers' tables per 16K-token proof (the `counted` profile of [tools/cost_model.py](../../tools/cost_model.py), RISK-2's 220,160 per layer per token; the queue item's "4.6×10^11"), whose `Requant`, `Silu` and `Range` tables have up to 2^32 rows ([RFC-0008 'Table kinds'](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#table-kinds)) and whose counters, `≤ 2^32 × 4 B` per table sharded across the GPUs ([08 §4](../../../docs/spec/08-performance-budget.md#4-memory-budgets)), live in HBM.
- `N_att = scores_per_token_per_ctx × S × T = 8,064 × 16,384 × 16,384 = 2.165×10^12` softmax lookups into `Exp` tables whose dense cores (≤ 2^24 entries, 64 MiB of counters per table, 8 MiB per GPU after sharding) fit a 50 MB L2; `N_all = N_big + N_att = 2.62×10^12`, the "about 2.6×10^12 at 16K tokens" of [SUR-0002](../../surveys/SUR-0002-lookups.md).
- `c_rand = 32 B / (3.35 TB/s × 0.6) = 15.9 ps`: the cost of one random 4-byte increment when every increment misses to HBM, one 32 B sector per access ([JMSS18] measure 32 B load granularity and 64 B L2 lines on Volta; NVIDIA GPUs since then fetch 32 B sectors) at the H100 SXM's 3.35 TB/s ([NVIDIA-H100], `secondary`) and the 60% utilization the knowledge base uses throughout ([VYTA25]). Sensitivity at 40% and 100% of the peak is reported.
- `Δ_max = N_all × c_rand`: the whole all-miss cost, charged as if the fastest distribution were free. Charging `N_att` at the HBM rate although its counters fit the L2 is deliberate: it makes the bound conservative and covers L2-atomic contention of unknown size.
- `D_P1 = T × (native_s_per_token + 1.15 × (commits_per_token + scores_per_token_per_ctx × S) × commit_ps)` at `S = T = 16,384` under `counted` (`commit_ps = 94`): the forward pass plus the commitments P1 streams, without the counting itself (including it would only lower the bound).
- Jitter floor `Z = 0.1%` of `D_P1`, with `Z = 1%` and `Z = 3%` beside it. Evidence for the floor: the only measured run-to-run variation of a multi-second GPU workload the search found is 0.98–6.46% over 1,000 repetitions of one GEMM kernel on one GPU ([GKHDWBW25], primary, V100–H200 fleets), RFC-0020 flags a coefficient of variation above 3%, and a live verifier adds network and host jitter on top; 0.1% is therefore a floor, not an estimate, and the bound at it is an upper bound on the leak. The CPU probe's own coefficients of variation are reported as the E1 jitter of this machine.
- Per epoch: `681 × B(Z)` with 681 the live proofs an epoch supports ([RFC-0012](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0012-weight-commitment.md) after #245: `(B_W − 8)/6 = 681`); the per-epoch total is about 681 different witnesses, so it is a sum of per-proof leaks, not a cumulative leak about one secret. The repeated-proof case (the same witness proven `k` times, `σ/√k`) is reported as `B(Z) + ½·log2 k`.

**Part (b), the E1 probe.** One Rust program, its own Cargo workspace in this directory, counts multiplicities over seeded key streams of `N = 2^27` keys into a counter array of `T ∈ {2^20, 2^24, 2^28}` entries (4 MiB, 64 MiB and 1 GiB of `u32` counters: the 4-byte counters of 08 §4; `u64`, the width of the production function, as a second arm) and reads a `u32` value table of the same size at the same keys (the table-read arm). Seven key distributions of the same length, from SplitMix64 with the seeds in the Method:

- `uniform` (every key independent and uniform over `[0, T)`: the all-miss case at `T = 2^28`),
- `zipf` (a hot set of 4,096 rows at uniformly drawn positions, ranks drawn from the law `P(rank k) ∝ 1/k` by `k = ⌊4096^u⌋ + 1`: the concentrated case),
- `sorted` (the `uniform` stream sorted ascending: the same multiset, the friendliest order),
- `sequential` (`key_i = i mod T`),
- `all_equal` (one row for every key: on a CPU a dependency chain through one counter, on a GPU the contended-atomic case the issue names),
- `gauss_narrow` and `gauss_wide` (two plausible witnesses: keys `clamp(T/2 + round(σ·z), 0, T − 1)` with `z` standard normal, `σ = T/2^10` and `σ = T/2^6`: an activation distribution 16× more dispersed than another).

Protocol as RFC-0020's gate: 3 warm-ups and 10 measured iterations per `(arm, T, distribution)`; median, minimum, maximum and coefficient of variation; a coefficient above 3% reruns the case once and the rerun is what is reported, flagged if still above. The counter array is zeroed and page-resident before each timed iteration, so the timed region is the counting loop alone (the production function's lazy page faults are a separate, CPU-backend-only finding). The quantities:

- `R_slow(arm, T) = t(slowest distribution) / t(uniform)`: the premise of part (a) holds on this CPU when the all-miss distribution is the slowest within 25%.
- `s(T) = (t_max − t_min) / t_max` over the seven distributions, `count32` arm: the spread of the counting pass, as a fraction of the slowest duration.
- `g(T) = |t(gauss_wide) − t(gauss_narrow)| / t(uniform)`, `count32` arm: how far apart two plausible witnesses lie, in units of the all-miss duration; `g / Z` distinguishable levels at jitter `Z` of that duration, their log2 the bits that separate the two.
- Per-increment costs in nanoseconds for every arm, size and distribution, and the CPU reference backend's per-proof spread `N_all × (c_slowest − c_fastest)` on one core (E1, reported, not judged).

**Part (c), the mitigations** under `counted` at 1K, 4K, 16K and 128K tokens, each as `Δμ` and as a fraction of the profile's `μ`:

- Envelope: `Round1` released at a public deadline. Cost per proof `= C_max − C_typical` of the increments, `C_max = N_all × c_rand` and `C_typical = C_max × t_typ / t_uniform` with `t_typ / t_uniform` the measured CPU ratio of the mean of the two Gaussian proxies to `uniform` at `T = 2^28` (an assumption carried from the CPU to the GPU, bracketed by the ratio 0, where the typical case is free and the envelope costs all of `C_max`), divided by the native GPU-seconds of the proof. The deadline also needs a safety margin above the typical run; `3σ` at `Z = 3%` of `D_P1` is priced as a separate line. The envelope's premise is that the slowest case is the all-miss case; if the GPU's contended-atomic case is slower, only an E2 measurement can size the deadline.
- Oblivious counting: a sort-based multiplicity pass. Operation counts: bucket oblivious sort at `6·N·log2 N` memory accesses ([ACNPRS20], the cheapest oblivious sort in practice) and Batcher's bitonic network at `(N/4)·log2 N·(log2 N + 1)` compare-exchanges ([Bat68]), each access or exchange moving 4-byte keys (one read and one write per key per access, 16 B per compare-exchange), at the same 60%-of-3.35 TB/s throughput, for `N = N_big` (the queue item's framing) and `N = N_all`; the memory needed to hold the stream (`4 B × N`, against 640 GB of HBM per node) is stated beside the count. The CPU probe times a bitonic network on `2^22` and `2^24` keys of the same streams as an E1 anchor of its distribution independence and its cost relative to direct counting.
- Stated exception: cost zero; risk `B(Z)` of part (a).

**Verdicts.**

- `validated`: (i) `R_slow ≤ 1.25` for the `count32` and `count64` arms at `T = 2^28` and for the table-read arm at `T = 2^24` (the dense-core size), that is, the all-miss distribution is the slowest within 25% on every arm the bound's premise needs; (ii) `s(2^28) ≥ 0.5` for `count32` (the channel is real: at least 2× between the extremes); (iii) `B(0.1%) ≤ 8` bits and `B(3%) ≤ 3` bits per proof under the pre-registered inputs; (iv) every coefficient of variation on those arms is at most 3% after at most one rerun.
- `refuted`: `R_slow > 2` on any of the three arms of (i) (a dependency-bound or contention-like distribution dominates the all-miss case, so the random-access ceiling does not bound the spread and the envelope's deadline cannot be set from it), or `B(0.1%) > 16` bits.
- `inconclusive`: anything else: `1.25 < R_slow ≤ 2` on an arm of (i); `s(2^28) < 0.5` with (i) and (iii) holding (the CPU shows no usable channel, so the probe cannot speak to the premise); or a coefficient of variation above 3% after the rerun on an arm of (i).

A count that differs between the direct loop and the sort-based reference is a defect in the harness, fixed and rerun before any number is read, never a verdict.

## Method

- **Harness.** `multleak`, one Rust program in this directory, its own Cargo workspace (empty `[workspace]` table), `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`, no dependencies: the counting loop is written as the production function writes it (`counts.get_mut(row)` with the `row < rows` check, [RFC-0008 'Fraction tree'](../../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#fraction-tree)), once over `u32` counters and once over `u64`; the table-read arm accumulates `table[key]` with wrapping addition into a `u64` that `std::hint::black_box` keeps alive; the sort-based reference sorts a copy of the stream with `sort_unstable` and counts runs; the bitonic arm is a textbook bitonic sorting network over a power-of-two prefix of the stream, followed by the same run-length count. The worktree this experiment runs in predates `instance.rs` (its logup crate has `lib.rs` only), so the production loop is reproduced, not linked; the loop is six lines and is quoted in the harness.
- **Inputs.** SplitMix64 streams of `N = 2^27` keys per distribution and table size. Seeds: `uniform` `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0001`, `zipf` ranks `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0002` and hot-set positions `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0003`, `all_equal`'s row `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0004`, `gauss_narrow` `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0005`, `gauss_wide` `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0006`, the value table `0x7E55_E7A0_0257_0007`; `sorted` is `uniform` sorted; `sequential` needs no seed. The Zipf rank as implemented is `k = ⌊4096^u⌋ + 1 ∈ [2, 4096]` for `u` uniform in `[0, 1)`, so `P(k) ≈ 1 / ((k − 1)·ln 4096)` and the hot set's first row is never drawn: 4,095 rows carry the stream, the first of them about 8% of it. Gaussian draws by Box–Muller from two 53-bit uniforms. Streams are generated outside the timed regions; the per-key costs are medians over `N` keys, so a 1 ms pass is 0.007 ns per key.
- **Protocol.** 3 warm-ups, 10 measured iterations; median, minimum, maximum, coefficient of variation (sample standard deviation over the mean); above 3% one rerun, reported and flagged. Times per pass in milliseconds and per key in nanoseconds. One process, one thread.
- **Correctness checks**, each aborting the run on failure: every count array sums to `N`; `count32`, `count64` and the sort-based reference agree entry for entry, and their FNV-1a digest is printed; `sorted` reproduces `uniform`'s digest; `sequential` gives `N/T` per row (`1` per row below `N` at `T = 2^28`) and `all_equal` gives `N` at one row and 0 elsewhere, both checked in closed form; the bitonic arm's run-length counts agree with the direct count of its prefix; the table-read arm's checksum agrees with a second pass in a different order (the sorted keys).
- **Translation (E0).** [bound.py](bound.py) reads `results/run.json` and computes part (a)'s bound at the three jitter floors, the per-epoch and repeated-proof figures, part (c)'s costs, and the `--set` values for the cost-model runs below; its output is `results/bound.txt`.

## Results

Every correctness check passed in all 21 `(T, distribution)` cases, before and after each arm was timed: the counts sum to `N`; the `u32`, `u64` and sort-based counts agree entry for entry; `sorted` reproduces `uniform`'s digest and gather checksum at every size; `sequential` and `all_equal` match their closed forms; the table-read checksum equals `Σ counts·table`; the bitonic network's exchange count equals `(m/4)·log2 m·(log2 m + 1)` and its run-length counts equal the direct count in all five cases. Three cases were rerun for a first coefficient of variation above 3% (`gather` 2^20 `uniform` → 0.38%, `count32` 2^28 `gauss_wide` → 1.53%, `bitonic` 2^24 `uniform` → 0.79%); none is flagged; the largest coefficient on a judged arm is 2.87% (`gather` 2^24 `sequential`). | Oblivious, bucket sort over the 2^32-row tables' lookups only (`bound.py`, no glue) | +217% | +123% | +44% | +6% |
| Oblivious, bitonic over those lookups only | +719% | +408% | +146% | +20% |
| Stated exception | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Operation counts behind the oblivious rows (E0): `N_big = 4.545×10^11` lookups per 16K-token proof, `log2 N` = 38.7, bucket oblivious sort 232 accesses per lookup (1,859 B, 925 ps at 2.01 TB/s; 462 ps if an access is one 4-byte touch), bitonic 385 compare-exchanges (6,154 B, 3,061 ps); `N_all = 2.62×10^12`, 248 accesses (985 ps) and 436 exchanges (3,469 ps) per lookup, against `c_ℓ` = 101 ps. The key stream of one proof is 1.8 TB (`N_big`) or 10.5 TB (`N_all`) against 640 GB of HBM per node, so a sort over the whole stream does not fit and a chunked variant costs more than these counts. The exception's risk is the bound of part (a).

## Analysis

**Against the criterion.** (i) On the three premise arms the all-miss `uniform` stream is the slowest distribution (`R_slow` = 1.000 for `count32` and `count64` at 2^28 and for `gather` at 2^24), under the 1.25 limit; (ii) `s(2^28)` = 0.919 for `count32`, above 0.5; (iii) `B(0.1%)` = 7.15 ≤ 8 and `B(3%)` = 2.51 ≤ 3 bits per proof under the pre-registered inputs; (iv) every coefficient of variation on those arms is below 3% after at most one rerun (none flagged). The pre-registered rule returns `validated`.

**What the probe adds beyond the rule.** The channel is real and large on a CPU: the counting loop's duration varies 12× between the extremes at 1 GiB of counters (3.475 against 0.283 ns per key) and 2.5× between two plausible activation distributions (`gauss_wide` against `gauss_narrow`, 1.940 against 0.778 ns per key), and the per-proof spread of the CPU reference backend would be hours. The bound is nonetheless small because on the production backend the whole secret-dependent cost, even charged at the HBM random-access ceiling for every one of the 2.6×10^12 lookups, is 14% of a pass that also runs a forward pass and streams 2.6×10^12 commitments at 94 ps each; the leak is the log of that 14% over the jitter. The unjudged rows carry the finding that matters for the envelope: at 2^20, where the counters fit the L2, the slowest stream is not `uniform` but `sorted` (2.35× `uniform`) and `all_equal` (1.42×), because consecutive increments of one address serialize on the store-to-load path at about 1.1–1.8 ns each while independent in-cache increments run at 0.27 ns. So for tables whose counters are cache-resident, and the `Exp` dense core's 8 MiB per GPU after sharding is one, the worst case is the repeated-address regime, which is the mechanism the issue predicts for GPU atomics ("contend on hot rows, the other way round"), not the miss regime. The pre-registered premise holds where it was pre-registered (HBM-resident counters, the dense-core table read, which has no dependency chain) and fails for cache-resident counters; the bound of part (a) covers the latter only through its deliberate overcharge (every `Exp` increment at the HBM rate), which bounds an L2-contended increment only while it costs less than 16 ps of GPU time, something only an E2 run can confirm (Threats).

**What leaks, and to whom.** P1's secret-dependent duration is the sum of the increments' miss costs: a function of the key multiset through its cache footprint, roughly the number of distinct 32 B sectors (eight counters each) a table's lookups touch, weighted by reuse distance. It is one scalar per proof about the dispersion of the lookup keys, the activations' accumulator values (`Requant`, `Silu`) and the softmax score differences (`Exp`) of the whole batch: "how spread the activations of this batch were", with at most about 140 distinguishable levels (7.2 bits) at a 0.1% jitter floor and about 6 levels (2.5 bits) at the 3% level RFC-0020 treats as ordinary noise, against a secret of some 10^11 bits of activations and 2.8×10^5 bits of tokens per 16K-token proof. It cannot be inverted to a token or an activation; a 16× change in the dispersion of the keys is visible at every jitter level, a few per cent is not. The recipient is a live verifier, who sees the latency before `Round1`; in non-interactive mode nobody observes P1. Per epoch, 681 proofs leak at most 681 × 7.2 bits about 681 different witnesses; a verifier who obtained all 681 proofs of one witness and averaged would learn at most 11.9 bits about it.

**The three options.** The exception costs nothing and carries the bound above (E0; 8.7 bits at 0.1% if the commit pass turns out as cheap as the `source` profile hopes). The envelope costs 2.7% of `μ(16K)` for the deadline at the all-miss case (4.4% if the typical case is free) plus a 2.8% safety margin at 3% jitter, about 5.5% in all, and its deadline cannot yet be set: the GPU's contended-atomic worst case, which the 2^20 rows show can exceed the miss case, is E2. Oblivious counting costs at least +44% of `μ(16K)` for the 2^32-row tables alone and +311% for every lookup with the cheapest oblivious sort in the literature, before the 1.8–10.5 TB key stream, which no node holds, is accounted for; the CPU anchor agrees (31–139× the direct count for the bitonic network, 4–12× for a non-oblivious sort). Oblivious counting is out by two orders of magnitude against the bits it would protect; the envelope buys about 7 bits per proof for about 5% of `μ` and needs an E2 measurement before its deadline can be stated; the exception is the only option whose cost and risk are both known today.

Threats to validity:

- **CPU against GPU, the main one.** The CPU's slow cases are dependency chains on one core; the GPU's are atomic serialization at the L2 and contention across SMs, measured on Volta at 36 cycles per uncontended global atomic rising to 76 at 32 threads on one address, with four contention scenarios in which the same-address case has the lowest throughput ([JMSS18], Table 4.2 and Figure 4.1); the GPU precedent for a timing channel through secret-indexed table accesses is [JFK16], which recovers an AES key from a kernel's execution time. Whether the contended case exceeds the all-miss case on an H100 as `sorted` exceeds `uniform` at 2^20 here, and by how much, is E2; the bound's overcharge (every `Exp` increment at the HBM rate) covers it only if a contended increment costs under 16 ps of GPU time on average. Production kernels privatize histograms (per-block shared-memory counters, warp aggregation), which changes both the cost and the channel; RFC-0018 'Kernels' does not yet say how the counters are updated.
- **The E0 inputs.** `Δ_max` assumes one 32 B sector per random increment at 60% of the HBM peak (E0 assumption; 40% and 100% bracket it at 7.7 and 6.4 bits); `D_P1` is the `counted` profile's forward pass plus commitments (the `source` profile's cheaper commitments raise the bound to 8.7 bits); the counting itself is excluded from `D_P1`, which only lowers the bound. The jitter floor 0.1% is 10–60× below the only measured GPU run-to-run variation found ([GKHDWBW25], 0.98–6.46% over 1,000 repetitions of a GEMM kernel on one GPU, hardware not split by generation), so the headline is an upper bound; at the measured jitter the leak is 2–4 bits per proof.
- **The translation of `t_typ/t_uniform`.** The envelope's typical-case ratio 0.391 is the CPU's; on a GPU the ratio between L2-resident and HBM-missing increments differs, so the envelope row is bracketed by `r = 0`.
- **Input distribution.** Seven synthetic streams stand in for activations; the two Gaussians are proxies for plausible witnesses, not measured activations (rule 6, no model data). Real keys also carry the class bits of tuple tables, which spread a Gaussian over several rows' ranges.
- **The counting loop is reproduced, not linked.** The worktree predates `instance.rs`; the loop is the quoted six lines over `u32` and `u64`, and the smoke run and the sort-based reference check its counts, but the production function's allocation (`vec![0u64; leaves]`, lazily faulted on first touch) adds a secret-dependent page-fault pattern on the CPU backend that this harness zeroes away before timing.
- **One machine, one day, one toolchain.** The in-cache floor (0.27 ns per key, about one increment per cycle) and the all-miss cost (3.5 ns per key at 1 GiB, with the page-table walks a 1 GiB array costs on 16 KiB pages) are this core's; another CPU moves both ends, though not the ordering the premise needs.

## Verdict

`validated`. No idea note changes; the result goes to issue #257: the leak is bounded at 7.2 bits per proof at a 0.1% jitter floor (2.5 bits at 3%; E0, `counted`), about 4,900 bits per epoch about 681 different witnesses, its content a per-table dispersion statistic of the activations; the envelope costs about 5.5% of `μ(16K)` (2.7% deadline, 2.8% margin; E0 from E1 ratios) and its deadline needs an E2 measurement; oblivious counting costs +44% of `μ(16K)` at the very least and +311% over every lookup (E0), with a key stream no node holds. Recommendation: the stated exception, recorded in 01 §6 and 06 with the statistic, the recipient and the bandwidth above, and RFC-0018 'Determinism' corrected (its premise "the verifier observes no timing" is false for a live verifier); the envelope is the fallback if the E2 run shows a GPU spread above the E0 ceiling, in its cheaper quantized form (Follow-ups).

## Follow-ups

- **E2 (RFC-0020 runner).** P1's counting kernel on one H100 over the seven streams of this harness at 2^24 and 2^32 counters (sharded as 08 §4 says), with Nsight's L2 hit rate, atomic throughput and DRAM bytes: the actual duration variance across key distributions, which settles (1) whether the contended-atomic case exceeds the all-miss case, as `sorted` and `all_equal` exceed `uniform` for cache-resident counters here, and so whether the envelope's deadline can be set from the HBM ceiling at all; (2) the GPU's own `Δ` and `t_typ/t_uniform`, which replace the CPU ratios in the bound and the envelope price; (3) the kernel design: privatized per-block counters and warp aggregation narrow both the cost and the channel, and RFC-0018 'Kernels' should say which is used. The pre-registered prediction from this report is a GPU spread at or below the E0 ceiling (14% of `D_P1` at 16K).
- **The specification change (issue #257).** INV-ARCH-2 in 01 §6 names multiplicity counting and table reads at secret keys as the data-dependent accesses; 06 records the leakage as an accepted risk: the statistic (per-table dispersion of the lookup keys through the counters' cache footprint), the recipient (a live verifier, through the latency before `Round1`), the bandwidth (≤ 7.2 bits per proof at a 0.1% jitter floor, ≤ 2.5 at 3%, E0; 681 × that per epoch about distinct witnesses), and the RFC-0020 measurement that will replace the E0 figures; RFC-0018 'Determinism' drops "the verifier observes no timing" and points to the risk row; RFC-0013's non-goal keeps pointing to INV-ARCH-2.
- **Quantized release, the cheap hardening.** Releasing `Round1` only at multiples of a public quantum `q` leaks at most one bit per quantum crossed ([AZM10] §2.2): at `q` = 5% of the predicted `D_P1` the bound falls from 7.2 to `log2(1 + 0.141/0.05)` = 1.9 bits per proof for an average delay of `q/2` = 2.5% of `D_P1`, about 0.8% of `μ(16K)`; it needs no worst-case bound, only a prediction, and misses cost one bit each. Price it against the E2 numbers before choosing it over the plain exception.
- **CPU backend hygiene.** `multiplicities` should allocate and zero its counters before the loop in a data-independent pass (or take a caller-provided buffer): the lazy first-touch page faults of `vec![0u64; leaves]` are a second secret-dependent cost on the CPU backend, unmeasured here. Low priority: the CPU backend is the test oracle, not the prover a live verifier times.
- **The 32 B sector and the 60% utilization** behind `c_rand` are assumptions; the E2 run's DRAM bytes per increment replace them.
