## Historical mechanism

Today ([RFC-0006 'Gadget selection'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0006-compiler-ir.md#gadget-selection), E0, primary): a `Requant` node with signed key width `b ≤ B_max = 32` lowers to `Requant1` (1 commitment, 1 lookup); `32 < b ≤ 62` lowers to `RequantSplit` with split point `s = b − 32` (2 commitments, 2 lookups: a `2^32`-row reduced table plus a `Range(s)` table and a committed low limb `z_lo`, [RFC-0009 `RequantSplit`](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0009-gadgets.md#requantsplit)). `B_max` is one global number, justified as "the largest table whose prover-side cost is amortized over a 16K-token batch". The RoPE rescale of Q and K ([RFC-0005 'Integer semantics'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0005-integer-model.md#integer-semantics): `(a, b) ↦ (a·C − b·S, a·S + b·C)` with 14-bit `C, S`, then `rq(·, r_q, −128, 127)`, key `< 2^44`) is a split for 16,384 + 1,024 = 17,408 elements per layer per token (SUR-0009 `[E0-this]` (a), E0, primary).

What changes:

1. **RFC-0006 'Gadget selection'.** `TableLimits.max_log2_rows` becomes a limit per table kind and use: default 32 everywhere, `Requant` for the post-RoPE Q and K rescale 34. Range analysis is unchanged; selection compares the instance's `log2 rows = b + log2 |classes|` against the limit of its kind, so the RoPE rescale lowers to `Requant1` when its rows fit `2^34` and to `RequantSplit` otherwise. Every other node sees the same rule as today. The limit is a public `QuantSpec` parameter in the claim list, so the verifier re-derives the same selection (INV-IR-5, SP-7).
2. **RFC-0008 'Table kinds'.** The RoPE `Requant { b, classes, r_q, −128, 127 }` instance has `|classes| · 2^b` rows, `2^34` at `b = 34` with one class. Structured evaluation is unchanged: the key is affine in the index and the value depends on the high bits only, so the verifier's `t̃(ρ)` costs `O(core)` = 256 values whatever `b` ([RFC-0008 'Structured evaluation'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#structured-evaluation), E0, primary). The row-count assumption is load-bearing: RFC-0005 `pot` mode allows up to 4 shift classes per tensor packed into the key as `(class, k)`, and `2^34` rows holds only if the RoPE rescale has a per-tensor shift (one class; RFC-0005 writes it as `r_q`) or the classes are folded by [IDEA-0023](IDEA-0023-dilation-shared-requant-tables.md)'s dilation within the 34-bit budget. With 2 classes the table is `2^35`, with 4 classes `2^36` (E0, primary: RFC-0008 'Table kinds' row count), and the decision table below prices each.
3. **08 §4.** The multiplicity-counter line becomes per table: `2^34 × 4 B = 64 GiB` for this instance, sharded over the 8 GPUs of the node (8 GiB each) against `2^32 × 4 B = 16 GiB` (2 GiB each) for every other wide table; the prover commits `2^34` multiplicities for this instance per proof ([RFC-0008 'Relation'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#relation)). One observation for the amendment, not specific to this idea: this instance receives `N = 17,408 × 126 × 16,384 = 3.59×10^10 > 2^32` lookups per 16K-token batch (E0, primary), and the padding rule adds the padding count to `m_0`, so a 4-byte counter can overflow for a concentrated key distribution (all-zero activations) even on an honest prover; the counter must be 8 bytes or checked, which [EXP-0010](../experiments/EXP-0010-multiplicity-timing-leak/README.md) measured at the same speed as 4 bytes on this CPU (`count64` against `count32`, E1, primary) but doubles the array to 128 GiB (16 GiB per GPU).
4. **Preconditions that bring the RoPE key to 34 bits** (each a separate idea in the compiler lens, none written yet): the weight-row `ℓ2` certificate on `W_q`, `W_k` (public per-tensor `2^k`, SUR-0009 seed 1) and the `‖y‖₂` relational fact from `RsqrtRow` (SUR-0009 seed 2, `[E0-this]` (d)) give 23–24-bit Q and K accumulators (E0, primary: SUR-0009 Question 2); the RoPE rotation adds the constant width plus one bit for the sum of two products, so 14-bit constants give a 38–39-bit key and 9-bit constants 33–34 bits (E0, primary: SUR-0009 candidate idea 3). Without the certificates the key stays at 44 bits and no table of any admissible size fits it.
5. **The decision rule, which is this idea's own content.** Widen a table from `2^32` to `2^{b'}` rows only when
   `(2^{b'} − 2^32) · p_row < N_split · (c_c + c_ℓ)`,
   where `p_row` is the measured table-side price per row (one committed multiplicity, one leaf `(−m_j, α − t_j)` with its share of the fraction-tree levels, one counter) and `N_split` is the number of split elements the widening removes per 16K-token batch. For the RoPE table `N_split = 3.59×10^10` (E0, primary), so the break-even price `p* = N_split / (2^{b'} − 2^32) · (c_c + c_ℓ)` is 8.4× at `b' = 33`, 2.8× at 34, 1.2× at 35, 0.56× at 36 and 0.13× at 38 (E0, primary: arithmetic in this note's scratchpad). Under the convention that a table row costs `c_c + c_ℓ` (IDEA-0023, the cost model's unit costs), `2^33`–`2^34` rows win by count and `2^36` or more lose. The rule applies to every table kind at once and is what OQ-0006-1 asked for; the cheapest experiment supplies `p_row`.

## Why it could work

- The RoPE split is the largest remaining split group once the norms are fixed: of RISK-2's 82,944 extra commitments and lookups per layer per token, the two norms are 32,768 (IDEA-0011 removes them with a relational fact), RoPE Q and K 17,408 (key `< 2^44`), and the two `minimal`-profile accumulators 32,768 (keys `< 2^58`–`2^60`, which no table can hold) (E0, primary: SUR-0009 `[E0-this]` (a), reconstructed from RFC-0005's tensor list). After IDEA-0011 the count is 187,392 per layer per token, `2.361×10^7` per token; after a RoPE fix 169,984, `2.142×10^7` (E0, primary).
- The table side is once per proof, and the lookup side is `N ≫ T`: the `2^34` table adds `2^34 − 2^32 = 1.29×10^10` rows per 16K-token batch against `3.59×10^10` split elements removed, a 2.8× margin by count (E0, primary: this note). Widening all ten wide tables, SUR-0002's seed, adds about `1.7×10^11` rows, 37% of linear lookups per batch, and loses (E0, primary: SUR-0002 Question 3); the per-table rule keeps the win and drops the loss.
- [YZL26] (E1, primary: DeepProve on a 32-thread CPU, 16 tokens) is the only measurement of a LogUp-GKR prover's table-side cost: B8 → B16 tables leave proving time unchanged at a fixed 0.823 M lookups (102.7 against 104.8 s) and B24 costs 2.6× (265.5 s), i.e. the table term is invisible while `T ≪ N` and dominant when `T ≈ N`. Here `T/N = 2^34 / 2^35.1 = 0.48` at 16K, inside the region where the table term is visible, which is why the per-row price must be measured rather than assumed. [Jo26] (E0, primary) states the same structurally: structure moves only a once-per-proof table term, and the table term is one of the three open levers.
- RFC-0006 fixes `B_max = 32` by an amortization argument, not by a measurement, and OQ-0006-1 is open with 34 as a named option (E0, primary: RFC-0006 text). The trade differs per table by three orders of magnitude: the `Exp` table has `2^22` rows and nothing to gain; the RoPE split costs `3.59×10^10` lookups and commitments per batch (E0, primary).
- The verifier's cost does not depend on the row count (RFC-0008 'Structured evaluation', E0, primary), so the widening is prover-side only.
- The weakest input: the fidelity of 9–10-bit RoPE constants is unmeasured anywhere (SUR-0009 Method: one search found only FPGA quarter-wave tables without an error analysis; `claim` level that it passes RFC-0005's gates).

## Kill criterion

Any one of:

1. The E1 calibration measures the table-side cost per row above 2.8 × (`c_c + c_ℓ`) at `T ≈ N` (the break-even of Mechanism 5 for `2^34 − 2^32` rows against `3.59×10^10` split elements; E0 arithmetic, correcting the 0.5× figure of the generation pass, which is the break-even at `2^36` rows). Then the widening loses at every unit-cost profile.
2. The RoPE key stays above 34 bits of rows after the certificates: no 9–10-bit RoPE constants pass RFC-0005's fidelity gates (ratio ≤ 1.03), the certificates give fewer than 3 bits on the Q and K accumulators, or the RoPE rescale has 2 or more shift classes that dilation cannot fold into 34 bits (then the table needs `2^35`–`2^38` rows, a loss by count or inside the price uncertainty).
3. 08 §4's per-table counter line (`≤ 2^32 × 4 B`) is firm for the 8-GPU node, or the 8-byte counters this instance needs (Mechanism 3) do not fit the 24 GB working set beside the other tables' counters.
4. The v0.2 commitment report measures a RoPE split count far below 17,408 per layer per token (the gain is proportional to it).

## Cheapest experiment

**Hypothesis.** In Tessera's combined fraction tree built on `tessera-sumcheck`'s kernels, the cost per lookup is flat within 5% for `T ≤ N/64` and rises with `T/N` above it (the [YZL26] insensitivity curve for Tessera's kernel shape), and the cost per table row at `T ≈ N` lies below 2.8 × the measured cost per lookup-side leaf plus one committed element (so kill criterion 1 does not fire at the counted unit costs).

**Method** (E1 here, this CPU; `s` for the harness, since EXP-0006's `leafcost` already builds the fraction-tree proxy): extend [EXP-0006](../experiments/EXP-0006-product-leaf-cost/README.md)'s fraction-tree proxy with a table side, leaves `(−m_j, α − t_j)` with seeded multiplicities summing to `N` (uniform, Zipf and all-equal distributions, as EXP-0010 seeds them), the multiplicity count over a `T`-entry counter array, and the table leaves joining the lookup leaves in one combined tree; `T ∈ {2^8, 2^12, 2^16, 2^20, 2^22, 2^24}` at `N = 2^22` and `N = 2^20` lookups; `tv-core` as the oracle for the tree's root (`n = 0`, `d ≠ 0`); RFC-0020's protocol (3 warm-ups, 10 iterations, median, coefficient of variation ≤ 3% or one rerun). Report ns per lookup against `T` and ns per table row at `T ≈ N`, translated to the H100 interval by EXP-0006's method (operation and byte counts at 60% of the int32 peak and 80% of HBM, [VYTA25]). Pre-registered criterion: `validated` if the curve is flat within 5% for `T ≤ N/64` and `p_row / (c_c + c_ℓ) < 2.8` at `T ≈ N` on the counted interval; `refuted` if `p_row ≥ 2.8 × (c_c + c_ℓ)` at the interval's low end. Plus, at E0 in the same report: the decision table above for every table kind at `b' ∈ {33, 34, 35, 36}` with the measured `p_row`, and the RoPE key-width arithmetic after the certificates (SUR-0009 `[E0-this]` (d)) for 9-, 10- and 14-bit constants.

**Reachable here.** E1 for the per-row price and the insensitivity curve; E0 for the decision and the key width. The 9–10-bit RoPE fidelity measurement needs `tessera-quant`'s harness and a checkpoint (research rule 6 forbids real weights here; synthetic weights do not measure fidelity), so it waits for the quantizer (RFC-0005 rollout). The shift-class count of the RoPE rescale waits for the v0.2 `TableSetManifest`.

**Effort.** `s`–`m` for the experiment (a few days on EXP-0006's harness plus the pre-registration); the RFC amendment to RFC-0006, RFC-0008 and 08 §4 is `m` in size but touches tier-A table definitions (`l` by the rubric's letter; no protocol version is frozen before v0.3). What stays unknown until an H100 (E2): the actual per-row price under the GPU's counting and hashing kernels, the 64–128 GiB counter array's tile alignment and shard traffic, and whether the envelope cost of EXP-0010 grows as the E0 observation predicts.
