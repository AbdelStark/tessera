## Mechanism

Setting. After EXP-0008 the level kernel of the `SoftmaxRow` forest (and, in IDEA-0018's form, of the fraction tree) computes each round's two node sums as a two-level sum over the pairs `x = x_hi · 2^lo + x_lo`: an inner sum over `x_lo` (one `x_hi` bucket, `2^lo` pairs with `lo = ⌈m/2⌉` for `m` free variables, at most 2^10 pairs at `m = 20`) of `eq_lo[x_lo] · V_L(X, x)·V_R(X, x)`, then the bucket result scaled by `eq_hi[x_hi]` and added into the round's accumulator (EXP-0008 README 'Method', step 2). Today every product in the inner sum is reduced: the node product `p = V_L·V_R` and then the scaling `eq_lo[x_lo] · p` each pay `reduce128` (`goldilocks.rs`: `mul` is `reduce128(a as u128 * b as u128)`, read 2026-10-17; `Fp2 × Fp2` is Karatsuba with three such products and a multiply-by-7, RFC-0002 'Quadratic extension'). The inner sum is a dot product of `eq_lo` with the vector of node products, and a dot product needs only one reduction.

Five prover-only changes, none of which alters a message:

1. **Deferred scaling at levels ≥ 2 (`Fp2` tables).** Per node and per `x_hi` bucket: compute `p = V_L(X)·V_R(X)` reduced, as today (Karatsuba `Fp2 × Fp2`, 3 `Fp` products). Then form the three Karatsuba partial products of `eq_lo[x_lo] · p`, with `e = eq_lo[x_lo] = (e0, e1)` and `p = (p0, p1)`: `e0·p0`, `e1·p1` (64×64 → 128 bits) and `(e0 + e1)·(p0 + p1)` (the sums unreduced, below 2^65, so the product is below 2^130). Add each into its own 192-bit accumulator (three `u64` words with carries), without `reduce128`. After the bucket, reduce each of the three accumulators once (`reduce128` of the low 128 bits plus the top word times `2^128 mod P`, or two chained `reduce128`), recombine the Karatsuba terms (`c0 = A00 + 7·A11`, `c1 = A_sum − A00 − A11`), and scale the bucket's `Fp2` result by `eq_hi[x_hi]` as today. The accumulator per node per bucket is 3 × 3 = 9 `u64` words, 18 for the two nodes, against 4 words of two `Fp2` accumulators today.
2. **Deferred scaling at the leaf level (`Fp` tables, round 1).** There `p = V_L·V_R` is one reduced `Fp` product and the scaling is `mul_by_base`: the two 64×64-bit products `e0·p`, `e1·p` accumulate unreduced into two 192-bit accumulators per node per bucket; one reduction each per bucket. IDEA-0022's integer window already removes the field multiplications of rounds 1–`v` of the leaf level where the values are small integers; this note applies to the leaf level's rounds after that window, and to every round of levels ≥ 2, so the two compose on disjoint products.
3. **Overflow rule, pinned.** Products below 2^130, buckets of at most 2^lo ≤ 2^10 pairs at `n = 21` (2^11 with a wider split): the sum is below 2^141 < 2^192, 51 bits of slack. `MAX_BUCKET_LOG` is a named constant with a `debug_assert!` on the bucket size at kernel entry and a test at the maximal bucket size; the reduction cadence is the bucket, never a count of products.
4. **Bit-identical messages.** The once-reduced sum of unreduced products equals the sum of reduced products modulo `P`; the recombination is the same Karatsuba identity. The round messages `p_j(0), p_j(2), p_j(3)` (and the fraction tree's) are therefore the same `Fp2` values the specified prover emits, which the experiment checks byte for byte against `prove_forests` and with `tv-core` acceptance, as EXP-0008 did.
5. **Same change, three kernels.** The fraction tree's split-`eq` kernel (IDEA-0018's one-relation form: the `eq_lo` scaling of the per-pair combined relation at two nodes, `count.py` rules `fraction_leaf_round`, `fraction_generic_round`), the `SoftmaxRow` forest's level kernel (item 1–2) and `prove_batch`'s generic `eq × product` path once it is restructured to split `eq`. On the GPU the 192-bit accumulate is `IADD3` with carry chains, 4–6 int32 operations per 128-bit product, and the per-bucket reduction is amortized over the bucket's pairs.

## Why it could work

- **The kernel is dot-product-shaped, and its count charges every reduction (E0, primary).** EXP-0008's `count.py` (read 2026-10-17): the candidate's rules are `split_round1 = 2 Fp mul + 2 mul_by_base + 2 Fp2 add` per pair and `split_round_ext = 4 Fp2 mul + 4 Fp2 add + 2 Fp2 sub` per pair, two of the four `Fp2` multiplications being the `eq_lo` scalings; `FIELD_OPS["mul"] = (18, 26)` int32 operations at the lazy and as-written ends, so no column of the count already defers a product's reduction. Per leaf of the whole tree the candidate is 15.04 `Fp`-multiplication equivalents (`eq` tables 0.02, round 1 3.0, its folds 2.0, `Fp2` rounds 6.0, their folds 3.0, build 1.0; README table) and 419 int32 operations (lazy); the `eq_lo` scalings are 2.0 of round 1's 3.0 (one `mul_by_base` per leaf = 2 `Fp` products) and 3.0 of the `Fp2` rounds' 6.0 (one `Fp2` multiplication per leaf = 3 `Fp` products): 5 `Fp` products per leaf, one third of the leaf's products. This is the check SUR-0011 asked for before writing the idea.
- **What a deferred reduction saves on the int32 pipe (E0, own, after EXP-0006's translation).** SUR-0011 reads the 18-operation Goldilocks multiplication as 8 for the 64×64 product and 10 for `reduce128`; a 192-bit add with carry is 4–6 int32 operations (three 64-bit adds as six 32-bit adds with carry, or two `IADD3`s plus carry handling). Deferring 5 products per leaf saves 50 and adds 20–30: 20–30 of 419 int32 operations, −5% to −7% of the forest kernel (E0). The per-bucket reductions (6 per node pair per bucket of 2^10 pairs at the top level, more at small levels, geometric over levels) add below 0.1 operation per leaf.
- **The CPU ratio is larger, so an E1 here is a sign test, not the GPU figure (E0).** On the M4's 64-bit ALU `reduce128` is about 5–6 instructions (`goldilocks.rs`: two wrapping operations with mask corrections, one 32×32 multiply, one canonicalization) and a 192-bit accumulate is 3 (`adds`, `adcs`, `adc`), so the saving is 2–3 of about 8 instructions per deferred product: about 2.25 of 15.04 equivalents per leaf (−15%, E0, rough). The E1 ratio therefore overstates the H100 ratio by about 2×; the experiment reports both.
- **The fraction kernel has the same shape with a smaller share (E0, primary).** `count.py`: `fraction_leaf_round = 6 Fp2 mul` per pair plus 2 binds (8 `Fp2` multiplications per pair-round), of which the two `eq_lo` scalings are 2 (25%); `fraction_generic_round = 10 Fp2 mul` plus 4 binds (14), scalings 2 (14%). A deferred `Fp2` multiplication saves about 30 int32 operations and adds 12–18 (three accumulators), 12–18 of the about 82 an `Fp2` multiplication costs (E0): about −4% of the leaf-level kernel and −2.5% of the generic level. The per-layer sumcheck rounds are about 12.5 of the 18.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents of one lookup ([E0-this], SUR-0002 row: 10 generic, 2.5 for a numerators-1 first layer, of 18.5), so `c_ℓ` moves by about −2% to −3% (own derivation) or up to −5% if the whole 68% round share saw the leaf-level ratio (the generation pass's figure, kept as the headline row below and marked optimistic).
- **Lazy reduction is standard where the headroom exists (E1, primary, other fields and CPUs).** [ZGLCZ26] (SUR-0011 row): a 49-bit prime chosen for IFMA's 52-bit multiplier so that lazy reduction enables fused fold-and-evaluate kernels, 21× single-thread end to end over a Goldilocks baseline (E1, primary, CPU; the analogue for Goldilocks is 128-bit accumulation, SUR-0011's reading, E0). [KDHR26] (TCHES 2026, SUR-0005): lazy reduction is routine in high-performance encoder kernels (E1, primary, AVX-512). [DDBDT26] §3 (SUR-0007 row): delayed reduction in sumcheck round sums, 1.37× alone on a degree-2 sumcheck over BN254 on an M4 Max (E1, primary, another field). None of these measured Goldilocks on an H100; the ratio here is E0 until EXP-0008's harness runs the arm.
- **The grant exists in the specification (primary).** RFC-0002 'Representation and reduction': "GPU kernels may keep values in `[0, 2^64)` between operations and must canonicalize before writing to buffers that are hashed, encoded or copied to the host." A 192-bit accumulator inside one kernel, canonicalized at the bucket, is that grant one word wider and leaves no non-canonical value in any buffer.

Keys [ZGLCZ26], [KDHR26], [DDBDT26] and [E0-this] are in [bibliography.md](../bibliography.md).

## Security gate

| Property | Effect | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Soundness bits (G-4, RFC-0014) | None. The round polynomials are the same polynomials RFC-0010 'Tree levels' step 2 and RFC-0008 'Fraction tree' define; the kernel changes the order of reductions inside one sum, and `Σ reduce(a_i·b_i) ≡ reduce(Σ a_i·b_i) (mod P)` exactly when the unreduced sum does not overflow, which the bound 2^141 < 2^192 guarantees with 51 bits of slack. Every message is absorbed before the challenge that depends on it, as today (INV-FS-1..4); no challenge, label, degree, point or verifier check moves. | Uniqueness upstream of the logits (SP-3, RFC-0009 Lemma 0) | None. No committed value, relation, table, range or prover-declared quantity is added; RFC-0009's relations and Lemma 0 are untouched. ## Prior rejection check

- [RFC-0002 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0002-field-arithmetic.md#alternatives-considered) item 4 rejects **Montgomery representation for the prover** because the special-form `reduce128` is faster and keeps values in natural form. This note keeps the special-form reduction and the natural form and only defers the reduction, which RFC-0002 'Representation and reduction' already permits between operations on the GPU. Not covered. Items 1–3 and 5 (smaller or larger fields, challenge extensions) are unrelated.
- [baseline.md §7](../baseline.md#7-already-rejected): the RFC-0002 row restates item 4 with "—" under "Reopens if"; no row covers the prover's reduction schedule or evaluation order.
- Overlap, not rejection, inside the knowledge base: [IDEA-0015](IDEA-0015-lazy-radix-encoder-ntt.md) mechanism 2 defers reduction inside NTT butterflies (the encoder, `c_c`); [IDEA-0010](IDEA-0010-shared-point-forest-fusion.md) mechanism 2 and [IDEA-0022](IDEA-0022-integer-leaf-level-round-window.md) use `u64`/`i128` small-integer arithmetic in the forest's first rounds where values are below 2^29, with one reduction per block; [SUR-0011](../surveys/SUR-0011-field.md) withheld this seed as an input to SUR-0007's [DDBDT26] §3 seed pending the dot-product-shape check. None of them defers a full-field product at every round and level of both trees.
- New now: EXP-0008's validated split-`eq` kernel makes every level's node sum an explicit dot product with a natural reduction point (the `x_hi` bucket), its `count.py` shows the reduction is charged on every product (one third of the leaf's products are `eq_lo` scalings), and the int32 and 64-bit translations give the first count of what deferral is worth on each pipe (−5% to −7% of the forest kernel on the H100's int32 pipe against about −15% on the M4). None, checked.

## Kill criterion

An E1 `deferred` arm in EXP-0008's `spliteq` harness on the M4 Max (the same three 2^22-leaf forest shapes and two fraction-proxy levels, bit-identical messages, `tv-core` acceptance) is within noise of or slower than the validated candidate on every shape: then the compiler already hides `reduce128`'s latency behind the loads of the two-level sum or the 192-bit carry chain costs what the reduction saved, and the H100 translation (which expects a 2× smaller ratio) has nothing to translate. Two further kills, each halving rather than refuting: (2) the int32 count of a 192-bit accumulate on Hopper exceeds 8 operations per product (then the GPU saving is at most 2 operations per product, under 2% of the kernel; E2 or a PTX/SASS count, E0); (3) the production port of IDEA-0021 lands with the accumulate already deferred (then this note is an implementation note of that port), or the RFC-0020 v0.1 gate measures the level kernel memory-bound on the H100 (then arithmetic savings do not show).

## Cheapest experiment

Hypothesis: on one Apple M4 Max performance core, the split-`eq` level kernel with the `eq_lo` scalings accumulated unreduced in 192-bit accumulators and reduced once per `x_hi` bucket emits messages byte-identical to EXP-0008's validated candidate and to `prove_forests`, is accepted by `tv-core`, and is at least 5% faster per leaf on each of the three 2^22-leaf forest shapes and at least 3% faster per entry on the two fraction-proxy levels (E1), with the ratio reported beside the E0 int32 count so the H100 figure can be read off the count, not the clock.

Method: add a `deferred` arm to [EXP-0008](../experiments/EXP-0008-split-eq-gruen-level-kernel/README.md)'s `spliteq` workspace (rule 2: own `[workspace]`, by-path dependencies on `tessera-field`, `tessera-poly`, `tessera-sumcheck`, `tv-core` as the acceptance driver; the 192-bit accumulator written in the harness in safe Rust with `overflowing_add`/`carrying_add`, no change to `tessera-field`'s public API, no `unsafe`), pin `MAX_BUCKET_LOG` with a debug assertion and a maximal-bucket test, run the three forest shapes and the two fraction-proxy levels under the RFC-0020 protocol as EXP-0008 did (seeded SplitMix64 inputs, 3 warm-ups, 10 measured iterations, iterations of at least 20 ms, median and CV ≤ 3%, baseline and candidate in the same harness, rule 4), require bit-identical proofs and transcripts and `tv-core` acceptance, and report `σ = 1 − T_deferred / T_candidate` per shape. Extend `count.py` with a `reduce128`-deferred column (multiplication 8 + 4–6 for the deferred products) for the H100 interval (E0). Pre-registered verdict: `validated` iff `σ ≥ 5%` on each forest shape and `≥ 3%` on both fraction levels; `refuted` iff `σ < 2%` everywhere; `inconclusive` between. Effort `s` (days: one accumulator type, two kernel variants, one count column; no RFC). Reaches E1 for the sign and the CPU ratio, E0 for the H100 figure. What stays unknown until an H100 run: the int32 cost of the 192-bit accumulate on Hopper (kill 2), whether the level kernel is compute-bound there (kill 3), and the true `c_π` and `c_ℓ` (RFC-0020 gate).
