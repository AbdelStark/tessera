## Mechanism

Setting. The leaf level `h` of the `SoftmaxRow` forest holds the keys `k_qi = c_q − x_qi`, true integers in `[0, 2^22)` (scores `|x_qi| < 2^21`, [RFC-0009 'SoftmaxRow'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0009-gadgets.md#softmaxrow)), in the prime subfield: `forest.rs` builds every level as `Halves<F::Base>` (verified in `crates/tessera-sumcheck/src/forest.rs`). The level's sumcheck is `Σ_x eq(r, x)·Ṽ_h(0 ‖ x)·Ṽ_h(1 ‖ x)` of degree 3, the pair bit being the lowest variable and the forest's tree index the highest (RFC-0010 'Tree levels', 'Layout'); round `j` sends `p_j(0), p_j(2), p_j(3)` under `tree/{ℓ}/round/{j − 1}` (RFC-0010 'Sumcheck messages'). Today the kernel evaluates each round at its nodes by the line rule and folds every table after every round (`kernel.rs`, `prove_batch`), the first round over `Fp` tables and the rest over `Fp2` after `bind_lowest` lifts the folded tables.

Four prover-only changes, none of which alters a message:

1. **Grid.** Fix a window `v` (`v = 4` as the headline, `3` and `5` in the experiment). Write a hypercube index of the level's `N/2` pairs as `(x_lo, x_hi)` with `x_lo` the `v` variables bound in rounds 1–`v` and `x_hi` the remaining `n − v`. A block is one value of `x_hi`: `2^v` consecutive pairs (`2^{v+1}` consecutive entries of the level, since the pair bit is lowest). For each block form the two multilinear extensions `V_L(z, x_hi) = Ṽ_h(0 ‖ z ‖ x_hi)` and `V_R(z, x_hi) = Ṽ_h(1 ‖ z ‖ x_hi)` at every `z ∈ {0, 1, 2}^v` by the engine's line rule coordinate by coordinate (`V(z_k + 1) = V(z_k) + Δ_k`, the rule `kernel.rs` already uses for one variable), and accumulate the grid `G[z] = Σ_{x_hi} w(x_hi) · V_L(z, x_hi)·V_R(z, x_hi)`, `3^v` entries per level instance. The weight `w(x_hi)` is the `eq` factor over `x_hi` alone: `eq(r_hi, x_hi)` as a table (or the two `√N` tables of the split-`eq` kernel when that idea lands). The `eq` factor over the window's own variables is not in the grid: by decomposability ([DDBDT26] Lemma 7) it is applied as public weights when each message is formed (item 3). The grid is accumulated in one pass over the level's leaves, in memory or, when the level is streamed, inside the regeneration pass of RFC-0018 'Leaf levels'.
2. **Small-value arithmetic** ([DDBDT26] §3). Along each window coordinate the line rule at `z_k ∈ {0, 1, 2}` has weights `(1 − z_k, z_k)` with `|1 − z_k| + |z_k| ≤ 3`, so `|V(z, x_hi)| ≤ 2^22 · 3^v < 2^29` at `v = 4` (E0, own bound). Every `V_L·V_R` is then a signed integer below `2^58` in magnitude: one `i64` product, no reduction. The scaling by the `Fp2` weight `w(x_hi)` (two components below `2^64`) is two `64 × 58`-bit products below `2^122` per component, accumulated in `i128` with one Goldilocks reduction per grid entry per `2^5` blocks (`2^5 · 2^122 = 2^127`). Per pair this is `(3/2)^v` small×small products and `(3/2)^v` small×big scalings (the `3^v` grid over `2^v` pairs), as SUR-0007 counts (E0). The `Fp` tables of the level are never touched by a field multiplication during the window.
3. **Messages.** Round `j ∈ [1, v]` of the level is computed only after the challenges `r_1 … r_{j−1}` have been squeezed (INV-FS order preserved): `p_j(X) = eq(ρ_j, X) · Σ_{z_{>j} ∈ {0,1}^{v−j}} [Π_{k > j} eq(ρ_k, z_k)] · G_{r_1 … r_{j−1}}[X, z_{>j}]`, where `G_{r_1 … r_{j−1}}` is the grid with its first `j − 1` coordinates collapsed by the degree-2 Lagrange weights of the challenges on `{0, 1, 2}` (each collapse is at most `3^{v−k}` `Fp2` multiplications, negligible), `ρ_k` are the window coordinates of the level's `eq` point, and the public linear factor `eq(ρ_j, X)` turns the degree-2 values at `X ∈ {0, 1, 2}` into the degree-3 message, extrapolated to `X = 3`. The message is then `p_j(0), p_j(2), p_j(3)` as today. After round `v` the level binds its `Fp` tables to `r_1 … r_v` once (one pass, lifting to `Fp2` as `bind_lowest` does today after round 1), and the per-round prover of `prove_batch` finishes the level's remaining `n − v` rounds unchanged. The verifier does what RFC-0010 step 3 says; it cannot tell which prover produced the transcript.
4. **Level `h − 1`.** Its values are products of two keys, below `2^44`; `|V(z)| < 2^44 · 3^v < 2^51` and `V_L·V_R < 2^102` fit `i128` unreduced, but the `Fp2` scaling does not without a third limb, so level `h − 1` takes the grid with `i128` small×small products and one reduction per product before scaling (saving the per-node reductions only). From level `h − 2` the values are full field elements and the generic kernel runs. The headline below counts the leaf level alone.

Count per pair of the leaf level over rounds 1–4 (E0, own, after SUR-0007's count and EXP-0006's int32 translation of 18 per `Fp` multiplication and 4 per addition): `1.5^4 ≈ 5` small×small `u64` products (about 2 int32 operations each on the H100's `IMAD` path: 10), 5 small×big scalings with delayed reduction (about 8 each: 40), `2 · 1.5^4 ≈ 10` `i128` accumulations (two 64-bit adds with carry each, about 8 int32 operations: 80), and the block's amortized reductions (`3^4 / (2^5 · 2^4) ≈ 0.16` per pair, about 3), about **135** int32 operations; against about **500** for the per-round prover's rounds 1–4 and their folds on the same pairs (EXP-0006 `count.py`: round 1 at 4.5 and its folds at 3.5 `Fp`-multiplication equivalents per leaf over all levels, `Fp2` rounds at 9.0 and folds at 4.5, of which the leaf level's rounds 2–4 take `(1/2)(1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8)` of the `Fp2` share; 18 int32 operations per equivalent). The `i128` accumulations are what caps the saving: they are 60% of the window's work.

## Why it could work

- **The primary source measures the mechanism (E1, primary, Apple M4 Max, BN254).** [DDBDT26] Table 4 and §8: delayed reduction alone 1.37× on a degree-2 sumcheck, round batching 4.10–6.01× on Spartan's first sumcheck in Jolt, 10–24× on linear combinations of small values, and the window rule `v* = log_{d+1}(d² κ)` with `κ` the big/small cost ratio (SUR-0007 source row). For `d = 2` and `κ = 10–29` the rule gives `v* = log_3(40–116) = 3.4–4.3`, which is why `v = 4` is the headline and `v = 3` the fallback.
- **`κ` is in the integer regime here, not the extension regime (E0, primary bounds).** Over Goldilocks the `Fp2/Fp` cost ratio is about 3 (SUR-0007 synthesis 2), where [BDT24]'s extension-field-only version buys below 1.3× (E0, SUR-0007's extrapolation below [BDT24] Table 1, which is why the integer leaves, not the field extension, carry this idea). When the small operands are integers below `2^29` multiplied as plain `u64` with one reduction per block, SUR-0007 puts `κ ≈ 10–29` (E0). The leaf level is exactly that case: RFC-0009 fixes the keys as true integers below `2^22` (primary), and `forest.rs` holds them in the prime subfield (verified).
- **The target is 40% of the forest and already measured (E1, primary, this machine).** EXP-0006's fit (predicted 31.1 ns per leaf against 31.2–31.7 measured on one M4 Max core, every forest shape within 2%) splits the forest into round 1 (15%) and its folds (12%), `Fp2` rounds (40%) and folds (20%), `eq` tables (12%), level build (2%). The leaf level is half of every per-level component (levels halve), and its rounds 2–4 take `1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 = 7/8` of its `Fp2` round and fold work, so the leaf level's rounds 1–4 and folds are `(4.6 + 3.8)/2 + (12.4 + 6.1)/2 × 7/8 ≈ 12.3` ns of 31.1 (40%, E0 over E1 components).
- **The integer path exists and pays in the engine already (E1, primary, code comment).** `kernel.rs` reports the zipped prime-subfield loop at 20 to 45% faster than the row loop on one M4 Max core (verified, module comment); EXP-0006 prices round 1 plus its folds at 27% of the forest. The window extends the base-field regime from one round to four and removes the per-node reductions the zipped loop still pays.
- **A small-field implementation exists (secondary, no benchmarks).** [Lambda26] implements [DDBDT26] Algorithm 6 over `BabyBear⁴` with `ℓ_0 = 3` small-value rounds then Gruen's `eq` handling, the window the `v*` rule predicts at `κ = 16`; it is the only 31-bit-field implementation SUR-0007 found, so the algorithm is known to transfer off BN254 at least once.

Key [DDBDT26] is in [bibliography.md](../bibliography.md); [BDT24] and [Lambda26] are defined in the source rows of [SUR-0007](../surveys/SUR-0007-sumcheck.md), and their bibliography lines are merged by the consolidation agent.

## Expected gain

Headline: the window saves about 73% of the leaf level's rounds 1–4 and folds (135 against 500 int32 operations per pair, E0), which are 40% of the forest (E0 over E1), so about −29% of `c_π`; rounded down to −27% for the grid's own passes (one pass to accumulate, one bind) and the `i128` emulation on the int32 pipe: `c_π` 73 → 53 ps under the `counted` profile. Floor: if the `i128` accumulations cost what EXP-0006 prices an `Fp2` addition at (and `v = 3` therefore beats `v = 4`), the saving is nearer −20% (73 → 58). Combined with the split-`eq` Gruen kernel of this sweep (SUR-0007: a quarter to a third of the level's multiplications, E0), the two savings on different components of the same kernel add to about 73 → 35, the figure the generation pass quoted; it is reported for the composition, not claimed by this note.

## Security gate

| Property | Effect | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Soundness bits (G-4, RFC-0014) | None. The round polynomials `p_j` are the same polynomials RFC-0010 'Tree levels' step 2 defines, emitted round by round, each after the challenge it depends on has been squeezed (INV-FS-1..4): the grid is a function of the level's tables and the level's `eq` point, both fixed before round 1, and of no future challenge. Integer accumulation is exact (every operand and partial sum is within `i64`/`i128` by the bounds above), so the messages are bit-identical to the per-round prover's; an overflow would make an honest message fail `p_j(0) + p_j(1) = claim` and could never help ADV-P. The verifier's checks, degree, points and `eq(r_i, r'_i)` factor are unchanged. | pass |
| Uniqueness upstream of the logits (SP-3, RFC-0009 Lemma 0) | None. No committed value, relation, table or prover-declared range is added; RFC-0009's `SoftmaxRow` relations and Lemma 5 are untouched. The overflow bounds are completeness obligations on the prover (checked by the experiment's `tv-core` driver), not soundness exposures. | pass |
| Zero knowledge (G-5, RFC-0013) | None. The per-stage masked sumcheck's masking polynomial is an additive term computed apart from the level's sum (RFC-0013 mechanism 1); the window changes how the unmasked sum is computed, not what is sent. Secret-class keys and grid values stay inside the kernel (05 §3, INV-ARCH-6); OQ-0013-2 stands as before. | pass |
| Verifier independence (SP-6) | None. No verifier code changes; `tv-core`'s `verify_product_level` keeps checking RFC-0010 from the specification. | pass |
| Post-quantum (hash-based commitments) | n/a. No commitment or hash changes. | pass |

Timing: the block structure, `v` and the reduction cadence depend on shapes and variable counts only, never on a key's value (INV-ARCH-2 holds).

## Prior rejection check

- [RFC-0018 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0018-gpu-prover-runtime.md#alternatives-considered) item 3 rejects **multi-variable sumcheck rounds** for the `(d + 1)^w` growth of message size and prover work. The window changes neither the messages nor the round count: the verifier receives `n` rounds of three `Fp2` values each; the `3^v` grid is prover memory, 81 `Fp2` values per level instance at `v = 4`. Not covered.
- [RFC-0010 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0010-proof-schedule.md#alternatives-considered) (layer-by-layer GKR, committing intermediates, independent per-layer challenges, one monolithic sumcheck, full coefficients) do not address the prover's evaluation order. Not covered.
- [baseline.md §7](../baseline.md#7-already-rejected): the RFC-0009 row rejects cheaper *certificates* and reopens on "a certificate cheaper than one product leaf per score"; this idea keeps the certificate and lowers the leaf's cost. No row covers prover-side evaluation order or delayed reduction.
- Overlap, not rejection: [IDEA-0010](IDEA-0010-shared-point-forest-fusion.md) mechanism 2 is the `v = 1` case (integer round 1 in `u128`, lift at the first bind; `triaged`, 0.87), [IDEA-0005](IDEA-0005-base-field-leaf-small-value.md) applies small-value first rounds to the fraction tree (`c_ℓ`, `triaged`, 0.32), and SUR-0004's base-field-leaf seed is the observation that the leaf level is integer, which `tessera-sumcheck` already implements. SUR-0007's "Round-batched streamed windows" seed uses the same grid for the pass count (`stream`); the two claim different terms and should share one experiment harness.
- New now: the `3^v` grid that keeps rounds 2–4 in the integer domain before any bind (IDEA-0010 lifts after round 1), Lemma 7's `eq` decomposition that makes the window compatible with a split `eq` table, a primary source with E1 numbers ([DDBDT26], read in full by SUR-0007; SUR-0003 had the abstract) and the `v*` rule that fixes the window size from `κ`, and EXP-0006's component split that locates 40% of `c_π` in the window's target.

## Kill criterion

The E1 kernel pair on the M4 Max (grid window for `v ∈ {3, 4, 5}` against `prove_batch` for rounds 1–`v` of the leaf level at 2^21 pairs, byte-identical messages) is less than 15% faster for the level as a whole: then the `i128` accumulations and the small×big scalings ate the saving, as SUR-0007's first killer predicts, and the idea collapses into IDEA-0010 mechanism 2. Two further kills, each halving the idea rather than refuting it: (2) RFC-0018's tile order for regenerated leaves cannot deliver `2^v`-pair blocks of consecutive hypercube indices with the pair bit lowest (a layout fact of the leaf stage to read; if unresolved the window applies to in-memory levels only); (3) the RFC-0020 v0.1 gate shows the leaf-level kernel HBM-bound on the H100, where EXP-0006's byte model says compute-bound ("memory never binds"; E2 decides), in which case fewer operations buy nothing.

## Cheapest experiment

Hypothesis: on one Apple M4 Max performance core, a `3^v` grid window over the forest's leaf level with `i64`/`i128` small-value arithmetic and Lemma 7's `eq` decomposition emits rounds 1–`v` with transcript state byte-identical to `prove_forests`, is accepted by `tv_core::tree::verify_product_level`, and at the best `v ∈ {3, 4, 5}` makes the whole forest at least 20% faster per leaf than the 31.2–31.7 ns baseline (E1), with the `i128`-accumulation share of the window's time reported as its own quantity.

Method: in the EXP-0006 workspace (rule 2: own `[workspace]`, path dependencies on `tessera-field`, `tessera-poly`, `tessera-sumcheck`, `tv-core` as the acceptance driver), implement the grid for the leaf level (optionally level `h − 1`), hand the once-bound `Fp2` tables to the engine for rounds `v + 1 … n`, and run EXP-0006's protocol: seeded synthetic keys below `2^22` (rule 6), 2^22 leaves in the three forest shapes, 3 warm-ups and 10 measured iterations, iterations of at least 20 ms, median and coefficient of variation, baseline and candidate in the same harness (rule 4). A `count.py`-style int32 translation of the window gives the H100 reading (E0). Pre-registered verdict: `validated` if the best `v` is at least 20% faster per leaf with identical transcript state and acceptance; `refuted` below 15% (kill criterion 1); `inconclusive` between. Effort `s`–`m` (days; the grid and the Lagrange collapse are a few hundred lines; no RFC). Reaches E1. What stays unknown until an H100 run: the cost of `i128` emulation on the int32 pipeline, whether the leaf kernel is compute-bound there (kill 3), and the true `c_π` (RFC-0020 gate).
