## Mechanism

Setting. After [IDEA-0021](IDEA-0021-split-eq-gruen-level-kernel.md)/[IDEA-0018](IDEA-0018-split-eq-fraction-level-kernel.md) (validated by EXP-0008), every round `j` of a fraction-tree level sends `p_j(0), p_j(2), p_j(3)` ([RFC-0010 'Sumcheck messages'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0010-proof-schedule.md#sumcheck-messages)) formed from two node sums `v(z) = Σ_{x_hi} eq_hi[x_hi] · Σ_{x_lo} eq_lo[x_lo] · R_z(x)`, `z ∈ {0, 2}`, over the level's pairs `x = (x_hi, x_lo)`; `R_z(x)` is the batched degree-3 relation at node `z` of pair `x`, `e·(o + γ) + γ²·o` on the leaf level (numerators 1, `count.py` `fraction_leaf_round`) and `dd·(ne + γ²·de) + γ·(no·de)` on a generic level (`fraction_generic_round`), every operand an `Fp2` value. Today every `Fp2 × Fp2` product is Karatsuba (`ext2.rs:108`: three base multiplications and one `mul_small(7)`), every base multiplication ends in `reduce128` (`goldilocks.rs:55-74`, verified: the two-step special reduction, about 10 of the 18 int32 lane operations of a Goldilocks multiplication under [E0-field]), and the kernel reduces each product before adding it (`kernel.rs` `zipped!`: `*p *= v` then `*sum += p`; `product_ext_sums`: `*sum += x.mul_by_base(b)`). The inner sum over `x_lo` is a dot product whose only consumer is an addition: nothing needs the reduced value of an individual product.

Four prover-only changes, none of which alters a message:

1. **Terminal products stay unreduced.** Classify each product of `R_z(x)·eq_lo[x_lo]` by whether its result feeds another product (then it must be reduced, as today) or only the sum (then it is terminal). Scaling first makes the relation's outer products terminal: on the leaf level, per node, `w_z = eq_lo[x_lo]·e_z` is reduced (one Karatsuba product), then `w_z·(o_z + γ)` and `(eq_lo[x_lo]·γ²)·o_z` are terminal, where `eq_lo·γ²` is a per-round table of `2^{⌈m/2⌉}` entries (negligible); 4 of the 6 `Fp2` products per pair are terminal. On a generic level, per node, `γ²·de_z`, `eq_lo·dd_z` and `(eq_lo·γ)·no_z` are reduced and `(eq_lo·dd_z)·(ne_z + γ²·de_z)` and `((eq_lo·γ)·no_z)·de_z` are terminal; 4 of the 10 products per pair are terminal. (A per-level linear pre-combination `t = ne + γ²·de`, which commutes with binding, would make one more product per node reducible away; it is a separate micro-optimization and not counted here.)
2. **Schoolbook, split accumulation.** A terminal `Fp2` product `(a_0 + a_1 X)(b_0 + b_1 X)` with `X² = 7` is computed schoolbook: four `64 × 64 → 128` base products `a_0 b_0`, `a_0 b_1`, `a_1 b_0`, `a_1 b_1`, no Karatsuba pre-sums (so no operand exceeds `2^64` and no result exceeds `P² < 2^128`), accumulated without reduction into three per-component sums `C_0 = Σ a_0 b_0`, `C_1 = Σ (a_0 b_1 + a_1 b_0)`, `C_7 = Σ a_1 b_1`. Each sum is held split: on the CPU as two `u128` words holding the low and the high 64-bit halves of the products (`lo_sum += p as u64; hi_sum += (p >> 64) as u64`), each below `2^{64 + k}` after `2^k` products, so `k ≤ 63` never overflows and `k = 16` is the CPU block; on the GPU as three 64-bit words with carry (the form SUR-0011 counted). The only per-product work beyond the four wide multiplications is the 128-bit add with carry, about 4–5 int32 lane operations each ([E0-field]'s accounting; the CPU does it in two instructions).
3. **One reduction per block.** A block is `2^k` consecutive pairs of one `x_hi` group (the inner sum of the split-`eq` kernel runs over `2^{⌈m/2⌉} ≤ 2^11` pairs at `n = 21`, so a block is at most that group and the reduction cadence is "once per `x_hi` group", `k ≤ 11`; a plain kernel without split `eq` takes `k = 16`). At block end each accumulator is reduced once: `value = reduce128(lo_sum) + reduce128(hi_sum)·(2^64 mod P)`, where `2^64 mod P = 2^32 − 1 = EPSILON` fits `mul_small` (`goldilocks.rs`), so the cost is two `reduce128`, one `mul_small` and one addition per word pair; the non-residue is folded as `c_0 = C_0 + 7·C_7` (one `mul_small(7)`), `c_1 = C_1`, and the reduced `Fp2` block value is added to the node sum exactly where today's kernel adds `h·acc[z]` per group (`count.py` `split_group`). Amortized over `2^k` pairs the block end is below 0.1 int32 operation per pair for `k ≥ 10`.
4. **Everything stored stays reduced.** The folds `V(r) = V_0 + r·(V_1 − V_0)` (`bind_lowest_in_place`), the `eq` tables and every value written to memory are canonical as today; the per-round message assembly, the recovery `v(1) = (c − ℓ(0)·v(0))·ℓ(1)^{-1}` and the claim update are unchanged. The block cadence depends on the level's variable count only, never on a value (branch-free 128-bit adds, the existing branch-free `reduce128`), so [RFC-0002 'Timing'](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0002-field-arithmetic.md#timing) and INV-ARCH-2 hold as before.

The same four changes apply unchanged to `product_ext_sums` on the product tree (two products and two scalings per pair per round: scale first, then both products are terminal) and to the stage sumchecks' node sums; this note prices the fraction tree only.

## Why it could work

- **The reduction is where a Goldilocks multiplication spends most of its lanes (E0, primary).** [E0-field] (SUR-0011, from the Plonky3 and Tessera code read): 18 int32 lane operations per Goldilocks multiplication, of which the four `32 × 32 → 64` partial products with carries are about 8 and the two-step special reduction about 10; a multiply-accumulate with the reduction deferred costs about 10 (8 for the product, 2 carry additions on a wide accumulator), −45% on the multiplication share of a dot-product kernel. Tessera's `reduce128` (`goldilocks.rs:55-74`, verified) is the same two-step reduction RFC-0002 specifies: a 128-bit split, one borrow-corrected subtraction, one `32 × 32` product, one carry-corrected addition and a canonicalization.
- **The post-Gruen fraction kernel is dot-product-shaped, which is the condition SUR-0011 set (E0, primary).** EXP-0008's `count.py` gives the candidate's per-pair-round work as `fraction_leaf_round = 6 Fp2 mul, 8 Fp2 add, 2 Fp2 sub` and `fraction_generic_round = 10 Fp2 mul, 10 Fp2 add, 4 Fp2 sub`, every product's result either an operand of one more product or a term of the node sum; by the classification of mechanism 1, 4 of 6 leaf products (67%) and 4 of 10 generic products (40%) are terminal. The leaf level and the generic levels hold equal numbers of pairs over a tree (levels halve), so about 50% of the level sumchecks' products are terminal (E0, own count).
- **Own int32 translation of the candidate (E0, primary, the scratch script's rules stated here).** With `count.py`'s low translation (multiplication 18, addition 4, subtraction 4, `mul_small(7)` 8), a Karatsuba `Fp2` product at 82 lane operations, a terminal schoolbook product at `4·8 + 4·acc` with `acc ∈ {4, 5}` per 128-bit accumulate, and the lines, relation additions and folds as `count.py` counts them: leaf level 572 → 404–420 per pair-round (−27% to −29%), generic level 932 → 764–780 (−16% to −18%); with the two and four `bind_ext` folds per pair included, 768 → 600–616 and 1,324 → 1,156–1,172; the two levels equal-weighted, −14.5% to −16.1% of the level kernels' lane operations (−20% to −22% on the round sums alone, folds excluded).
- **Measured on a CPU in another field (E1, primary via SUR-0007).** [DDBDT26] §3 and Table 4, Apple M4 Max, BN254: delayed reduction alone 1.37× on a degree-2 sumcheck, 1.09× once split `eq` is in place (the regime this note starts from), 10.5–23.7× on linear combinations with one reduction. Over BN254 the reduction is a larger share of a multiplication than over Goldilocks, so these ratios are an upper bound for the mechanism here, not a prediction.
- **Headroom bits are the lever, not the prime (E1, primary, baseline unstated).** [ZGLCZ26] (MamaBearZKP, CCS 2026): a 49-bit prime chosen for lazy reduction gives 21× single-threaded and 45× on 8 threads over a Goldilocks baseline on a CPU; SUR-0011 finding 5 draws the lesson that Goldilocks has `2^-32` relative headroom in 64 bits, so on a GPU the equivalent of MamaBear's lazy reduction is 128-bit accumulation, which is this note.
- **The target is the largest unit cost (E0 count fitted to E1).** `c_ℓ` is 101 ps in the `counted` profile ([baseline.md §2](../baseline.md#2-the-cost-model), EXP-0006), 18.5 of its 24.5 `Fp2`-multiplication equivalents in the level sumchecks ([E0-this]); EXP-0008 measured the post-Gruen fraction proxy at 39.98 ns per pair-entry on the leaf level and 75.36 ns on the generic level on one M4 Max core (E1), the harness this note's experiment extends.

Keys [DDBDT26] and [E0-this] are in [bibliography.md](../bibliography.md); [E0-field] and [ZGLCZ26] are defined in the source rows of [SUR-0011](../surveys/SUR-0011-field.md) and their bibliography lines are merged by the consolidation agent.

## Expected gain

Headline (E0, own count): the level kernels lose 14.5–16.1% of their lane operations (folds included), and the level sumchecks are `18.5 / 24.5 = 75.5%` of a lookup's work ([E0-this], textbook count), so `c_ℓ` 101 → 89–90 ps; the row below uses 89. Optimistic row (the generation pass's figure): if the generic level's three reduced products per node can also be deferred (for example through the `t = ne + γ²·de` pre-combination and a second scaling table), both levels approach the leaf's −29% and `c_ℓ` → about 80 ps. Memory-floor row: [E0-this] puts the fraction kernel's HBM floor at 86 ps unfused (288 B per lookup at 3.35 TB/s) and 57 ps with two rounds fused; the headline sits just above the unfused floor, the optimistic row below it, so 80 ps needs fusion depth 2 (IDEA-0003-class) to show on the H100, and the 74 ps the generation pass quoted is reported for the composition with that fusion, not claimed here.

## Prior rejection check

- [RFC-0002 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0002-field-arithmetic.md#alternatives-considered) item 4 and [baseline.md §7](../baseline.md#7-already-rejected) row 2 reject **Montgomery form for the prover** because the special-form reduction is faster. This idea keeps the special-form reduction and calls it once per block instead of once per product; it re-proposes nothing rejected. Items 1–3 and 5 (31-bit fields, a pairing field, larger extensions) are not touched.
- [RFC-0008 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0008-logup-and-tables.md#alternatives-considered) (one-hot and univariate lookups, base-field challenges) and [RFC-0010 Alternatives Considered](../../docs/rfcs/RFC-0010-proof-schedule.md#alternatives-considered) do not address the prover's reduction schedule; RFC-0008 NG-6 names kernel optimizations as out of scope, not rejected. No row of baseline.md §7 covers reduction scheduling.
- [SUR-0011](../surveys/SUR-0011-field.md)'s candidate said "not a new idea note unless the counts show the kernels are dot-product-shaped", with the first killer that fraction nodes are single products or sums of two (saving 0–25%). Answered: the node *values* `(n, d)` are products, but the level sumcheck's node *sums* over `2^20` pairs per round are dot products, and by EXP-0008's rules 67% of the leaf level's and 40% of a generic level's products are terminal (mechanism 1); the survey's second concern, that [E0-this] and EXP-0006 already assume delayed reduction in their 18-operation count, is false: [E0-field] decomposes the 18 as product plus reduction and `count.py` translates every multiplication, terminal or not, at 18.
- Overlap, not rejection: [IDEA-0005](IDEA-0005-base-field-leaf-small-value.md) mechanism 3 accumulates *small-value* integer coefficients of the leaf level's first rounds in 64-bit MACs (`triaged`, 0.32); [IDEA-0022](IDEA-0022-integer-leaf-level-round-window.md) does the same with a `3^v` grid on the product tree's integer leaf level (`triaged`, 3.1); [IDEA-0010](IDEA-0010-shared-point-forest-fusion.md) mechanism 2 is the integer first round. All three need small integer operands and so cover only the leaf level's first rounds; this note defers the reduction of full-width `Fp2 × Fp2` products on every level and every round, composes with them (where they apply, their small products are already terminal) and with the split-`eq` Gruen kernel it starts from.
- New now: EXP-0008's measured, counted kernel structure (the condition SUR-0011 set), the terminal-product classification with its 67% / 40% shares, a primary source for the GPU lane-operation split of a Goldilocks multiplication ([E0-field]), and the `Fp2`-specific trick that schoolbook with three split accumulators removes the Karatsuba pre-sums and so keeps every operand below `2^64`.

## Kill criterion

The E1 kernel pair on the M4 Max (EXP-0008's fraction proxy at `N = 2^22`, delayed-reduction node sums against `prove_split_batch`'s, bit-identical messages) gains less than 10% per pair-entry on the leaf level, where the count predicts −22% with folds and −29% on the round sums: then the reduction is not where the time goes (the lines, additions and folds are) and the mechanism cannot reach the headline on any level. Two further observations halve the idea rather than refute it: (2) the generated assembly of the production `prove_batch` already hoists `reduce128` out of the accumulation loop (read it: today `kernel.rs` reduces each product before `+=`, and a compiler cannot legally defer a canonicalizing reduction, so this is a check, not an expectation); (3) the RFC-0020 v0.1 measurement (E2) finds the post-Gruen fraction kernel HBM-bound at fusion depth 1, where EXP-0008's count says compute-bound by 1.7×, in which case nothing below 86 ps shows until two-round fusion lands.

## Cheapest experiment

Hypothesis: on one Apple M4 Max performance core, in EXP-0008's harness, fraction-level node sums with four-product schoolbook `Fp2` terminal products accumulated unreduced in split `u128` low/high sums and reduced once per `x_hi` group emit round messages and final evaluations bit-identical to `prove_split_batch`'s, are accepted by `tv_core::sumcheck::verify_batch`, and are at least 15% faster per pair-entry on the leaf level (`[eq, d_e, d_o]`, numerators 1; count −22% with folds) and at least 8% on the generic level (`[eq, n_e, d_o]`, `[eq, n_o, d_e]`, `[eq, d_e, d_o]`; count −13% with folds), with the terminal-product share and the per-block reduction count reported as their own quantities.

Method: in the EXP-0008 workspace (research rule 2: own `[workspace]`, path dependencies on `tessera-field` and `tessera-sumcheck`, `tv-core` as the acceptance oracle), add `fraction_leaf_sums_delayed` and `fraction_generic_sums_delayed` beside the existing sums with a `DelayedFp2Acc { c0_lo, c0_hi, c1_lo, c1_hi, c7_lo, c7_hi: u128 }` and its `reduce() -> Fp2` (two `reduce128`, one `mul_small(EPSILON)` and one addition per word pair, `mul_small(7)` to fold `c7`), a `#[cfg(test)]` proptest that `reduce()` of `2^16` random products equals the reduced sum, then EXP-0008's protocol: seeded synthetic denominators and numerators (rule 6), `N = 2^22`, 3 warm-ups and 10 measured iterations of at least 20 ms, median and coefficient of variation, baseline and candidate in the same binary (rule 4). `count.py` gains the candidate's rules (`unreduced_fp2_mul`, `acc128` at 4 and 5 lane operations) and recomputes the H100 interval as EXP-0006/0008 did (E0). Pre-registered verdict: `validated` if both level thresholds are met with bit-identical messages, acceptance and CV ≤ 3%; `refuted` below 10% on the leaf level (kill criterion 1); `inconclusive` between. Effort `s` (two days; the accumulator and two sum functions are under two hundred lines; no RFC). Reaches E1 for the ratio, E0 for the GPU translation. Caveat on direction: the M4 Max's `mul`/`umulh` pair makes a wide product two instructions and a 128-bit add two, so on the CPU the reduction is a larger share of a multiplication (about 10 of 12) than on the H100's int32 pipe (about 10 of 18) and the accumulate is cheaper; the CPU ratio therefore overstates the GPU ratio, and the count, not the clock, carries the translation. What stays unknown until an H100 run: the lane cost of a 128-bit accumulate, whether the kernel is compute-bound at fusion depth 1 (kill criterion 3), and the true `c_ℓ` (RFC-0020 gate).

## Refutations

The arithmetic is exact: `mul` is `reduce128(a as u128 * b as u128)` (crates/tessera-field/src/goldilocks.rs:73, 55-70, verified), schoolbook products of canonical operands are < P² < 2^128, 2^k ≤ 2^16 low/high halves accumulate in u128 without wrap, and Σp = lo_sum + 2^64·hi_sum with 2^64 ≡ 2^32 − 1 (RFC-0002:89) reduces correctly; c_0 = C_0 + 7·C_7 is the X² = 7 rule. The RFC-0013 masking polynomial is an additive term outside the node sums and the reduction cadence depends on the level's variable count only, so no leak or timing dependence is added. |
| prior art | survives | Not in the specification and not rejected: RFC-0002:88 only permits GPU kernels to hold values in [0, 2^64) between operations and demands canonicalization before hashed buffers; it specifies no accumulation schedule, and the production code reduces every product (`reduce128` at crates/tessera-field/src/goldilocks.rs:55-74, `mul` at :74, verified).
