# `c_π` on H100: product-tree levels

The product-tree leaf unit cost of [RFC-0020](../../rfcs/RFC-0020-cost-model.md#measurement-protocol-v01-gate), measured on 2026-11-09 with the microkernel of [#64](https://github.com/AbdelStark/tessera/issues/64). The work is one forest of 2^16 trees of 2^14 leaves (2^30 leaves, rows of 2^14 as RFC-0020 fixes), leaves generated on the device. It builds the forest's levels bottom-up and runs its 14 degree-3 level sumchecks in `Fp2` on a transcript, with RFC-0010's labels and messages ([RFC-0010 'Tree levels'](../../rfcs/RFC-0010-proof-schedule.md#tree-levels)).

## Result

| Measurement | Median | Min | Max | CV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build and prove the forest, 2^30 leaves | 84.56 ps | 84.51 ps | 84.63 ps | 0.05% |

**`c_π` = 84.6 ps per leaf**, against an assumed 12 ps: `ρ_π = 7.05`. Before any run is timed, the benchmark proves the same forest with the CPU prover `tessera_sumcheck::prove_forests` on the same transcript. Every level's messages, the leaf claim and the transcript state that follows are equal, at the full 2^30 leaves.

The workspace (levels, bound tables, round buffers: about 24 GB) is allocated once and reused across runs, as RFC-0018's memory scheduler pools device memory. The first version allocated and zero-filled it on every proof and measured 159 ps; allocation cost 75 of those.

Evidence level E1 on the target hardware. The environment is that of the [`c_c` report](cc-h100.md): one H100 80GB HBM3, driver 580.126.20, CUDA 12.8, clocks not locked.

### Where the time goes

The device does the levels' products, the split `eq` tables of each round, and each round's three sums (`S(0)`, `S(1)`, `S(2)` of `Σ w·E·O`). Each round after the first is fused with the binding of the round before it. The host runs the transcript, one round trip per round, 315 rounds per proof. Per level, from an instrumented run (not the timed one):

| Batch level (`n` variables) | 13 (29) | 12 (28) | 11 (27) | 10 (26) | 0–9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ms | 36.3 | 18.8 | 9.9 | 5.5 | 11.9 |

Each level costs about half the one below it, as the tables halve. Building the levels takes about 4 ms more. The deepest level is 40% of the 90.8 ms a proof takes.

## How far this is from the floor

A level's sumcheck materializes its bound tables in `Fp2` (16 bytes an entry). With every round fused, a proof still reads and writes about 96 GB per 2^30 leaves, half of it at the deepest level. At the H100's measured 3 TB/s that is about **30 ps per leaf of memory traffic** for this algorithm, before any arithmetic. Each round's arithmetic, about 20 base-field multiplications per leaf summed over the rounds and levels, overlaps it only partly.

Two levers remain, each on the order of tens of percent, not the factor of seven to the assumption:

- **Gruen's trick.** Derive `S(1)` from the round's claim, as the CPU prover does, and skip a third of the products.
- **Fewer bytes per round.** Keep the first round's binding implicit, binding twice from the base-field level, and write narrower tables.

**`ρ_π` stays well above 2** under any implementation of this level structure on H100.

## Reproduce

```sh
cargo test --release -p tessera-gpu --features gpu --test trees    # byte-identical to prove_forests
cargo run --release -p tessera-bench --features gpu --example gpu_unit_costs -- pi
```

`--trees-log S` changes the number of trees (`2^S` trees of `2^14` leaves). The tests in `crates/tessera-gpu/tests/trees.rs` cover ten shapes: single trees, one level, tall and wide forests, and 2^6 trees of the benchmark's height 14. They also cover the refusal of malformed forests.
